# Thailand Retirement Visa
## Thailand Retirement Visa: Key Facts at a Glance
| Requirement | Details |
| Visa Type | Non-Immigrant O (applied for inside Thailand) or Non-Immigrant O-A (applied for outside Thailand) |
| Validity | 1 year, renewable annually |
| Length of Stay | Up to 1 year per extension; a re-entry permit is required to leave and return without losing the visa |
| Age Requirement | 50 years or older at time of application |
| Employment | Not permitted under this visa |
| Reporting | 90-day address report to Thai Immigration |
| Financial Requirement | 800,000 THB in a Thai bank account, or 65,000 THB in monthly income |

The Thailand Retirement Visa allows foreign nationals aged 50 and above to live in the country long-term. It's technically granted as an extension of a Non-Immigrant visa, but functions as a renewable residency option for as long as requirements are maintained.

Some long-term residents use several years of retirement visa renewals as a step toward later applying for Permanent Residency in Thailand.
## Non-Immigrant O vs. Non-Immigrant O-A: Side by Side
|  | Non-Immigrant O (Most Popular) | Non-Immigrant O-A |
| Process | Two-step: 90-day visa first, then extend 1 year in Thailand | One-step: full year granted before you travel |
| Where You Apply | Thai Embassy/Consulate outside Thailand | Thai Embassy/Consulate outside Thailand |
| Initial Validity | 90 days, then extended to 1 year at a local immigration office | Full 1 year from the date of entry |
| Health Insurance | Not required | Required (3,000,000 THB coverage, COVID-19 inclusive) |
| Police Clearance | Not required | Required, issued within the last 3 months |
| Best For | Applicants comfortable doing a follow-up extension once in Thailand, who want to skip the police clearance and health insurance requirements | Applicants who are fine with that extra paperwork in exchange for getting the full year secured upfront |

Both are applied for the same way: through a Thai Embassy/Consulate outside Thailand. Neither can be obtained by walking into a Thai immigration office from scratch. The real difference is what you get on day one, and what it costs you in paperwork.

Most applicants choose the Non-O route. It's a two-step process (90 days first, then extend once you're in Thailand), and it skips the health insurance, police clearance, and medical certificate that O-A requires upfront.

If you're already in Thailand on a tourist visa or visa exemption, you'll generally need to leave the country first to apply for the 90-day Non-Immigrant O at an embassy or consulate, then re-enter and extend once you're back.

#### Core Eligibility

Age: 50 years or older at the time of application

Admissibility: Not restricted from entering Thailand under the Immigration Act B.E. 2522

Employment restriction: No work permitted, including remote work for overseas employers or unpaid volunteer work

Medical requirement: A medical certificate confirming the applicant does not have leprosy, advanced-stage tuberculosis, drug addiction, elephantiasis, or third-stage syphilis
#### Financial Requirements: Three Pathways

Immigration officers now review bank records and supporting documents more closely, including bank letters confirming the balance.

Thai Bank Deposit: Maintain at least 800,000 THB in a Thai bank account under your name. Funds must be held for 2 months before your first application, and 3 months before each renewal.

Monthly Income: Show a monthly income or pension of at least 65,000 THB, confirmed via an embassy certification letter or 12 months of bank transfer records.

Combination Method: Combine bank savings and annual income so the total equals 800,000 THB per year.

Siam Legal Expert Insight — The 400,000 THB Balance Rule: If you use the 800,000 THB deposit method, the full amount must remain in the account for 3 months after your visa is approved. After that, the balance must not fall below 400,000 THB for the rest of the year. Dropping this amount below can cause issues at your next renewal.

## Is This Visa Right for You?

Fully Retired: You've finished working and want a simple, lower-cost way to stay long-term.

Settling in One Location: You plan to live in a specific area like Phuket or Chiang Mai, where the retirement visa is widely accepted for local arrangements like banking and address registration.

Prefer Flexibility Over a Big Upfront Commitment: You'd rather handle a modest annual renewal than commit to a large one-time membership fee or a high investment threshold.

When to consider other options:

Higher Passive Income: If you clear $80k+ in annual passive income, the LTR Visa trades your annual renewal and 90-day reporting for a 10-year stay with only an annual check-in.

Want the Year Secured Without Ongoing Upkeep: The Thailand Privilege Visa swaps annual financial requirements for a one-time membership fee starting at 650,000 THB, with stays of 5 to 20 years.

Under 50: You don't qualify for the Retirement Visa yet. Consider the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) or the Thailand Privilege Visa instead.
#### Income Shortfall Due to Exchange Rate

Profile: Retiree from the UK, age 60. John applied using the 65,000 THB monthly income method based on his UK pension, calculated at an earlier exchange rate. By his appointment, the Baht had strengthened, and his income converted to only 64,200 THB, below the requirement. His application was rejected, and he was given a short window to leave Thailand.

Siam Legal takeaway: Applicants using foreign income should build in a margin for exchange rate movement, or use the deposit method if income sits close to the minimum.
#### Bank Deposit Timing Issue

Profile: Retiree from the US, age 58. Maria transferred 800,000 THB exactly 60 days before her appointment, but her bank recorded the funds as available for only 59 days due to processing time. Her application was delayed, requiring a temporary extension and a return visit.

Siam Legal takeaway: Build in extra time beyond the minimum seasoning period, and confirm the bank record reflects the full holding period before submitting.
## What is the difference between the Non-Immigrant O and O-A visas?

The Non-Immigrant O visa is processed inside Thailand by converting an existing visa to a 90-day stay, then extending it to one year, and generally requires less in the way of health insurance. The Non-Immigrant O-A visa is applied for outside Thailand and grants a full year upon entry, but requires police clearance, a medical certificate, and health insurance.
## Can I volunteer on a retirement visa?

No. Under Thai law, volunteering is considered a form of work, which requires a work permit that cannot be issued under a retirement visa.
## What is the "seasoning" rule for the 800,000 THB deposit?

Funds must be in the account 2 months before the initial application, 3 months before each annual renewal, and after approval, the full amount must remain for 3 months before it's allowed to drop (not below 400,000 THB) for the rest of the year.
## Can my spouse apply if they are under 50?

Yes. A spouse can apply for a Non-Immigrant O dependent visa, allowing both partners to stay under one financial requirement, subject to supporting documents.
## Can I convert a tourist visa into a retirement visa inside Thailand?

Yes, if you have enough time remaining on your current stay. You can convert to a 90-day Non-Immigrant O visa and then apply for a 1-year extension without leaving Thailand.
