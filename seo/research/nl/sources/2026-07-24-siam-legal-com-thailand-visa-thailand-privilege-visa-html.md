### Program Overview

Is the Thailand Elite Visa still available?

The visa program is fully active, but it is no longer branded as "Thailand Elite Visa." It now operates under the name Thailand Privilege Visa. While the branding has changed, the core benefits remain the same, including long-term visa options ranging from 5 to 20 years.

What is the difference between the Thailand Elite Visa and the Thailand Privilege Visa?

The key difference is flexibility. Under the former Thailand Elite Visa program, membership packages offered a fixed set of benefits. The Thailand Privilege Visa introduces a Points System, allowing members to customize their experience (e.g., redeeming points for flights or health checkups).

Do Thailand Privilege Card members receive the same immigration benefits as Thailand Elite Visa holders?

Yes. As with the old Thailand Elite Visa, you do not need a re-entry permit, you can open a Thai bank account easily, and you get access to the Elite Personal Liaison for 90-day reporting.
### Eligibility & Application

What are the eligibility requirements for a Thailand Privilege Visa?

There are no age, financial, education, or employment requirements to join. However, applicants must still meet these conditions:

Hold a valid foreign passport with at least 1 year of remaining validity

Have no record of significant visa overstays in Thailand (no more than one overstay within the past three years)

Have no criminal record, bankruptcy history, or other disqualifying issues

Have not previously held a Thailand Volunteer Visa

Pass the required background check

Education Visa holders may apply but must submit additional verification documents. Applicants under 20 years old require parental consent.

Are any nationalities restricted from applying for the Thailand Privilege Visa?

Most restrictions have been lifted. TPC now permits applicants from all countries on the UN sanctions list except the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea). This includes nationalities that were previously ineligible, such as:

Asia: Afghanistan, Pakistan

Africa: Central African Republic, Cameroon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Guinea, Ghana, Liberia, Nigeria, Republic of the Congo, São Tomé and Príncipe, Sierra Leone, Somalia, Sudan

Middle East: Algeria, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Palestine, Syria, Yemen

Applicants from these nationalities should still confirm current eligibility, as background-check requirements and processing times can vary.

Can I apply for a Thailand Privilege Visa if I am currently overstaying in Thailand?

No. You must resolve any overstay and leave the country before applying; the Thailand Privilege application does not grant a visa extension or forgive an existing overstay. A history of overstays, particularly more than one or two within the past few years, can also affect approval of a new application even after the overstay itself has been resolved. Speak with our team before applying if you have any overstay history, so we can assess your situation first.

Can I switch to a Thailand Privilege Visa if I currently hold a different visa type?

In most cases, yes, but the process depends on which visa you currently hold:

Tourist Visa, Visa on Arrival, or visa exemption: You can apply normally. If you are already in Thailand and your current entry is still valid, your Privilege Entry Visa can typically be affixed at the Immigration Bureau in Bangkok.

Non-Immigrant Visa (e.g., Non-B work visa, Non-O marriage visa, Non-OA retirement visa): Thai immigration law only allows one active visa type at a time, so your existing non-immigrant visa must be cancelled before your Privilege Entry Visa can be activated. Our team will walk you through this cancellation step so there is no gap in your legal status.

Education (ED) Visa: You may still apply, but additional verification documents are required. As long as you can provide proof that you genuinely attended classes, most cases are approved. Note that your ED Visa must also be cancelled before the Privilege Entry Visa can be activated, if it is still valid at that time.

Yes. This is automatically arranged after you receive your membership ID and welcome letter, so no separate application is needed. ### Visa Compliance & Ongoing Obligations

What happens if my passport has less than 5 years of validity remaining?

Your Privilege Entry Visa will be issued to match your passport's remaining validity rather than the full 5-year visa term. For example, if your passport expires in 2 years, your visa will be valid for that same 2-year period rather than 5 years. When you renew your passport, the remaining validity of your membership can be transferred to the new passport. We can coordinate with the TPC team on this transfer for you.

Can I work in Thailand while holding a Thailand Privilege Visa?

No. The Privilege Entry Visa is classified as a tourist visa, so it does not permit you to apply for a Thai work permit or take up local employment. You can still be a shareholder (up to 49%, the legal limit for foreign ownership) or director of a Thai company without a work permit, and there are no restrictions on remote work performed for an employer or clients based outside Thailand. If you need to work legally for a Thai company, you can obtain a separate Non-Immigrant B visa and work permit. Your Privilege membership will remain active and continue running, but your visa will switch to the work visa instead.

What is the TM30 requirement, and does it apply to Thailand Privilege Visa holders?

TM30 is a long-standing Thai immigration requirement under which a landlord, hotel, or property owner must report a foreigner's stay at their address within 24 hours of arrival. It applies to all foreigners in Thailand, including Thailand Privilege members, and responsibility for filing it rests with the host rather than the visa holder. You may still be asked to present a TM30 receipt when completing your 90-day report or other immigration procedures, so it's worth confirming with your landlord or hotel that it has been filed. We generally only need the TM30 to request your visa affixation appointment at the Chaengwattana Immigration Office, and in some cases, we can help issue it on your behalf if you provide your landlord's ID and blue book.

What happens if I do not complete my annual stay extension on time?

Each entry under the Privilege Entry Visa permits a stay of up to one year before an extension is required if you do not leave and re-enter the country. Failing to extend on time before the entry stamp expires can result in an overstay, which carries the same daily fine that applies to any overstay in Thailand regardless of visa type, and may affect future applications or renewals. Your annual extension booking is handled by Member Service, and our team can coordinate with them on your behalf. We recommend scheduling your extension well before the expiry date to avoid any risk of overstay.
