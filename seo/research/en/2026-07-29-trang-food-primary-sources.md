## Primary and specialist source findings

- A Journal of Thai Food Culture study traces Trang roast pork through Chinese migration, Cantonese roasting knowledge, locally available spices, individual brick ovens and locally produced firewood. It describes the dish as a Trang geographical-indication product and a Thai-Chinese cultural link.
- Current 2026 qualitative research on Trang culinary-cultural experience draws on 32 representatives across public, private and eight community contexts. It identifies food resources, culture, community life and participation as the core, supported by quality/taste, cultural authenticity, community involvement and management readiness.
- The Tourism Authority of Thailand consistently connects Trang breakfast with roast pork and dim sum. Its older venue, festival, market-hour and opening-time details are not treated as current guarantees.
- TAT's Peranakan heritage material supports Trang roast pork as an early breakfast partner to varied dim sum and describes overnight marination with Chinese herbs, spices and honey. The DFS PDF parse returned no text, so the indexed official extract is recorded transparently.
- Travelfish supports the municipal market, Centrepoint evening layer, southern curry-and-rice, Chinese and Muslim-Thai influences, and a vegetarian-food layer. Its venue, price and schedule details are historical observations only.
- Trang cake from Ban Lum Phu Ra and southern directions such as `gaeng som`, `gaeng tai pla`, stink-bean dishes and turmeric fish create a broader table than dim sum alone.
- WHO and the UK Food Standards Agency remain the primary safety and allergy frameworks.
