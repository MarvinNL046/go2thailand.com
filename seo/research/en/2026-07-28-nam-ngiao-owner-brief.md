## Dominant intent and owner boundary

The page owns **Nam Ngiao / Nam Ngiew / Nam Ngiaw / Khanom Jeen Nam Ngiao** as a Northern Thai tomato-rich noodle sauce or curry-like broth, commonly built with pork, dried chilli and fermented soybean, ladled over rice noodles and finished with fresh, sour and crisp accompaniments. Blood, dok ngiao cotton-tree flower core, pork rib, minced pork, shrimp paste, fish sauce, specific herbs and toppings are familiar signals but are not universal.

Keep these independent:

- Khao Soi: Northern curry noodles with a different noodle, coconut and crisp-noodle architecture.
- Khanom Jeen Nam Ya: a separate fish-curry sauce owner, commonly associated with Central or Southern preparations.
- Guay Tiew Nam Ngiao as a documented Chiang Rai service variation with flat fresh rice noodles—not a separate canonical owner.
- Boat noodles, Tom Yum noodles, generic pork noodle soup, tomato soup and generic khanom jeen intent.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **Name the sauce and noodle separately.** `Nam ngiao` is the tomato-rich Northern sauce or broth; `khanom jeen` names the round rice-noodle service. Chiang Rai also documents flat fresh noodles under `guay tiew nam ngiao`.
2. **Blood is familiar but not compulsory.** Cooked blood cubes or liquid blood can add body and texture, but a documented specialist version explicitly treats them as optional. Diners should ask rather than infer from colour.
3. **Dok ngiao is iconic, not a taste guarantee.** The dried inner part of the red cotton-tree flower contributes a fibrous texture and cultural signal; one Northern-born specialist omits it, so it cannot be an authenticity gate.
4. **The core is tomato, savoury fermented depth and chilli—not coconut cream.** Pork, tomatoes, dried chilli and fermented soybean recur across independent sources. Shrimp paste, fish sauce, aromatics and exact sweetness vary.
5. **Rice noodles do not prove gluten-free status.** Tua nao, tao jiew, miso, soy seasoning, stock, paste and shared preparation require a complete label and kitchen check; shrimp paste and fish sauce add crustacean or fish boundaries.
6. **Khao Soi is a useful contrast, not a synonym.** Tourism Thailand presents the dishes as two separate Northern choices; Nam Ngiao is tomato-rich and commonly paired with rice noodles, while Khao Soi has its own coconut-curry and crisp-noodle structure.
