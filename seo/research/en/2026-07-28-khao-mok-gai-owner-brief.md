## Dominant intent and owner boundary

This page owns the traveller-first dish entity **Khao Mok Gai / Thai chicken biryani**: aromatic yellow spiced rice cooked with marinated chicken, commonly finished with fried shallots and served with a green herb-chilli sauce, cucumber and sometimes broth. It explains Thai-Muslim and southern context, how it differs from other chicken-rice dishes, how to order and what to verify.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **It is a Thai biryani expression, not merely “yellow chicken rice”.** Current Thai-specialist and institutional sources identify Khao Mok Gai as Thailand’s chicken-biryani form with a complex spice profile.
2. **Thai-Muslim context is essential.** Hot Thai Kitchen links the dish particularly to areas with Thai Muslim communities; Thailand Foundation places it within southern cuisine shaped by maritime trade and Indian, Malay, Chinese and wider Southeast Asian exchange.
3. **The plate has three decisions.** Rice-and-chicken expression, fried shallot/cucumber accompaniments and the green herb-chilli sauce each affect taste and dietary checks.
4. **Mild rice does not mean a heat-free meal.** The aromatic rice can be gentle while the green dipping sauce carries fresh chilli and acid. Serve and taste the sauce separately.
5. **Muslim culinary heritage does not certify every current plate.** Halal suitability depends on chicken source, stock, yoghurt or dairy, seasoning, alcohol-containing flavourings, sauce and shared kitchen handling. Current certification or accountable vendor evidence matters.
6. **Gluten-free is not automatic.** Rice and whole spices can be naturally gluten-free, but stock cubes, spice blends, sauces, fried shallots and shared equipment can add wheat or cross-contact.
