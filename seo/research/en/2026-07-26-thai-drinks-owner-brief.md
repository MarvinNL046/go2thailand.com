## Content advantage

- SERP competitors mainly provide recipes, catalogues or short lists. The owner instead starts with four flavour families and six practical first drinks.
- Cha yen is explained as a set of choices—tea, sweetness, milk and ice—rather than one universal recipe.
- All 25 existing detail routes remain reachable through four compact editorial rows.
- Four Thai ordering phrases change sweetness, milk or ice without pretending phonetic text can replace an allergy conversation.
- Water guidance distinguishes utility treatment standards from building plumbing and traveller uncertainty; it does not claim that all Thai tap water is either safe or unsafe.
- Alcohol guidance uses the TAT update effective 29 May 2026: general sales from 11:00 to 24:00, age 20+, and location, licence or temporary restrictions still possible.
- No fixed street prices, timeless beer percentages, health promises or unsupported popularity ranking.

## Commercial fit

- One broad Klook food-tour route appears after the informational decision support and tells readers to verify the actual listing.
- One compact Amazon card contains only two contextual OneLink routes: a reusable bottle for water already known to be safe and Original Thai Tea mix for exploring cha yen at home.
- The bottle is explicitly not presented as a purifier. Both products use current-offer language, product checks, `nofollow sponsored` and a visible Amazon Associate/OneLink disclosure.
- This establishes the food-page rule: cookware, books or ingredients are eligible only beside a visible cooking or tasting task; generic product carousels are not.
