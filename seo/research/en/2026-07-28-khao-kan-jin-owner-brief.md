## Dominant intent and owner boundary

The page owns **Khao Kan Jin / Khao Kan Chin / Khao Ngiao / Chin Som Ngiao** as one Northern Thai and Tai Yai/Shan steamed-rice parcel. Cooked rice is kneaded with pig's blood, seasoning and garlic oil; minced pork appears in documented versions but is not universal. The mixture is enclosed in banana leaf, steamed and served with crisp garlic, shallot, chilli and fresh sides.

Keep these independent:

- Nam Ngiao: a Northern noodle soup with tomatoes, meat and pieces of cooked blood; the similar word `ngiao` does not make it the same dish.
- Khao Tom Mat: a sweet glutinous-rice and banana parcel, commonly enriched with coconut milk; banana-leaf wrapping alone does not merge the intent.
- Khao Soi, Khao Jee, Khao Niao and generic Northern Thai rice pages.
- Blood sausage, blood pudding and other global blood-dish intent.
- Recipe-only, restaurant-near-me, calorie, nutrition and universal-health intent.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **Three common names describe one owner.** Chiang Mai University records Khao Kan Chin, Khao Ngio and Chin Som Ngio; Michelin describes Khao Ngiao and Khao Kan Chin as names for the same Tai Yai-origin Northern dish.
2. **`Kan` describes the mixing action.** Michelin explains the Northern term as kneading or mixing the cooked rice with pig's blood, which is more useful than treating the romanisation as an ingredient list.
3. **The blood is mixed through the rice.** This is not rice served beside a blood curd or sausage. It colours and seasons the rice before the parcel is steamed.
4. **Minced pork is a branch, not an absolute.** CMU documents a minced-pork formula while explicitly noting that some recipes omit it. The blood boundary remains even when visible meat is absent.
5. **Banana leaf is packaging and aroma, not proof of sweetness.** Khao Kan Jin is a savoury rice parcel; Khao Tom Mat belongs to a separate sweet sticky-rice-and-banana intent.
6. **The garnish changes the final bite.** Fried garlic, garlic oil, fried chilli, shallot, cucumber, coriander or sprouts can add crunch, heat and freshness to an otherwise softly steamed parcel.
