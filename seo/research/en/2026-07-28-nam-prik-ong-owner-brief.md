## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- Nam Prik Ong is a cooked Northern Thai chilli dip or relish built around tomato and minced pork; it is not a smooth universal sauce and not the same as Nam Prik Pao.
- Familiar versions use dried chilli, shallot, garlic, tomato, minced pork and salt. Shrimp paste, fermented soybean, fish sauce, tamarind, coriander, spring onion or other seasonings vary by cook.
- The taste can combine tomato acidity and sweetness, savoury pork, salt and chilli heat. Heat and sweetness are not fixed.
- Fresh or boiled vegetables, sticky rice and pork crackling are familiar accompaniments, not mandatory inclusions.
- Standard versions are not vegetarian because of pork and may contain shrimp paste or fish sauce. Soy or wheat can appear through fermented soybean, seasoning sauces or packaged paste.
- `Gluten-free`, `halal`, shellfish-free and fish-free status must be checked from the complete paste, meat, seasoning and shared preparation.
- Minced pork must be cooked safely; colour, tomato redness or a simmering surface does not prove safe doneness. Use a tested recipe and current food-safety guidance.
- Never publish a fixed price, calorie count, universal health verdict, guaranteed heat, automatic dietary status, permanent restaurant ranking, one authentic formula or invented history.
