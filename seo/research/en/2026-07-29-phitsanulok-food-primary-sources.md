## Evidence-backed information gain

### Local table rather than one invented signature dish

Phugan and Inket's Pibulsongkram Rajabhat University study surveyed 640 long-term residents across 64 municipal communities. It recorded 148 foods (126 savoury dishes and 22 desserts) and selected five representative meal sets. The useful editorial conclusion is diversity built from locally available ingredients—not that one dish can stand in for the whole city.

The paper supports careful examples such as:

- chicken curry with banana stalk;
- mixed-vegetable soup;
- freshwater fish fried with garlic;
- grilled-fish or fermented-fish chilli relishes;
- red curry with smoked dried fish and coconut milk;
- lotus-stem yellow curry with shrimp;
- banana-stalk soup with grilled fish;
- fish steamed with curry paste and coconut cream in banana leaf;
- jackfruit curry;
- steamed banana cake, dried banana and coconut-filled sweets.

These are heritage directions, not guaranteed restaurant availability or universal recipes. Ingredient and transliteration detail must remain qualified.

### Noodle and snack layer

The Tourism Authority of Thailand's three-day article places Sukhothai-style tom-yam noodles close to Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat and records coconut-milk ice cream, processed banana snacks, fried taro, noodles, steamed buns, curries and `khanom wong` made from glutinous-rice flour, coconut milk, sugar and sesame. Historic venue names, prices, phone numbers and hours are excluded from evergreen recommendations.

Sukhothai-style noodles are treated as a useful lower-northern direction available in Phitsanulok, not as a claim that the dish originated in the city. Blood, pork, offal, fish sauce, peanuts, sugar and shared noodle water must be checked rather than inferred from the English label.

### Market and district logic

TAT describes a local evening-market layer along the Nan River and a food-focused market near the railway station. Travelfish independently supports three broad evening zones: Phra Ong Dam Road, the riverside/night-bazaar direction and the railway-station market. These sources establish spatial food rhythms, but their published schedules and stall lists are historical snapshots. The page must tell travellers to confirm today's operation.

TAT's 2026 `Taste the Local @ Phitsanulok` announcement confirms that local ingredients, community producers, contemporary drinks and culinary storytelling remain part of the province's active tourism positioning. The four-day event itself is not presented as a permanent attraction.
