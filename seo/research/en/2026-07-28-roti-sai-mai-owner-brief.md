## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- The sweet pairs a very thin soft roti sheet with long silky pulled-sugar threads. It is assembled by the eater: add a small bundle, fold or roll, then bite.
- The wrapper commonly contains wheat flour and may also contain tapioca flour, water, oil and salt. Pandan can colour/flavour green wrappers. Milk, coconut milk, sesame or grains appear by vendor.
- The floss is stretched cooked sugar rather than aerated fairground cotton candy. Flour and oil can enter its production, and colour/flavour varies.
- Sweet threads and a lightly salty, tender wrapper create the defining contrast. Caramel aroma, pandan, sesame, colour, thickness and chew vary.
- Wheat/gluten is intrinsic to the documented wrapper. Tapioca does not make a wheat-based roti gluten-free; dedicated alternative flour and separate equipment would be required for a different safe product.
- Many familiar versions may fit a vegetarian diet, but milk, other fat, glaze, colour, shared surfaces and vendor variation must be checked. Vegan status is not automatic; ask about dairy, egg, honey and processing aids rather than relying on appearance.
- Muslim-community heritage is culturally important but is not a universal halal certificate for every modern vendor. Check current certification or sufficient ingredient and handling transparency according to the diner's standard.
- Sesame may be visible or embedded; milk can be hidden; soy, peanut and other allergens may enter through shared stalls or variations. State allergies before buying a mixed bag.
- Humidity and time affect the two components differently: sugar can soften or clump and roti can dry. Buy from active turnover, keep wrapper and floss protected as sold and eat promptly; never invent one universal shelf life.
- Never publish a fixed price, calorie total, health verdict, automatic vegetarian/vegan/halal status, permanent stall ranking or hours, uncontested single-inventor story, universal colour/flavour or one authentic formula.
