## Fact and safety boundaries

- Sai Ua / Sai Oua is a fresh, aromatic Northern Thai pork sausage. It is not defined by fermentation or sourness.
- `Sai` refers to intestine/casing and Northern `ua` to stuffing in recurring Thai explanations. Caseless patties or rolls are home adaptations, not proof that every market product lacks casing.
- Pork and pork fat are the familiar base. Chicken, plant-based or commercial variants may exist but need their own ingredient confirmation.
- Curry-paste and herb signals can include dried or fresh chilli, lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallot, makrut lime leaf, coriander root, coriander leaf, turmeric, cumin or other spices. There is no single mandatory list.
- Fish sauce and shrimp paste appear in documented versions and create fish/shellfish and vegetarian boundaries. A curry paste may also contain hidden fish, shrimp, soy or wheat.
- Natural casing is commonly pork intestine. Ask when pork, halal status or casing matters; the filling alone does not answer the question.
- Sai Ua should be fully cooked. A browned or charred casing does not prove the centre is safe; use a tested method and current food-safety guidance.
- Sticky rice, raw or steamed vegetables, nam phrik noom, pork crackling and khan-tok dishes are familiar accompaniments, not mandatory service.
- Heat varies with curry paste and chilli. A premixed or pre-cooked sausage usually cannot be made milder to order.
- Never publish a fixed price, calorie count, universal health verdict, permanent market ranking, guaranteed heat level, automatic gluten-free or halal status, fixed shelf life, raw-centre reassurance or one authentic formula.
