## Dominant intent and owner boundary

The page owns **Tam Khanun / Tam Kanun / Tam Banun / Northern Thai pounded young-jackfruit salad** as a cooked Lanna dish: unripe jackfruit is made tender, pounded with a chilli-aromatic paste and briefly stir-fried. Minced pork or pork belly, shrimp paste, fermented fish, fish sauce, tomato, makrut lime leaf, fried garlic, dried chilli and fresh herbs vary by cook.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **This salad is cooked twice.** Young jackfruit is first boiled or otherwise made tender, then pounded and stir-fried with paste. It is not a raw, lime-dressed salad like Som Tam.
2. **Young and ripe jackfruit do different jobs.** Tam Khanun needs unripe, pale, fibrous flesh rather than the sweet, fragrant yellow or orange flesh described for ripe fruit by Thailand’s Department of Agricultural Extension.
3. **The texture should remain coarse and fibrous.** The jackfruit is pounded enough to absorb paste but need not become a smooth purée. Pork, seeds, core pieces and garnish alter the bite.
4. **Visible jackfruit does not make it vegetarian.** Chiang Mai University documents minced pork, shrimp paste and boiled fermented fish in one Lanna expression; Saveur independently documents pork belly, shrimp paste and fish sauce.
5. **Tomato and pork form are variations.** The CMU version includes minced pork and cherry tomatoes; Saveur notes restaurant, market and home versions with peanuts, minced pork or pork rinds, tomato, fried garlic or chilli.
6. **Tam Khanun and Kaeng Khanun are separate.** Michelin and CMU list the pounded salad and the liquid jackfruit curry independently; the shared young fruit does not merge their search intent.
