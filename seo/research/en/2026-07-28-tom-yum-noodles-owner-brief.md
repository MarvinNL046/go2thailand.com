## Dominant intent and owner boundary

The page owns the fresh Thai noodle-stall family **guay tiew tom yum**: a selected noodle and toppings seasoned in a hot, sour, salty and sweet tom-yum style, served either with broth (`nam`) or dry/soupless (`haeng`). It is a traveller-first recognition, ordering, allergen and cultural guide, not a universal recipe.

Keep these independent:

- `/food/tom-yum-goong/`: the classic prawn soup owner, normally built around aromatic herbs such as lemongrass and galangal.
- Boat Noodles: dark stock, pork/beef and optional blood have a different identity.
- Yen Ta Fo and Sukhothai Noodles: separate noodle families even when a vendor offers a tom-yum seasoning option.
- Instant Mama, Koka, Nissin and retailer queries: acknowledge as packaged-product intent, but do not let commercial packet terms redefine the fresh noodle-shop dish.
- Generic Tom Yum recipe, calorie, celebrity-recipe and retailer-price intent.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **Tom yum is a seasoning mode in this context.** Hot Thai Kitchen explicitly distinguishes noodle-stall tom-yum seasoning from the familiar lemongrass-infused soup. Lime juice, roasted chilli flakes, ground peanuts and often ground pork move a clear noodle bowl toward sour, spicy, salty and sweet balance.
2. **Soup or dry is a real ordering decision.** The noodle and topping family can be served with broth or tossed soupless with seasoning. Current Michelin restaurant results independently show wet and dry tom-yum noodles in actual Thai shops.
3. **The noodle remains a choice.** Small rice noodles are familiar, but wider rice noodles, rice vermicelli, glass noodles, wheat-and-egg bamee and instant Mama noodles may appear. The selected noodle changes texture and allergen checks.
4. **A white rice noodle does not make the bowl gluten-free.** Soy sauce, stock powder, meatballs, fish balls, shared strainers and optional wheat noodles still matter.
5. **Vegetarian, vegan and halal are not visual judgements.** Pork stock, ground pork, fish sauce, dried shrimp, fish balls, meatballs, seasoning powders and shared pots can be hidden.
6. **Packaged tom-yum noodles are a different use case.** They are useful travel or pantry products, but packet ingredients, allergens, portion size and preparation instructions are brand- and market-specific. The page must direct readers to the current label rather than publish one nutrition or ingredient claim.
