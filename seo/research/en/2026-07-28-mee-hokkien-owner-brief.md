## Ownership boundary

`/food/mee-hokkien/` owns Phuket-style Mee Hokkien / Hokkien mee: local identity, Hokkien-Chinese and Phuket context, thick yellow wheat-and-egg noodles, moist wok-fried texture, familiar pork/seafood/egg/greens signals, taste, heat, ordering and dietary checks.

## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- Phuket Mee Hokkien commonly uses thick yellow wheat-and-egg noodles. Thin rice-noodle variants can exist at a shop, but they are a separate choice rather than evidence that the core yellow noodle is gluten-free.
- Pork, squid, prawns, fish, fish cake, char siu, egg, choy sum, Chinese kale/cabbage and shallot occur across documented bowls. No single bowl contains all of them.
- The noodles are wok-fried but can remain moist and sauce-coated, between dry fried noodles and soup. Stock, soy or oyster-style seasoning can drive this texture.
- Heat is often secondary to savoury, smoky and gently salty flavours; chilli flakes, chilli sauce or vinegar may be added at the table. Ask rather than assume mildness.
- Wheat/gluten and egg are direct noodle checks. Prawn, squid, fish, fish cake, oyster sauce, soy, pork stock, char siu and shared wok/oil add shellfish, mollusc, fish, soy, wheat, pork and cross-contact boundaries.
- A seafood-only visual does not prove pork-free stock or halal status. A vegetarian request needs verified noodles, stock, sauces, protein and separate wok handling.
- Seafood, pork and egg need complete cooking and safe holding. Runny egg is an optional shop choice, not a universal requirement or safety guarantee.
- Never publish a fixed price, guaranteed restaurant ranking, calorie total, health verdict, automatic gluten-free/halal status, guaranteed mildness, one authentic ingredient list or invented origin chronology.
