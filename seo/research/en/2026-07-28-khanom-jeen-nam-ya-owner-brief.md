## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- Khanom jeen names the noodle system; nam ya names this fish-curry sauce. The full dish is not a curry soup with noodles mixed in at the stove.
- Fresh khanom jeen noodles are round rice noodles. Some are fermented and gently tangy; many current versions are not. Never state fermentation as universal.
- Fish is the identity of nam ya. Documented versions can add shrimp paste and fish sauce, making both fish and crustacean checks necessary.
- Coconut milk, chilli, lemongrass, galangal, fingerroot, turmeric, garlic/shallot, tamarind and palm sugar are familiar signals, not one mandatory formula.
- Noodles are commonly portioned at room temperature while curry is ladled warm or hot; vegetables, herbs, pickles and egg may be added separately. Actual service temperature and hot-holding require observation.
- Classic nam ya is not vegetarian or vegan. A vegetable sauce over khanom jeen is a different sauce and must be named and checked independently.
- Rice noodles do not make the full plate automatically gluten-free: curry paste, fish sauce, seasoning, pickles, fried sides, substitute wheat noodles and shared equipment can add gluten.
- Seafood can be halal, but shrimp paste, fish sauce, curry paste, stock, packaged seasoning, alcohol-containing additions and shared preparation vary. Verify certification or the kitchen rather than inferring from fish.
- Fresh noodles, cooked fish sauce, coconut milk, eggs and cut vegetables are perishable. Do not publish one universal room-temperature window or shelf life; use current vendor handling, product labels and food-safety guidance.
- Never publish a fixed price, calorie total, health verdict, automatic dietary status, permanent restaurant ranking, universal heat score, exact shelf life, compulsory fermentation or one authentic formula.
