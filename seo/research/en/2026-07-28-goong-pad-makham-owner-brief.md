## Dominant intent and owner boundary

The page owns **Goong Pad Makham / Thai tamarind prawns** as a traveller-first southern Thai prawn dish: prawns coated with a glossy tamarind-led sauce balanced with palm sugar and fish sauce or another salty seasoning, commonly supported by shallot, garlic, coriander and optional chilli. Fried shallots, dried chilli or cashews are variations rather than compulsory identity.

Keep these independent:

- Pad Thai: a rice-noodle dish that may also use tamarind but has a different component system.
- Goong Ob Woon Sen: baked or braised prawns with glass noodles.
- Tom Yum Goong and Pla Goong: separate soup and salad owners.
- Generic sweet-and-sour prawns, Chinese tamarind shrimp and global prawn-sauce recipes.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **Tamarind is the acid, not an automatic premixed sauce.** A documented Thai specialist advises checking ready-to-use tamarind paste for origin, colour and pourable consistency; pulp prepared with water is another route. Product sugar, salt and additives vary.
2. **The balance is the owner.** Tamarind, palm sugar and fish sauce create sour, sweet and salty tension. Chilli is useful but optional, so the sauce should not be universally described as hot.
3. **Prawn technique varies.** A documented home expression sears prawns and finishes them in reduced sauce; current Michelin Southern examples include fried or deep-fried prawns topped or coated with tamarind sauce. Both belong without claiming one universal technique.
4. **Cashew is a real variation, not a default.** Thailand Foundation documents a named tamarind-prawn-and-cashew variant. Tree-nut checks therefore matter even when the base dish photograph has no nuts.
5. **Dietary boundaries are hidden in the sauce.** Crustacean is intrinsic; fish sauce, chicken stock, soy/oyster sauce, wheat-containing bottled sauce, fried-shallot coatings and shared oil may appear. Prawn does not automatically mean halal or gluten-free.
6. **Pad Thai shares tamarind, not the dish owner.** One is a prawn-and-sauce plate usually paired with rice; the other is a composed stir-fried rice-noodle dish with egg, peanut and other signals.
