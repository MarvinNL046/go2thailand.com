## Source evidence and conservative use

- Useful evidence: the Thai-language official page identifies kaeng man nok as a local Phatthana Nikhom dish and describes man nok as an ingredient used in savoury and sweet preparations.
- Boundary: health claims on the source page are excluded and the dish is presented as a province route, not a guaranteed old-town menu item.

### Travelfish — Food and drink in Lopburi

- URL: <https://www.travelfish.org/eatandmeet/thailand/central_thailand/lopburi/lopburi/eat>
- Current DFS parse: 4,433 markdown characters.
- Useful evidence: the city has a municipal morning-market layer, a station-side evening-market layer and Talad Tha Pho; examples include stewed pork rice, fried chicken, grilled pork, rad na, roti, coconut pancakes, filled sticky rice, crisp sweets and pork noodles.
- Boundary: named venues, prices, hours and market schedules are historic editorial observations and are not copied as current guarantees.

### Michelin Guide — Lawo culinary traditions

- URL: <https://guide.michelin.com/th/en/article/features/why-you-should-visit-lopburi-and-discover-the-legend-of-the-culinary-arts-in-lawo>
- DFS produced a zero-content capture; current SERP evidence still exposed the title and relevant snippets.
- Useful evidence: Lopburi's food identity is described through Lao/Thai Puan and Thai Boeng traditions, seasonal fish and vegetables, kaeng som Puan and tamarind-led cooking.
- Boundary: the owner uses these as cultural directions rather than claiming one universal recipe, current venue or Michelin-listed Lopburi restaurant.

### Thepsatri Rajabhat University — local-food teaching material

- URL: <https://library.tru.ac.th/aritc/images/academic/book/b65672.pdf>
- DFS produced a zero-content capture; the indexed university text exposes pla som fak, pad prik khing khai khem and tom pre as Lopburi food vocabulary.
- Boundary: transliteration and recipe details vary. The page explains what to ask for and never promises availability.
