## Dominant intent and owner boundary

The page owns **Tom Som Pla / Thai sour fish soup** as a traveller-first dish family: fish gently cooked in a light sour broth whose souring agent and aromatic structure vary by region and kitchen. Tamarind is familiar, while southern examples may use Garcinia/asam, bilimbi, roselle or other local sour fruit. Fish, sourness and a broth-led service are stronger identity signals than one compulsory spice paste, colour or heat level.

Keep these independent:

- Tom Yam Pla: the fish branch of the tom yum family, commonly structured around lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime and lime-led hot-sour seasoning.
- Gaeng Som / Kaeng Som: a sour curry with a curry-paste and often vegetable-led structure; southern versions can be intensely hot and turmeric-yellow.
- Tom Khlong: a separate sour soup family commonly associated with roasted or dried aromatics and fish.
- Pla Tom Kamin: a southern turmeric-fish soup that sources sometimes group near Tom Som, but whose turmeric identity deserves a named distinction.
- Generic Thai coconut fish soup, fish stew, retailer, health, calorie and recipe-only intent.

## Evidence-backed information gain

1. **Tom Som is a family, not one fixed formula.** Current Michelin reporting describes several possible souring agents and a familiar paste with kapi, shallot and coriander root; a Phuket family expression is far simpler and uses sour fruit, shrimp paste, lemongrass and shallot.
2. **Sour fruit can identify place.** The documented Phuket Tom Som Pla Sai uses sand whiting and explains that southern provinces choose among Garcinia/asam, salak, hibiscus, tamarind and young leaves according to local availability.
3. **Turmeric is a branch, not the universal definition.** A Thai academic cuisine record links Tom Som with Pla Tom Kamin as a mild, clear yellow southern soup, but the Phuket and Michelin records show versions without compulsory turmeric.
4. **Fresh fish matters, but one species does not.** Michelin names mackerel, mullet, sea bass and tilapia; the Phuket source centres local sand whiting and allows other fresh firm fish. Availability and sustainability remain vendor-level questions.
5. **The hidden allergen is often the broth.** Fish is intrinsic. Kapi or shrimp paste adds crustacean; stock, bottled seasoning, soy or shared utensils can add further allergens. A clear appearance does not prove gluten-free, halal, vegetarian or crustacean-free status.
6. **Search results confuse three sour owners.** Tom Som prioritises a sour fish broth; Tom Yam Pla uses the tom-yum aromatic family; Gaeng Som is curry-paste led and commonly includes vegetables. The page should answer the comparison without redirecting any owner.
