## Fact and safety boundaries

- Boat Noodles are a Central Thai noodle-soup family historically associated with vendors serving from boats along canals. The exact canal, inventor and decade should not be stated as settled without stronger primary evidence.
- Small bowls are a strong historical and modern service signal, but portion size is not fixed and a restaurant may serve one larger bowl.
- Pork and beef versions both occur. Sliced meat, meatballs, liver or other offal can appear, but no one combination is mandatory.
- Blood is a documented traditional broth enricher and may be associated with `guay tiew nam tok`. It is not guaranteed in every current bowl; blood-free versions exist. Ask the vendor rather than making a visual assumption.
- `Nam tok` can mean a blood-enriched noodle broth in this context, while another Thai dish family uses the same words for a grilled-meat salad. Context and the complete menu name matter.
- A dark, concentrated broth can combine bones or stock, soy sauces, fermented soybean paste, fish sauce, sugar, vinegar and aromatic spices such as cinnamon, star anise, coriander, galangal or garlic. No one ingredient list is universal.
- Sen lek is a familiar rice-noodle choice, but sen mee, sen yai, bamee, glass noodles or instant noodles may be offered. Noodle choice changes wheat, egg and preparation boundaries.
- Morning glory / water spinach, bean sprouts, Thai basil, coriander, fried garlic, chilli flakes, chilli vinegar, sugar, fish sauce and pork rind can appear in the bowl or at the table.
- Blood, meat, liver and meatballs require regulated sourcing and tested cooking. Do not tell travellers that boiling automatically proves safe handling or provide casual home substitution advice.
- Soy, wheat, fish, egg, meat stock, pork, beef, blood, offal and shared strainers/pots create allergen, dietary and halal checks. Rice noodles alone do not make the complete bowl gluten-free.
- Never publish a fixed price, number of bowls, calorie count, health verdict, guaranteed heat level, automatic halal or vegetarian status, universal blood content, permanent restaurant ranking or unsupported storage time.
