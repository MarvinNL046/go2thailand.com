## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- Gaeng Hang Lay is a rich Northern Thai pork curry commonly slow-braised until tender. Pork belly, pork neck or ribs appear in documented versions; no single cut is mandatory.
- It is commonly distinguished from many central Thai curries by the absence of coconut milk, but verify the actual kitchen rather than treating an editorial pattern as an allergen guarantee.
- Ginger, garlic, tamarind, dried chilli and warm spice are recurring signals. Hang Lay powder, masala-style spice, pickled garlic, peanuts, pineapple, palm/brown sugar, shrimp paste and regional vegetables vary.
- The flavour can be rich, aromatic, sweet-sour and gently to moderately hot. It is not automatically mild, sweet or nutty.
- Peanuts and shrimp paste appear in documented versions. Fish sauce, soy, wheat or packaged seasoning may appear elsewhere; ask about the complete curry paste and kitchen.
- Standard Gaeng Hang Lay contains pork and is not vegetarian. Pork and possible shrimp/fish seasonings require a direct halal check.
- Pork pieces must be cooked safely and braised until the chosen cut is tender. Surface colour, sauce thickness and long cooking time alone are not a documented doneness measurement.
- Never publish a fixed price, universal recipe, guaranteed origin story, permanent best restaurant, calorie total, health verdict, guaranteed heat or automatic gluten-free/halal/nut-free status.
