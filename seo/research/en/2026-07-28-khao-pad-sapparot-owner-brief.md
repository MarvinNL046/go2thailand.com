## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- `Khao` means rice, `pad/phat` means stir-fried and `sapparot/sapparod` means pineapple. Spelling variation is normal.
- Pineapple is integral. Fresh is strongly represented in Thai specialist methods, while canned appears in other versions; do not universalise one source or sweetness level.
- Curry powder gives the characteristic warm golden, aromatic direction. Turmeric, white pepper or other spice blends can appear; this is not a wet Thai curry and one curry-powder brand is not compulsory.
- Jasmine rice is the familiar base. Freshly cooked rice can work when the complete method controls moisture; “day-old rice” is not a safety or authenticity requirement. Previously cooked rice must be cooled, stored and reheated according to current official guidance.
- Shrimp, egg, cashew and raisins form one recognisable expression but are not mandatory in every bowl. Chicken, pork, tofu, mixed seafood or no visible protein can appear.
- A hollowed pineapple is optional theatre. The rice remains the same owner when served on a plate; the shell must be clean and dry and does not make the dish safer, fresher or more authentic.
- The dish is often mild and warmly aromatic. Phrik nam pla or other chilli condiment may make a bite hot; ask for it separately.
- Crustacean can enter through shrimp or shrimp paste; fish through fish sauce; egg and cashew are common; soy and wheat/gluten can enter through soy/oyster/seasoning sauces; mollusc, sesame, peanut and coconut can appear by variation or shared wok.
- A vegetable-looking bowl is not automatically vegetarian or vegan because fish sauce, shrimp paste, egg and stock may remain. A halal order needs actual protein, sauces, alcohol-containing seasonings and shared-wok checks rather than a “no pork” shortcut.
- Never publish a fixed price, calorie total, health verdict, automatic dietary status, permanent restaurant ranking, compulsory pineapple shell, universal mildness, exact storage time beyond cited official guidance or one authentic formula.
