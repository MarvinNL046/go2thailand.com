### Amanpuri

[Amanpuri](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/amanpuri-thailand)

Amanpuri is a golden oldie, and they simply don't make ‘em like this anymore. The Thai temple-like pavilions designed in 1988 have been imitated by resorts all over Southeast Asia, but these, scattered across Phuket's palm-tufted hills, accented with timeworn Buddhist antiques and fresh orchids, still feel like the real deal. The resort cascades down five tiers of sand-coloured stairs, meeting one of the loveliest little beaches on this side of the island. Days start with the sound of hornbills rustling in the palm leaves overhead and end with the pling-plong from the ranat ek (a traditional Thai percussion instrument) that fills the main terrace with music every evening. The property frequently appears in Condé Nast Traveller ’s Spa Guide, and for good reason: its Holistic Wellness Centre, which offers everything from crystal healing sessions to hi-tech IV infusions and medical check-ups, is one of Thailand’s very best. Despite the worldwide acclaim, the resort remains blissfully serene even during the high season, although scoring the best canvas-covered beach cabanas can turn into a game of musical chairs. Ask one of the hosts to try to reserve your favourite one.

Where to stay in Phuket

Phuket is the largest of [Thailand's islands](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/thailand-islands-guide), and flights from London are direct, so hotel options are vast. It’s blessed with more than 30 different bays and beaches, and while they all score top marks in terms of postcard prettiness, their vibes (and, therefore, their accommodation line-ups) are varied. It's also exceptionally well-connected. Just a short speedboat ride away are Koh Pangang – home of the infamous full moon party – and Koh Toa, where daytrippers can enjoy some of the clearest snorkelling spots in the area. But on the island itself, there's plenty to explore.

Most of the holiday action happens around the bays on its western coast, from the laidback Nai Harn and Rawai beaches in the coral-fringed deep south to the seemingly endless stretch of crowd-free sand of Mai Khao on the island's north. The nightlife around Patong is vibrant, Kamala is best for family fun, and there’s an inspired restaurant scene around the expat hub of Bang Tao. Cross the island's jungle-cloaked heart for a culture fix in Phuket Town, where cafes and cocktail bars contradict the Sino-Portuguese warehouses lining its oldest streets. Nearby, the quiet and craggy coastline of Cape Panwa delivers unforgettable views over the island-dotted Phang Nga Bay. Here, we've narrowed down where to stay to our favourite Phuket hotels.

## Sri Panwa

Plenty has changed on Phuket since Sri Panwa opened its doors 20 years ago, but this hotel’s secluded spot on the southernmost tip of the island will help you feel blissfully cut off from the lively hubbub elsewhere. It’s here, on a steep hillside dotted with astonishing suites and villas with pools, that you’re just as likely to bump into a celebrity (Gordon Ramsay, Jason Statham and Rita Ora have all stayed) as much as on an oldtimer (some guests have been visiting twice a year for two decades).

It’s tempting to hole up in your suite or villa for your entire stay: each has a private pool, simple, clutter-free interiors and sensational sea views. But venture out, and you’ll discover what really makes Sri Panwa special: like sinking into a floor cushion and sipping cocktails beneath an apricot-hued sky at Baba Nest (easily one of the best sundowner spots on the island) or settling in for a 15-course Japanese tasting menu at iki.

In the the thatched-roof rooms of the spa you can try any number of treatments, from the quirky (mango sticky rice body polishes, for example) to the highly effective (the Thai herbal healer starts with a warm compress of galangal, kefir, lime and lemongrass and is followed by a pleasingly deep tissue massage that will quickly soothe any long-haul flight tension). More recently, a collaboration with the BDMS clinic has seen everyone from stressed-out CEOs to burnt-out influencers (yes, really) check in for two- to 14-night sleep programmes, with personalised sleep testing and curated IV therapy, mindfulness, sleep healing and massage sessions that promise to address the root causes of sleep disruption. And while the focus of a stay here is on slow moments and deep relaxation, you’re just a 20-minute drive from the pretty architecture and charming energy of Phuket Town.

Price: from around £330 per night

## The Pavilions

It’d be right to address the metaphorical elephant in the room: what The Pavilions doesn’t have is direct access to its own stretch of beach. While this is a dealbreaker for some, it’s not for the 20- and 30-something couples that choose this hotel – these are people who are happy to lounge by their own private pool in the morning, before hopping on a moped and venturing out to see more of Phuket – and its beaches – in the afternoon. Its location, on the north-west coast of Phuket, makes it a good spot to start exploring from, but there’s plenty to keep you here too, including two pools (one of which is adult-only) and a huge lounger-lined lagoon with blue and green LED lights that sparkle at night.

While the interiors in some suites and villas may raise a few eyebrows, with their ketchup-red sofas, butterfly-adorned rugs and black furniture, it’s a refreshing and fun break away from the more traditional Thai decor you’ll find at a lot of hotels. And if that is more of your thing, some rooms are much more toned down, with creamy palettes, floaty fabrics and four-poster beds. Both room categories are huge in comparison to other options on the island, particularly the villas, which come with private pools and give you exceptional bang for your buck. Restaurants include a great Thai and a good Italian for when pad thai fatigue sets in, and there’s a dreamy spa, surrounded by jungle greenery, with a treatment menu featuring Moringa Project products, a family-run business that uses oil from the pods of the moringa, or ‘miracle’ tree.

Price : from around £159 per night
## Rosewood Phuket

This hotel may be located within eyeshot of Patong Beach, the island's rowdy epicentre, but from the moment you turn up at its monogrammed entrance gates, it feels a world away. Sprawled over a lush hillside dotted with ancient banyan trees and indigenous foliage, its 71 elegant villas and suites feel more like island homes than hotel digs, thanks to their cosy living-room spaces decorated with keepsakes, fresh flowers and coffee-table books. The Asaya spa is one of Rosewood’s best in the region, where serious spa junkies should book themselves into the Asaya Villa, a spacious day-use spa retreat with a private watsu pool and Himalayan salt sauna. The four restaurants, providing treats from Italian comfort food to poolside surf and turf, are roomy and relaxed, with Ta Khai (modelled after a Thai fishing village, dishing up authentic Thai plates from open kitchens) being the rightful signature spot. There’s also a seriously good kids’ club – wooden toys, educational activities, the works – making it a great stay for the whole family.

Price: from around £450 per night
## Banyan Tree Phuket

The adjoining stretch of Bang Tao beach might not be the most picturesque, and the setting between apartment buildings not the most enticing – but none of that matters after you’ve checked in at one of Banyan Tree’s private pool villas. Somewhat confusingly, the villas – classic Thai affairs with pointed roofs and teakwood floors – are spread over three distinct areas: book the adult-only Spa Sanctuary villas for all-you-can-spa perks, or the DoublePool villas for twin pools and heaps of space. But even the entry-level villas at the primary address offer plenty of comforts, with open-air bathtubs, walled gardens and pools long enough for laps.

Price: from around £240 per night
## Aleenta Phuket-Phang Nga Resort & Spa

Strictly speaking, Aleenta Phuket is located in the Phang Nga region, just to the north of Phuket. The reason you’re here? To escape the crowds. The beach is the perfect place to do that: the palm-fringed sweep of sand is dotted with a few quiet-seeking travellers and some friendly dogs. The contemporary all-white beachside villas are the perfect rooms to make the most of the setting: with large pools and rooms each with its own entrance (separated from the main villa living space), they’re perfect for groups of friends or grown-up families. Little guests will be happy here too, with a small but sweet kids' club featuring a shallow pool and slide, plus activities like tie-dye t-shirt making. Settle in for relaxed lunches and evening meals – think burgers or curries – at the beachside restaurant, or try the plant-based restaurant, Seasons, one of the few in the region. The chef’s multi-course tasting menu is worth the trip to Aleenta, even if you’re not staying.

Price: from around £180 per night

## COMO Point Yamu

There are only a few hotels of note on Phuket’s sunrise-facing east coast, with COMO Point Yamu being the brightest of the bunch – and in our opinion, one of the best hotels in all of Thailand. Set on the tip of Cape Yamu, this wellness-focused place balances out its straight-lined design with inherently Thai touches (gorgeous Buddhist artworks among them) and a pinch of Mediterranean flair courtesy of Italian designer Paola Navone. Rooms are sleek, done up in crisp whites and electric-blue hues, and have fabulous views over Phang Nga Bay. There is no direct beach access, so soak up the sun in the brilliant 330ft pool.

Price: from around £200 per night
## The Surin

Sharing the same beach and architect – the late Ed Tuttle – as Amanpuri next door, this long-standing classic on Phuket's west coast has garnered a loyal following for its well-priced rooms and outstanding service. The dramatic Thai-inspired Lomtalay restaurant and sweeping granite staircases leading up to it have Tuttle's fingerprints all over it, as does the lounger-lined hexagonal swimming pool clad in chalkboard-black anthracite. Rooms, on the contrary, are bright and breezy affairs with palettes of white and grey warmed up with touches of gemstone-hued silk. All have ocean-facing lounge decks, and some come with private plunge pools. Another perk: cream-coloured Pansea Beach is one of Phuket's prettiest and is hard to reach for non-hotel guests, leaving it blissfully empty of tourist crowds.

Price: from around £250 per night
## Keemala

Look for Phuket inspiration on Instagram, and you’re guaranteed to come across Keemala, a highly photogenic hotel pitched in the Kamala rainforest on the island’s west coast. Its hillside retreats range from glamorous safari tents to two-storey tree houses and come with private pools and adjoining sundecks (book the Bird’s Nest Pool Villas if you’re after extra space). The interiors meld safari chic with [jungle](https://www.cntraveller.com/topic/jungle) dwellings – teakwood walls, stone-carved bathtubs and fabrics in earthy tones. If you manage to leave your cocoon, the nearest beach is a 10-minute drive away, but Keemala’s expert Mala Spa and health-focused Mala Restaurant (opt for the southern Thai specialities) provide just as many opportunities to up the feel-good factor.

Price: from around £400 per night
## Sala Phuket

Homegrown hotels generally play second fiddle to the big-brand-name digs on Phuket, but SALA Phuket is a notable exception. Don’t let its location near the airport turn you off – noise from planes is minimal, and wonderfully deserted Mai Khao beach on its doorstep is one of the island's prettiest. There are a handful of balcony suites, but the pool villas are the ones to book. Each is a white-washed sanctuary accentuated by shades of taupe, grey and Portuguese-tiled, open-air bathrooms that reference the island’s trade history. The SALA Spa has an excellent reputation, as does the restaurant – order the fragrant beef-cheek curry with mushrooms and betel leaves.

Price: from around £120 per night
## V Villas Phuket

With just 19 villas cantilevered on the hills above Ao Yon bay on Phuket's southeastern tip, this straight-lined and high-ceilinged retreat is a bijou hideaway that's big on service and views. Each of its sun-flooded villas, some with multiple bedrooms, is hugged by a private infinity pool long enough for laps and appointed with a butler on call for boating excursions, spa reservations and floating breakfasts (if that's your jam). The main restaurant, YON Beach House, serves a Thai-European medley of tuna tartare, tiger prawn-topped noodles, and squid ink risotto. And even if you're not sleeping over, the lounge-y, daybed-dotted rooftop bar is worth a stop for the spectacular sunsets alone.

Price: from around £547 per night
