## Best Seafood Restaurants in Pattaya

Seafood is Pattaya’s strongest suit — a working fishing coast, daily catches, and fierce competition. The best of it isn’t in central Pattaya at all: it’s a 15-minute drive south, along the Na Jomtien waterfront, where big open-air seafood houses sit right on the sand.

### 1. Pupen Seafood — Best Overall

Where: Na Jomtien beachfront (south end of Jomtien Beach) Price range: ฿400–900 per person Best for: Fresh whole fish, sunset tables on the sand, groups

Pupen is the Na Jomtien seafood house locals and Bangkok weekenders point you to first — 4.3 stars across more than 19,000 Google reviews, which for a Thai seafood restaurant is remarkable consistency at scale. Tables run right down to the water, the seafood display is out front so you pick what you want, and the kitchen cooks it Thai-style without fuss.

Order the steamed sea bass with lime and garlic (pla kapong neung manao), stir-fried crab with yellow curry powder (poo pad pong karee), and grilled prawns by the kilo. Mains mostly land in the ฿200–500 range; a serious spread for two runs ฿800–1,800 depending on how far you go on crab and prawns.

Practical tip: Go for the 5–6pm slot — you get the sunset off the water and beat the queue. A Grab from central Pattaya costs ฿150–250 each way.

Choose-your-own-catch is standard at Pattaya’s best seafood restaurants — a display of live crab or fresh-landed fish tells you exactly what you’re getting.

### 2. Nang Nual — The Institution

Where: South end of Walking Street, on the water Price range: ฿500–1,200 per person Best for: Special occasions, large groups, eating seafood in town

Nang Nual has been feeding Pattaya visitors since the 1970s and is still the best seafood option that doesn’t require leaving town — a big two-storey pavilion over the water at the quiet end of [Walking Street](https://pattayapointer.com/areas/walking-street/). Service is built for large groups and the seafood is reliably good, if priced above the Na Jomtien houses for the location.

Highlights: grilled river prawns, curry-powder crab, and whole fish grilled or steamed, priced by weight — confirm the per-kilo price before they take it to the kitchen, which is standard practice at every weight-priced seafood house in Thailand.

### 3. Preecha Seafood — The Local Favourite

Where: Na Jomtien, on the beach Price range: ฿350–800 per person Best for: A quieter, more Thai alternative to Pupen

A few hundred metres from Pupen and the place many Thai families pick instead — slightly cheaper, less famous, same sea-on-three-sides setting and the same day-fresh Gulf seafood. More than 9,000 Google reviews. If Pupen has a 45-minute queue (weekends in high season), walk down the beach to Preecha and you give up very little.

## Best Thai Restaurants in Pattaya

Thai food in Pattaya ranges from ฿60 street plates to white-tablecloth royal Thai. These are the sit-down places worth your evening.

### 4. Ruenthai — Best for a Proper Thai Dinner

Where: Second Road (opposite the Central Pattaya area) Price range: ฿300–600 per person Best for: Couples, first-timers, a Thai dinner with atmosphere

Ruenthai is a traditional teak-house compound in the middle of the city — garden seating, carved wood, and classical Thai dance performances in the evenings. It’s touristy in the good sense: the setting is the draw, and the kitchen doesn’t coast on it.

Must-order: tom yum goong with river prawns, massaman curry, som tum made properly spicy if you ask, and the grilled seafood platters. Mango sticky rice to finish. Book or arrive by 6:30pm for a garden table.

Classic Thai plates — som tam, rice and stir-fries are the backbone of any good Pattaya food run.

### 5. Nara Thai Cuisine — Reliable, Air-Conditioned, Excellent

Where: Terminal 21 shopping mall, North Pattaya (2nd floor) Price range: ฿300–600 per person Best for: Families, non-adventurous eaters, guaranteed quality

Nara is the Bangkok restaurant group whose Pattaya branch sits in Terminal 21 — and at 4.6 stars over 1,500+ reviews it’s arguably the most consistently rated Thai restaurant in the city. Pad thai with river prawns, tom kha gai, green curry, crab fried rice — the standards, executed properly, in air conditioning, with an English menu. If you’re taking someone who’s nervous about plastic-stool dining, this is the answer.

### 6. Leng Kee — Best Budget Thai (and Open Nearly Around the Clock)

Where: Central Pattaya Road (Pattaya Klang) Price range: ฿100–300 per person Best for: Solo travellers, late-night bowls, eating where locals eat

Leng Kee is a Pattaya institution — a big, bright, no-frills Thai-Chinese restaurant on Central Pattaya Road that’s been running for decades and barely closes. Roast duck on rice, khao tom (rice soup), pad kra pao with a fried egg, and noodle plates, most under ฿150. If someone asks where to get the best pad kra pao in Pattaya at 2am, this is the standing answer. Thai families at half the tables at any hour is the quality guarantee.

## Best International Restaurants in Pattaya

Pattaya’s expat population (40,000+ long-termers) and its mix of Russian, German, Scandinavian, Indian, Chinese and Australian visitors has produced a genuinely strong international scene.

### 7. Casa Pascal — Best European Fine Dining

Where: Second Road (near Soi Diana) Price range: ฿1,200–2,800 per person Best for: Special occasions, wine lovers, the Sunday brunch

Swiss-owned Casa Pascal has been the benchmark for European fine dining in Pattaya for over 15 years, and its 4.6-star average across 1,300+ reviews says the standard hasn’t slipped. Imported European ingredients — truffles, aged cheese, proper veal — combined with local seafood, plus the best wine cellar in the city.

The Sunday brunch buffet is legendary in the expat community and books out — reserve a week ahead. Weekday evenings, the duck breast and the pan-seared sea bass are the signatures.

Casa Pascal brings genuine European fine dining to Pattaya — imported ingredients, a serious wine list, and 15 years of consistency.

### 8. Glass House Boho — The Beachfront Stunner

Where: Na Jomtien beachfront Price range: ฿400–900 per person Best for: Couples, long lunches on the sand, sunset dinners

Glass House Boho is the most photographed restaurant on this coast — a plant-filled, driftwood-and-rattan space that spills onto the Na Jomtien sand, with nearly 7,000 Google reviews behind it. The menu is Thai plus seafood plus enough Western dishes to keep a mixed table happy; the cooking is better than a place this pretty needs to be. Book a beachfront table ahead on weekends. Combine it with a [Jomtien Beach day](https://pattayapointer.com/guides/jomtien-beach-complete-guide/) — it’s the natural lunch stop.

### 9. The Sky Gallery — Best View in Pattaya

Where: Pratumnak Hill, cliffside above Cozy Beach Price range: ฿400–900 per person Best for: Sunset, proposals, showing off the coastline

The Sky Gallery is a sprawling lawn-and-terrace restaurant on the Pratumnak cliffside with the best dining view in the city — the full sweep of the bay towards Koh Larn. More than 10,000 reviews and still 4.3 stars. The menu runs Thai and international; the food is good, the view is the point. Go 45 minutes before sunset, take the lawn beanbags for drinks or a proper table for dinner.

### 10. Beefeater — Best for Steaks

Where: Second Road Price range: ฿600–1,800 per person Best for: Steak lovers who’ve had enough rice for one week

With Bruno’s gone, Beefeater Steak House and Pub is the clear steak leader — a Pattaya fixture with a 4.6-star average across nearly 4,000 reviews, which for a farang steakhouse is close to unheard of. Imported beef cooked to the temperature you actually asked for, proper sides, honest portions. The fillet and the ribeye are the orders; book on weekends.

## Best Restaurants for Families in Pattaya

Feeding kids in Pattaya is rarely a problem — the city is very used to families — but knowing where to go saves time and meltdowns.

### 11. The Market Pattaya Bistro & Grill — The Easy Option

Where: Ground floor, Central Festival, Beach Road Price range: ฿300–600 per person Best for: Kids, air conditioning, everyone-finds-something menus

Inside Central Festival mall with a 4.7-star rating, The Market covers Thai standards, Western grills, burgers and pasta in a bright, casual room — high chairs, English menus, staff who’ve seen every toddler meltdown imaginable. After a hot beach morning, an air-conditioned mall lunch is not a compromise, it’s a strategy. The Hilton tower above the mall also houses [Horizon](https://pattayapointer.com/hotels/hilton-pattaya/), Pattaya’s best rooftop, if the adults want a sunset drink later.

Pattaya’s restaurant scene is family-friendly — most spots have high chairs, English menus, and Western options alongside Thai dishes.

### 12. Cabbages & Condoms — Quirky, Good, Charitable

Where: Pratumnak Hill, beachfront at the [Birds & Bees Resort](https://pattayapointer.com/hotels/birds-bees-resort/) Price range: ฿350–700 per person Best for: Memorable meals, good Thai food, supporting a good cause

Yes, the name is exactly what you think — the restaurant is run by Thailand’s Population & Community Development Association and its profits fund public health work. What most guides get wrong is the location: it’s not in town, it’s a genuinely lovely terraced garden running down to a private beach on Pratumnak Hill. The Thai food is properly good (4.4 stars, 3,200+ reviews), kids find the mannequins dressed in condoms hilarious, and sunset from the lower terrace is a bonus nobody expects.

## Best Cheap Eats in Pattaya (Under ฿300)

Pattaya’s best-value meals are in its night markets and local Thai spots — and the night markets double as an evening out.

### 13. Thepprasit Night Market — The Big One

Where: Thepprasit Road, between Sukhumvit and Jomtien Open: Evenings, roughly 5–10:30pm; busiest Friday–Sunday

Pattaya’s biggest night market and the best single stop for cheap eating in the city — hundreds of stalls of grilled squid and prawns, moo ping (pork skewers), som tum pounded to order, pad kra pao, boat noodles, khao kha moo, mango sticky rice and Thai desserts, nearly everything ฿40–120. Two people eat themselves silly for ฿400. It’s a 10-minute Grab from central Pattaya and worth the trip.

### 14. Tree Town and Soi Buakhao

Where: Soi Buakhao, central Pattaya

[Tree Town](https://pattayapointer.com/activities/tree-town-pattaya-night-market/) is an open-air food-and-bar village in the middle of Soi Buakhao — dozens of stalls doing Thai and international plates at ฿80–200, tables in the middle, live music at night. The surrounding Soi Buakhao strip and the daytime [Buakhao Market](https://pattayapointer.com/activities/buakhao-market/) are central Pattaya’s honest-price food zone generally; if a menu on Beach Road annoys you, walk ten minutes inland and pay half.

### 15. Rompho Market — Jomtien’s Local Food Hub

Where: Thepprasit Soi 17, Jomtien side

Rompho is where Jomtien’s Thai residents and long-stay expats actually eat — a working market ringed with food stands doing duck noodles, khao man gai, grilled chicken with sticky rice, and curries by the ladle at local prices (฿50–100 a plate). Zero polish, full flavour. Come hungry, point at what looks good.

Pattaya’s markets are the best place to buy fresh tropical fruit at local prices — often ฿40–80 for a full bag of rambutan or mangosteen.

### Beach Road / Central Pattaya

The most tourist-heavy strip, with prices to match. Best plays: The Market at Central Festival for families, Horizon on the Hilton’s 34th floor for a rooftop dinner, and the honest side-soi Thai spots between Beach Road and Second Road. Staying centrally at the [Amari Pattaya](https://pattayapointer.com/hotels/amari-pattaya/) or [Hilton](https://pattayapointer.com/hotels/hilton-pattaya/) puts all of it in walking range.

### Second Road / Soi Buakhao

The heart of real-price dining. Casa Pascal, Ruenthai and Beefeater anchor the Second Road end; Tree Town and a hundred cheap kitchens anchor Soi Buakhao. If you only learn one food neighbourhood in Pattaya, learn this one.

### Walking Street / South Pattaya

Nang Nual for seafood over the water, plus the late-night noodle and grill stalls that feed the bar crowd. After 9pm this area is fully in nightlife mode — see our [Pattaya nightlife guide](https://pattayapointer.com/guides/pattaya-nightlife-guide/).

### Pratumnak Hill

Pattaya’s quiet green hill holds two of its best meals with a view: The Sky Gallery on the cliffside and Cabbages & Condoms on its private beach.

### Jomtien and Na Jomtien

The seafood coast. Pupen and Preecha on the Na Jomtien sand, Glass House Boho for the pretty version, Rompho Market for the local version. Calmer than town in every way — a full day of eating from market breakfast to sunset seafood works well here.

### Naklua / North Pattaya

The most local-feeling area, plus Terminal 21’s polished food options (Nara Thai on level 2, a solid food court on the top floor). The fish market end of Naklua supplies half the seafood restaurants in this guide.

## What to Order in Pattaya: Essential Dishes

If you’re new to Thai food, these dishes best showcase Pattaya’s ingredients and cooking style:

Pla Kapong Neung Manao — steamed sea bass with lime, garlic and chilli (฿300–500) — the benchmark dish at any seafood house

Poo Pad Pong Karee — stir-fried crab with yellow curry powder and egg (฿400–800) — uniquely Thai, and the thing to order

Tom Yum Goong — hot and sour prawn soup (฿150–350) — best with fresh Gulf prawns

Thai Standards:

Pad Kra Pao — holy basil stir-fry with your choice of protein (฿60–150) — order it “kai dao” (fried egg on top); Leng Kee and the night markets do the best in town

Khao Pad — Thai fried rice (฿60–150) — the reliable fallback

Som Tum — green papaya salad (฿50–120) — say “mai phet” if you can’t take full spice

Something Different:

Khao Kha Moo — braised pork leg over rice (฿60–100) — the slow-food specialty of Thai street kitchens

Pad See Ew — wide rice noodles with Chinese broccoli (฿60–150) — better than pad thai, less touristy

Yam Woon Sen — glass noodle salad with seafood (฿100–180) — bright, spicy, refreshing

### When to Eat

Thai lunch runs 11am–1pm; dinner starts from 5:30pm. The Na Jomtien seafood houses and view restaurants are best hit an hour before sunset. Night markets run roughly 5–10:30pm.

### How to Order Without Thai

Point at menus with photos. Most restaurants have English menus. Google Translate’s camera function handles Thai script well. At weight-priced seafood restaurants, always confirm the per-kilo price before the fish goes to the kitchen.

### Hygiene & Water Safety

Drink bottled water only — never tap water

Reputable restaurants and busy stalls use purified ice (the cylindrical kind with the hole is factory-made)

Busy local restaurants beat quiet ones — high turnover means fresher ingredients

### Tipping

Not compulsory, appreciated. Budget spots: round up. Mid-range: ฿40–100. Fine dining (Casa Pascal, Horizon): 10% is generous, and check whether service charge is already on the bill.

### Dietary Requirements

Most Thai restaurants can adapt dishes. Tell staff “kin jay” (vegetarian, also excludes garlic and onion) or “mai sai neua sat” (no meat). Gluten-free is harder — Thai soy and oyster sauces contain gluten, so communicate clearly.

## FAQ: Eating in Pattaya

What are the best restaurants in Pattaya?

Pupen Seafood (Na Jomtien) for seafood, Ruenthai and Nara Thai Cuisine for Thai, Casa Pascal for fine dining, The Sky Gallery for the view, and Beefeater for steak. All verified open as of July 2026.

Is Pattaya food expensive?

No — it’s some of the best-value dining in Southeast Asia. A proper Thai meal costs ฿100–250; a full seafood dinner runs ฿500–900 per person; fine dining starts around ฿1,200 a head. Budget travellers eat very well at the night markets for under ฿150.

What’s the best area for restaurants in Pattaya?

Second Road and Soi Buakhao for the widest honest-price choice in town; Na Jomtien for seafood; Pratumnak for meals with a view. Beach Road itself is mostly overpriced — walk one block inland.

Is the seafood in Pattaya fresh?

Yes, at the right places. The Na Jomtien seafood houses and Nang Nual work from daily local catches with visible displays and huge turnover. Avoid pre-cooked seafood buffets — that’s where quality drops.

What’s the best restaurant in Pattaya for a special occasion?

Casa Pascal for European fine dining, The Sky Gallery for a sunset table over the bay, Nang Nual for a spectacular seafood feast. All three take bookings and are used to celebrations.

Are restaurants in Pattaya child-friendly?

Very. Most have high chairs, simple Western options, and patient staff. The Market at Central Festival, Cabbages & Condoms, and Nara Thai Cuisine are all excellent with kids.

Can I find food late at night in Pattaya?

Easily — Leng Kee on Central Pattaya Road runs nearly around the clock, Soi Buakhao has late noodle spots, and the Walking Street area serves until 2am or later. See our [Pattaya nightlife guide](https://pattayapointer.com/guides/pattaya-nightlife-guide/).

## Final Thoughts: Pattaya’s Food Scene in 2026

Pattaya doesn’t get enough credit as a food destination. The seafood alone — fresh, abundant, cheap by global standards — is worth the trip. Add real depth of Thai cooking once you leave Beach Road, an international scene built for one of Asia’s most mixed visitor bases, and night markets that turn dinner into the evening’s entertainment, and Pattaya is quietly one of the best-value food cities in Asia.

Skip the illuminated-sign buffets on Beach Road. Head to the Na Jomtien sand for seafood, to Soi Buakhao for honest Thai, to Thepprasit when you want to eat six things for ฿300. That’s where Pattaya’s food scene actually lives.
