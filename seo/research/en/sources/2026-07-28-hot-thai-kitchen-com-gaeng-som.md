# Rustic Thai Sour Curry with Shrimp (Gaeng Som)

Gaeng som แกงส้ม is a spicy and sour curry that is a staple in Thai dining culture. It is easy to make and one of my mom's favourites, so we had it often growing up. It's also one of the healthiest Thai dishes! It is rare to see Thai restaurants overseas offer gaeng som, likely because it is unlike any other Thai curries you know, but if you're up for trying something new and delicious, you're at the right place!

## What is a sour curry anyway?

Gaeng som, literally means "orange curry" or "sour curry." It is a water-based curry, meaning it contains no coconut milk like other popular Thai curries most people know. This makes it very light, brothy, and some people think of it more of a soup, though to Thais, it is considered a gaeng or a curry. It's popular all over the country, and you can find it both in home and restaurant kitchens.

The sour flavour comes mainly from tamarind. It uses a very simple curry paste made mainly of dried chilies, garlic, and shallots, and this simplicity makes it a very popular curry to make at home. In the south of Thailand, turmeric is often into gaeng som which makes it yellow, so people call it gaeng leuang which means "yellow curry."

Gaeng som is usually seafood-based, which the most common proteins used being fish and shrimp. One unique feature of gaeng som is that the broth is thickened with pounded white fish. It typically contains a lot of vegetables. You can use anything from cabbage, carrots, napa cabbage, long beans, cauliflower, daikon and green papaya, making it a great veg-drawer-cleanout recipe!

## Ingredients and Important Notes

Here are all the ingredients you need with notes and frequently asked questions about them. For amounts, see the full recipe card below.

#### Gaeng Som Curry Paste Ingredients

Dried chilies. You can make this curry as spicy or as mild as you want by choosing spicy or mild chilies. I use a combination of mild guajillo or puya peppers mixed with a few arbol chilies to add some heat. When in doubt, go with mild chilies as you can always add spicy chilies later.

Shallots, roughly chopped

Fingerroot aka grachai or krachai (pic below). This is a rhizome with a cooling and medicinal aroma (in a good way) that is classic to the flavour of gaeng som. You can buy it fresh or brined, but you will need to look at stores that carry a lot of Southeast Asian products. If you can't find it, it is okay to omit, but it is an important part of the paste so I would do your best to find it!

Fermented shrimp paste ( gapi ). A funky, purplish grey paste made from fermented krills or small shrimp. It adds a lot of great umami to all Thai curries.

#### Curry Ingredients

Chicken, pork or fish stock, unsalted. For best flavour, try making your own [homemade Thai chicken stock](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/chicken-stock/) !

White fish filet, any white fish such as cod or tilapia is fine. The fish will be mashed up to thicken the broth, so don't need to get anything expensive!

Fish sauce.

Tamarind paste (pic below).This is the main seasoning that makes the curry sour so it cannot be omitted. You can buy Thai tamarind paste ready-to-use in tubs, often labelled "tamarind concentrate," or you can make it using tamarind pulp following my [DIY tamarind paste tutorial.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/making-tamarind-paste/)

Palm sugar, chopped. Or sub brown sugar. [Read more about palm sugar.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/guide-to-palm-sugar/)

Vegetables of your choice: I am using napa cabbage, long beans, carrots, and green papaya. But you can use other vegetables you wish, other common options include cauliflower, green beans, cabbage, and daikon.

Shrimp, peeled and deveined

Lime juice (optional). You may or may not need it. I like to add a little lime and adjust acidity at the end, but you can also just add more tamarind.
