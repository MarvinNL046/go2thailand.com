# Authentic Pad Thai Recipe - The Real Deal!

Take it from a Thai person, it's hard to find a really good, authentic pad thai outside of Thailand. I've searched high and low and concluded that the best option is to master cooking it at home. This recipe is the real-deal, traditional pad thai with no compromises.

This won't be like the syrupy, wet takeout with clumpy noodles. You'll get properly wok-fried rice noodles lightened with beansprouts, and a complex-yet-balanced flavour of sweet, salty and sour. There's a good reason this recipe has over 2 million views!

## What does REAL traditional pad thai look like?

The typical takeout pad thai in N. America looks nothing like pad thai in Thailand. A good pad thai should not be super sweet, nor should it be a big clump of noodles with bits of green onions and a sprinkle of bean sprouts.

Here are all the characteristics of a good pad thai you should be aiming for:

The flavour should be a balance of sweet, salty and sour in equal measure. Most importantly it should not be primarily sweet. Being overly sweet is the single biggest pitfall of N. American pad thai.

It should not be saucy. When properly cooked, all the sauce should be absorbed into the noodles. This also allows ingredients to toast and deepen the flavour.

The noodles should be lightened with a LOT of bean sprouts. Thai people don't like things to be heavy, so the noodles should be laced with plenty of bean sprouts, AND there should also be extra served on the side for extra freshness.

It should have lots of "bits" which gives flavour and textural complexity : Aside from beansprouts it should also have shallots, garlic, tofu, dried shrimp, peanuts, and garlic chives. If it's just a plate of 90% noodles, it is not good pad thai!
## Ingredients and Notes

Here's everything you'll need, with more details on a few less common ingredients and how to substitute them. For the amounts, see the recipe card below.
#### FOR PAD THAI SAUCE:

Tamarind paste - Tamarind is the sour component of the sauce. Store bought is fine, but homemade does have better flavour. Here's [how to make tamarind paste from pulp.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/making-tamarind-paste/) If buying ready-to-use tamarind paste, the tub is sometimes labelled "tamarind concentrate" (pic below). Make sure you buy a product of Thailand, not India, and it should be a brown, pourable liquid.

*IMPORTANT NOTE ON TAMARIND : The sourness of tamarind varies greatly between brands, so after feedback from a few people that their pad thai is too sour, I have changed the amount of tamarind to 3-4 Tbsp. Taste your tamarind, and if it's so sour it makes you pucker, use only 3 Tbsp. If it doesn't make you pucker, 4 tablespoon is fine. If you're not sure, start with 3 and you can always add more lime at the end.

Palm sugar, chopped - Choose high quality palm sugar when possible, for more info check out my [ultimate guide to palm sugar.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/guide-to-palm-sugar/) If not available, substitute equal weight of light brown sugar.

Fish sauce - this is the salty and umami component. Good fish sauce is perhaps the most important thing for flavour. Squid or Megachef are both good Thai brands. See also: [How to choose good quality fish sauce.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)

PS. You can make a big batch of the sauce and keep it in the fridge for weeknight pad thai. Check out this [large-batch recipe for pad thai sauce](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/5-min-pad-thai/).
#### FOR THE PAD THAI:

Rice noodles, soaked in room temp water for 1 hour. This recipe is tested using Erawan Brand size medium (pic below) which is the traditional size used in Thailand. If using a different brand or size, you might need to adjust the amount of water and soaking time.

Shrimp, or another protein of your choice. In Thailand, pad thai ONLY comes with shrimp. But if you want to use another meat, no problem. If you prefer chicken, pork, or beef, cut into thin slices and marinate it with a bit of soy sauce.

Shallots, roughly chopped

Garlic, chopped

Dried shrimp. If possible, do not get the tiny, papery-thin dried shrimp with the little eyes still on them. Get larger ones which will offer more texture and are less salty. Give them a rinse before using and roughly chop them. Can be omitted if allergic.

Pressed tofu (pic below), cut into small pieces. Sometimes labeled as bean curd, this is the firmest of all tofu. Extra firm tofu can be substituted, but if you feel like they might break in the wok, pan-frying them first until golden will firm up the exterior.

Sweet preserved radish ( chai po waan, pic below). Can be omitted if not available. These are sweet-salty chewy bits of preserved daikon, but make sure you get one that is labelled SWEET, otherwise it is too salty. You'll likely need to go to a Thai grocery store to find these, and it may come whole or pre-chopped. Japanese yellow daikon pickle, called takuan, makes for a decent substitute.

Chili flakes - Optional but recommended. Pad thai is really much better when it's a little bit spicy!

Roasted peanuts, unsalted, roughly chopped. I much prefer buying raw peanuts and roasting them whole, as the flavour is much fresher.

Bean sprouts - Go for mung bean sprouts, not soybean sprouts.

Garlic chives - If you can't find these at your regular Asian store, try looking at Japanese or Korean markets also. You can substitute green onions but the two really do taste different. If using green onions, finely chop them as they have a much stronger taste than garlic chives.

Lime wedges for serving. A squeeze of lime right before eating adds a lovely fresh tartness that I think is so important!

## Step-By-Step

Here's a bird's eye view of the process, but if this is your first time I HIGHLY recommend watching the full video tutorial so you have a good idea of what needs to happen since it moves quickly once you start wokking!
#### For the Pad Thai Sauce

Chop the palm sugar finely.

Add the sugar to a small pot and let it melt over medium heat.

Let the sugar caramelize into a dark brown colour then immediately add water to stop the caramelization (it will bubble aggressively)

Add fish sauce and tamarind paste, then turn off the heat. At this point the sugar will not be dissolved, but let it sit while you prep other things, and by the time you need it the sugar should be dissolved completely.
#### For the Pad Thai

Soak the noodles in room temp water for at least 1 hour until completely pliable. Then drain and cut noodles in half. You can soak the noodles a few days ahead of time, then drain and keep in the fridge until ready to use.

Meanwhile prep all your ingredients according to instructions. For the dried shrimp, rinse them in water and roughly chop.

Heat a wok over high heat, add oil and sear the shrimp, or whatever protein you're using, until browned on one side, then flip and cook the other side. Once fully cooked remove from the pan.

In the same wok, add a little more oil if needed, add shallots, garlic, tofu, dried shrimp, preserved radish, and chili flakes and saute until the garlic starts to turn colour.

Add the soaked noodles and the sauce.

Toss until all the sauce has been absorbed into the noodles. You can turn off the wok and taste a noodle to see if it's done. If it is too chewy, add a splash of water and keep it cooking for a minutes or so until the additional water has been absorbed.

Turn the heat up to high, then push the noodles to one side and add the eggs to the space you've made, use your spatula to break the yolks.

Put the noodles on top of the eggs and let it cook for 20-30 seconds without stirring until the egg is almost cooked through.

Flip the noodles and then stir to break up the eggs.

Add the protein back in along with the peanuts, bean sprouts and garlic chives and turn off the heat.

Toss just until the beansprouts are incorporated into the noodles.

Plate, putting the shrimp on top and garnish with more peanuts, if desired. If you want, you can add a squeeze of fresh lime over it before eating!
### Pro Tip #1: Emergency Noodle Soaking

If you're about to cook and realized you forgot to soak the noodles, don't panic! They can be soaked in hot off-the-boil water for exactly 3 minutes. You MUST set a timer and place them in cold water immediately after. If you over soak them they will quickly become too soft, so I generally try to avoid this.

## Common Issues and FAQ's

Over the years I've received a lot of questions related to making pad thai, here are all the questions that have come up frequently. Hope it helps!

You used the wrong tamarind. You probably used "tamarind concentrate" from India, which is entirely different from the one we use in Thailand and is much more concentrated.

A few possibilities: first, do not boil the noodles before using. Soak them as per the recipe, and add them to the pan raw. Second, do not crowd the pan. If you crowd the pan you'll trap too much steam. I recommend making no more than 2 portions in one wok at a time.

Rest assured, your pad thai is NOT supposed to be orange! Many restaurants will add ketchup and paprika to boost the colour in order to make it look more appealing, but this is not traditional.

However, we DO make a type of pad thai that is a little orange, and that colour comes from shrimp tomalley, such as in this [glass noodle pad thai recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/glass-noodle-stir-fry/). Or it can come from chilies, such as in this [Easier Pad Thai recipe.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/easier-pad-thai/)

It's not the best dish to make for a large group because the only way to do it is to cook multiple batches. Crowding the pan will risk ending up with mushy noodles, not to mention the noodles don't sit well and are best when eaten fresh off the wok. If they sit for too long the noodles will start to clump up together.

Rice noodles do not keep well, so you should not make more than you plan to eat. One day in the fridge isn't too bad; it can be microwaved or re-fried in a pan and will taste "fine." But after multiple days in the fridge and the noodles can turn to mush when reheated. If you want to be able to reheat, make my [glass noodle pad thai recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/glass-noodle-stir-fry/) as it reheats much better than rice noodles.

Don't modify this recipe! Use my awesome [vegan pad thai recipe instead](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/vegan-pad-thai/) !
