# The Best Time to Visit Thailand for Less Rain and Fewer Crowds

Where to go, how long to stay, and how to avoid monsoon season.

The best time to visit Thailand depends on where you want to go in the country. The famous Land of Smiles has such a diverse array of landscapes—from the mountainous north where the cities of [Chiang Mai](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/chiang-mai-thailand-achariyar-rojanapirom-locals-guide) and [Chiang Rai](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-hotels-in-thailand) await, to the tropical islands like [Krabi](https://www.cntraveler.com/hotels/thailand/krabi/phulay-bay-a-ritz-carlton-reserve) and [Koh Samui](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/koh-samui-thailand-white-lotus) in the south, with energetic and sprawling Bangkok in the middle of it all—that you could go at any point in the year and find a local that's a fit for your travel needs and interests. To better plan the Thai trip that's right for you, we spoke to two of Condé Nast Traveler 's Top Travel Specialists who are experts in designing itineraries in Thailand: [Matthew McLean](https://www.cntraveler.com/contributor/matthew-mclean) of [Audley Travel](https://www.audleytravel.com/) and [Duncan Greenfield-Turk](https://www.cntraveler.com/contributor/duncan-greenfield-turk) of [Global Travel Moments](https://www.globaltravelmoments.com/).

No matter how you organize your first (or tenth) journey through Thailand, you're sure to encounter a land of stunning scenes in both the cities and countrysides, as well as warm hospitality. “The country is so much more than just busy streets and beaches,” says Greenfield-Turk about Thailand. “The joy is in the land's contrasts: a temple visit at dawn, a Michelin-starred meal at dusk, with a quiet kindness that carries through it all.” Here are our tips for the best time to visit Thailand, as well as where to go and how long to stay.

## When is the best time to visit Thailand?

One of the best times to visit Thailand is during the dry seasons, which generally runs from [November](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-places-to-travel-november) to [April](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-places-to-travel-in-april), says McLean of Audley Travel. That's usually when there's less rain and lower humidity, but given the tropical climate, you should always pack a [travel umbrella](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/best-travel-umbrellas) when visiting Thailand. “The summer months are referred to as the ‘green season’ and can see short and sharp downpour showers,” he says, but the rain doesn't necessarily have to derail your plans. (It's always a good excuse to post up in a lobby bar at one of the [best hotels in Bangkok](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-hotels-in-bangkok).) Besides, if you head to the islands, you can chase the good weather: McLean suggests visiting the island of [Koh Samui](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-hotels-in-koh-samui) in the Gulf of Thailand, which gets very little rain even in the summer.

If you want to avoid the crowds, Greenfield-Turk says the best time to go to Thailand is between late [October](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/the-best-places-to-travel-in-october) and early [December](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-places-to-travel-december) (to avoid the holiday rush), and again from [May](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-places-to-travel-may) to early [June](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-places-to-travel-june). “That’s when you’ll find lush landscapes, a gentler pace, and that sense of stillness that lets you feel Thailand rather than just see it,” he says. “One of my clients recently travelled during May and spent a morning alone on a long-tail boat through Phang Nga Bay. The stillness, they said, was the best moment of their entire trip.”
## What is the cheapest time to visit Thailand?

Unsurprisingly, the cheapest time to visit Thailand coincides with the rainy season. If you’re willing to deal with unpredictable weather, then you could bag a few hotel deals visiting Thailand during this time. “May and September are typically the lowest priced months to visit Thailand,” McLean says. “It can be hot in May, but it's not fully in the rainy season. September is generally the rainiest month to visit.”

On top of that, an affordable trip during the shoulder seasons doesn't mean compromising on luxury and comfort. “Traveling just before or after peak season often means quieter resorts, more attentive service, and experiences that feel entirely your own. This is luxury at its most considered,” says Greenfield-Turk. “A couple I designed a journey for last June had entire restaurants to themselves and ended up being personally shown around a local night market by the chef. Moments like that simply don’t happen in high season.”
## When is peak season in Thailand?

Peak season in Thailand is traditionally November through February, when the weather is at its most pleasant, says Greenfield-Turk. That said, that's also peak celebration season in the country, McLean says, which celebrates festivals like Loi Krathong (Lantern Festival), the Lunar New Year, or Songkran. You can still have a good time when you visit during these occasions, but it's good to calibrate your expectations—you will see a massive influx of domestic and international travelers wherever you go.
## For a first visit to Thailand, where should I go?

When flying into Thailand from abroad, you're most likely to fly through Bangkok, the country's capital. “It’s the heart of modern Thailand, where street food culture meets design-forward hotels and rooftop bars with soul,” says Greenfield-Turk. After a few days of getting acclimated to the time zone in Bangkok, McLean suggests adding a trip to Chiang Mai in the north—a culture and craft hub of Thailand—then going on to a beach location. “I find that this is the perfect mix of a bit of history and culture, stunning natural beauty, wildlife and elephants, amazing cuisine, and some relaxation,” he says.
## How long should I stay in Thailand?

Any amount of time you spend in Thailand is time well spent, certainly. A week gives you a taste; two allows you to truly exhale and explore; and three gives you one of the most immersive experiences possible, says Greenfield-Turk. You can easily get all types of experiences—from city break to nature adventures and beachside relaxation—thanks to the country's excellent regional connectivity, with point-to-point flights that take you where you need to go seamlessly.

Generally speaking, McLean says, “the essential amount of time would be 9 to 12 days in country in order to cover at least 3 different locations. For those with extended time to travel, I would recommend 14 to 18 days, which would allow you to explore at least 4 different destinations.”
### East coast islands

The best [Thai islands](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/the-best-islands-in-thailand-for-every-traveler) in the Gulf of Thailand— [Koh Samui](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/my-favorite-airbnb-koh-samui-island), Koh Phangan, Koh Tao—tend to get less rain than the rest of the country (even in the rainy season) and are at their best from the end of January until mid-March. January is the freshest month—great for after the [Christmas](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/best-places-to-go-at-christmas) and [New Year's Eve](https://www.cntraveler.com/gallery/best-places-new-year-celebrations-around-the-world) party festivities. February is the driest month, one of the sunniest, when the heat is still bearable; March is sunny and dry, too. The European and American summer break crowds hit the Gulf islands (Koh Samui, Koh Pha-Ngan and Koh Tao) during July and August, so heading here in September usually hits the sweet spot for both crowds and weather. The average temperature never drops below 77ºF in Koh Samui and most of the other popular islands; so even if there is the occasional rain shower, it will always be pretty balmy.
### West coast islands

Over on Thailand's west coast and the Andaman Sea—where you'll find the Thai islands of [Phuket](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/the-rebirth-of-phuket), Krabi, Koh Lanta, Koh Phi Phi—the optimum time for a beach holiday is slightly earlier, in December and January, when fresher weather, maximum sunshine, and minimum rainfall combine to create the best climate for a beach vacation.
## When is the best time to visit Bangkok?

[Bangkok](https://www.cntraveler.com/destinations/bangkok) is stiflingly humid practically all the time (discomfort levels range from 'high' to 'extreme' year-round), but the best time to visit Thailand's capital is in December and January when it is dry and a relatively bearable 87 to 89ºF average during the day, and 68ºF at night (the coolest Bangkok ever gets). But also consider July and August, when there are great hotel deals to be had—and the rain only lasts for a short while in the afternoon. Avoid the months of April and September. April is unbearably hot and humid; September is hot and torrentially wet.
## When is the best time to visit Chiang Mai and the north?

In Thailand's cooler north, [Chiang Mai](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/chiang-mai-thailand-achariyar-rojanapirom-locals-guide) and the lovely little town of Pai have much more pleasant climates than Bangkok and the south. Go from December to February for lovely sunny days (around 84ºF) and cooler evenings (the temperature dips to 55ºF—and even lower in the highlands, so dig out some warm clothes if you're trekking).
