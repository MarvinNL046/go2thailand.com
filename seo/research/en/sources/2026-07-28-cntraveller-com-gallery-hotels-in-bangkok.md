# The best hotels in Bangkok
### Dusit Thani Bangkok

[Dusit Thani Bangkok](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/dusit-thani-review-bangkok)

Don’t be fooled by Dusit Thani’s gleaming new facade, because this grande dame hotel has been a Bangkok landmark since the 1970s. Once the city’s premier luxury lodging, it faded over time as flashier hotels emerged. Now, the family-owned Dusit Thani group has rebuilt it from scratch, cutting the room count by half but doubling its size. Drawn up by Hong Kong-born designer Andre Fu, the interiors balance sleek modernity (lots of beige, marble, and blonde woods) with nods to the building's heritage: the Benjarong-painted pillars from the original Thai restaurant now grace the lobby, while the original multi-tiered waterfall still fills the courtyard with a babbling buzz.

Even the entry-level rooms feel expansive, and all offer uninterrupted views over Lumphini Park’s lush canopy (best enjoyed with a morning coffee from the gold-trimmed bay windows). Some of the hotel's most beloved restaurants – Thai diner Benjarong, and the Vietnamese Thien Duong – have been moved to a landscaped garden nearby, while new venues, such as Italian chef Umberto Bombana’s first Asian outpost, Cannubi, and the Thai-Chinese Pavilion restaurant have already proved instant classics with the local high-society crowd. Even if you're not sleeping over, stop by for drinks at the split-level rooftop bar, which is designed as a homage to Studio 54 muse Elsa Peretti, and comes with a roving martini trolley and eye-popping city views.

Price: Rooms from around £340 per night.

### Mandarin Oriental, Bangkok

[Mandarin Oriental, Bangkok](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/mandarin-oriental-bangkok)

Some hotels lose their luster after a while. Others only get better with age. The Mandarin Oriental Bangkok, sitting chocolate-box pretty on the eastern bank of the Chao Phraya River, firmly belongs in the latter category. This Southeast Asian hidey-hole was already around when Joseph Conrad was still gallivanting the globe; Somerset Maugham famously recovered here from a gnarly malaria spell. But even though a handful of suites still bear their names, this grande dame doesn't feel mired in the past. Frequent spruce-ups, the last one in 2019 to the tune of $90 million, have kept her looking tip-top, swapping outdated browns and beiges for jewel-toned textiles and fresh floral patterns.

Food and drinks-wise, it has its finger on the pulse of Bangkok's hi-so (high society) tastes, with an ever-evolving lineup of a dozen-or-so restaurants. Afternoon tea at the rattan-chaired Authors Lounge remains lovely as ever (and is a must, even if you're not sleeping over), while the teakwood-trimmed Oriental Spa, with its CBD oil-infused massages, ice baths, and rubdowns with herbs from northeast Thailand's Isan region still sits in a league of its own. The beloved 'Em O' will ring in her 150th year of service soon, but gives even the big-buck newcomers a run for their money. They just don't make 'em like this anymore.

Price: Rooms from around £470 per night.

### Capella Bangkok

[Capella Bangkok](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/capella-bangkok)

With numerous new arrivals adding to a galaxy worth of five-star stays, the Thai capital has no shortage of swanky beds to book. But it's only once in a blue moon that a hotel with such star power as the Capella Bangkok comes around. Opened just a few years ago on a garden-clad estate along the Chao Phraya River, this low-rise pile of blonde woods and creamy marble entered the scene with perks the city hadn't yet seen before: suites with travertine Jacuzzi tubs bubbling on their balcony, and villas with private pools and tropical gardens pitched up right on the waterfront. The look of it all – from the sprawling wellness centre to the airy Living Room lounge – is decadent in its simplicity, leaning on lots of straight lines in taupe, light grey, and cream to not distract from those sweeping river views visible from every room and restaurant.

On the food front, there's honest Thai home-cooking at Phra Nakhon and a Mediterranean-tinged fine diner by Argentine hot-shot chef Mauro Colagreco, while guests flock to the boudoir-like Stella Bar after dark to sip on drinks inspired by some of Bangkok's most storied districts (try Chinatown's Long Lai, with vodka, pandan, and coconut vinegar). And for those moments in between, a small army of Culturists (that's Capellian for butler) is at the ready to arrange intimate barbecue parties on your verandah, or chef-guided tours around the Charoenkrung district's best street eats. In a city where luxury often screams, Capella proves that it's OK to whisper.

Price: Rooms from around £650 per night.

### Aman Nai Lert Bangkok

[Aman Nai Lert Bangkok](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/aman-nai-lert-bangkok)

In the centre of Bangkok in April 2025, Aman debuted its second property in the country where its first resort opened in 1988. Imagine this new hotel as the daughter of Phuket’s [Amanpuri](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/amanpuri-thailand) — she grew up and ran off to the big city to socialise, but still retained her rich cultural heritage, warm-heartedness, and chic sensibility. Intentionally, the locally sourced materials and completely custom furniture, art and objects relate back to Nai Lert's Heritage House, revealing details and insights in an exceedingly elegant way. Meaningful connections are around every turn, from the 3,000-plus painted wood spinning tops backdropping the concierge and front desks to the texturised expanse of hardwood screens carved in Chiang Mai that mesmerised me with their gleaming gold leaf appliqué rivers.

In a foodie mecca, Aman Nai Lert Bangkok’s culinary contributions hold their own; Two of them, intimate Japanese dinner theatre destinations with just two seatings per night, are worth booking a room for, as it’s the only way would-be diners can indulge. Kathryn Romeyn

Price: Rooms from around £1,060 per night.

### The Siam, Bangkok

[The Siam, Bangkok](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/the-siam-bangkok)

Compared with many of its rivals, this Bangkok stalwart doesn’t have a location that’s wildly convenient: the postcard sights need a boat transfer, and most top tables are more than a 30-minute taxi ride away. But for me, and the legions of other loyal fans of this monochrome masterpiece by hotel guru [Bill Bensley](https://www.cntraveller.com/video/watch/9-sustainable-lessons-from-architect-and-hotelier-bill-bensley-conde-nast-traveller), that’s the point. Pitched up on a lush riverside plot in the hushed Dusit district, The Siam feels like a portal to a bygone Bangkok. Even though it opened a decade ago, it’s easy to imagine it as a plush playground for the city’s erstwhile big guns, the mid-century movers and shakers whose heirlooms – vintage Pan Am posters, tattered travel trunks and chipped ceramics – adorn the marble-floored hallways.

I’d be hard-pressed to point friends to a lovelier lunch spot than the hotel’s Thai restaurant, set between the timeworn pillars of three ancient teakwood houses, where butlers in dressy black sarongs deliver the kind of classic Thai hospitality – lilting “sawadee ka” greetings and tables set with fresh orchids – that’s becoming harder to find. None of which is to say that the hotel is stuffy – there’s a tattoo parlour in the spa. An ongoing refurb is keeping suites and private-pooled villas looking fresh. And with the opening of a Jim Thompson homeware boutique in the wooden house the illustrious silk magnate helped procure for its former owners, things have come full circle. The Thai capital’s five-star hotel scene gets plusher every year, but this timeless beauty remains in a league of its own.

Price: Rooms from around £540 per night.

Address: The Siam 3, 2 Khao, Vachirapayabal, Dusit District, Bangkok 10300, Thailand

### The Standard, Bangkok Mahanakhon

[The Standard, Bangkok Mahanakhon](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/the-standard-bangkok-mahanakhon)

There were barely any surprises left when I checked in just days after the hotel’s official opening at the end of July 2022 – every one of Bangkok’s high-heeled fashionistas and socialites had already TikTok-ed every art-studded nook, DJ set, and Drag Queen Bingo party. And that’s unlikely to change anytime soon because this high-voltage clout magnet in the city’s tallest skyscraper draws the Thai and international in-crowd like bees to a honeypot.

The Standard is a five-star with a vibe Bangkok’s hotel scene was still missing, swapping out the predictably serene, Thai-inspired interiors and chi-chi fine dining restaurants of most of its competitors for a riot of colours, clubby restaurants, and the country’s highest rooftop bar. That’s not to say it’s all-out debaucherous, though: The swirling pool, with the CBD’s skyline as its backdrop, is a lovely spot to spend a lazy afternoon, and most of the staff (a mix of Thais and peppy young ex-pats) are a chatty, smiling bunch.

Price: Rooms from around £170 per night.

### Four Seasons Hotel Bangkok at Chao Phraya River

[Four Seasons Hotel Bangkok at Chao Phraya River](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/four-seasons-hotel-bangkok-at-chao-phraya-river)

The plans for Four Seasons’ return to Bangkok (a prior location closed in 2015) predate the body blow that travel took during the pandemic, but the Jean-Michel Gathy–designed reincarnation on the banks of the Chao Phraya feels tailored to Bangkok residents anyway. For visitors who have already ticked off the city’s sightseeing hits, it offers serious staying-put appeal: a multitiered swimming pool with thick-cushioned loungers for all-day lingering; vast suites and bedrooms with deep soaking bathtubs; and floor-to-ceiling windows that frame knockout river views.

The four restaurants are set for lazy lunches, while the bossa nova–humming BKK Social Club is helmed by Philip Bischoff, formerly of the glitzy bar Manhattan at the Regent Singapore. The contemporary art collection has an urban edge: Towering works depicting the curves of the Chao Phraya or the folds of a monk’s robe hang from the lobby’s marble walls, while a rotating exhibition puts the spotlight on local talent. Over the years, indie galleries and artsy boutiques have turned this corner of Bangkok into the city’s de facto design district, a transformation that is complete now that the Four Seasons is back.

Price: Rooms from around £420 per night.

### The Ritz-Carlton, Bangkok

[The Ritz-Carlton, Bangkok](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/bangkok/the-ritz-carlton-bangkok)

The Ritz-Carlton's entry into Bangkok brought with it a serene stay in an evolving corner of the city with easy access to major sites and the brand's signature impeccable service. It sits on the One Bangkok campus, a new $3 billion development with hotels, office buildings, condos, a massive shopping mall, an entertainment complex, and plenty of public art and outdoor space. Guest rooms begin on the eighth floor and go up to 24, half of which have balconies or outdoor space. Room types start at a deluxe king or deluxe double, many with standalone tubs and walk-in rain showers, and expand up to The Ritz-Carlton Suite, a two-bedroom with a massive walk-in closet, dining room and bar, Lumpini Park views, and lounge access.

Be sure to book a meal at Duet by David Toutain (whose eponymous Paris restaurant has two Michelin stars); his team serves modern French cuisine in the form of a seasonal tasting menu heavy on seafood, fresh vegetables, and ornate presentation. Madison Flager

## Waldorf Astoria Bangkok

The Waldorf Astoria Bangkok occupies the sculptural curves of the Magnolias Ratchadamri Boulevard tower, smack in the heart of Bangkok's mall-lined CBD. Its interiors, designed by André Fu, echo the brand's legendary Art Deco style with marble, brass, and subtle Thai motifs. The lobby's soaring sculpted screens and metallic sheen nod to the glam of Waldorf Astoria's New York City flagship and set the tone for the 171 rooms and suites upstairs, which all feature floor-to-ceiling windows, travertine-covered bathrooms, and gorgeous accents in colourful Thai silks.

On the 16th floor, a temperature-controlled infinity pool looks out over the green stretch of the Royal Bangkok Sports Club, while a few floors up, the Bull & Bear grill room and the paint-splattered The Loft cocktail bar pair their steaks and negronis with one of the city's best sky bar views. After a day around Bangkok's hubbub, this is precisely the kind of hush-hush, hydrangea-scented haven you'll want to return to.

Price: Rooms from around £400 per night.

Address : 151 Ratchadamri Rd, Lumphini, Pathum Wan, Bangkok 10330, Thailand

## Park Hyatt Bangkok

Soaring over central Bangkok's ritzy Phloen Chit district like a giant metallic swirl, the Park Hyatt Bangkok rang in an exciting new era for Bangkok's high-end hotel scene when it opened atop the landmark Central Embassy mall in 2017. It came with a serious pedigree: the rooms and suites, clad in creamy travertine, pale wood veneer and Thai-inspired accents in orange and black, were drawn up by the design big-shots of Yabu Pushelberg, while New York-based outfit AvroKO transformed the upper floors into a steampunk bar and grill with a hidden whiskey room stocking more than 150 rare labels.

After breakfast, the triple-height Embassy Room on the lobby floor turns into La Marina, a sprawling Italian seafood spot serving up southern staples such as seabream in cherry tomato stew and crudo from Sicilian red tuna.

Price: Rooms from around £245 per night.

Address: Central Embassy, 88 Witthayu Rd, Lumphini, Pathum Wan, Bangkok 10330, Thailand

## The Peninsula Bangkok

With its towering facade akin to a mothership in a 1970s Space Age movie, the Peninsula is a defining feature of the Chao Phraya River banks. And even though the hotel has been welcoming loyal guests since the late 1990s, it managed to stay relevant throughout the following decades. Its rooms, all done up in teak, cream and Thai silk, deliver some of the best views in all of Bangkok (thanks to the building's perch on the western bank of the river, they face the skyscraper-studded downtown), and amenity-wise, the 36th- and 37th-floor suites with hot tubs on their private terraces are hard to beat.

But it's the experiential programming that sets this hotel apart: from artist-in-residence exhibitions to plant-based Chinese tasting menus and complimentary wellness activities (including Aquafit classes, mindfulness breathing workshops and sunrise yoga at a nearby temple), the Pen has its finger on the pulse of what's trending in travel.

Price: Rooms from around £380 per night.

Address: 333 Charoen Nakhon Rd, Khlong Ton Sai, Khlong San, Bangkok 10600, Thailand
## The Sukhothai

Plenty have tried, but few hotels in Thailand have managed to hit that sweet spot between classic marble-and-teak Thai and straight-lined modernism quite like The Sukhothai. This three-decades-old Bangkok stalwart, located just out of earshot from the city's cacophonous business district, has garnered a loyal following for its spacious rooms and Zen-inducing courtyards dotted with lily ponds and rain trees – all wrapped in a timelessly elegant design by the late architects Ed Tuttle and Kerry Hill.

Between terracotta stupas and timeworn Buddhist wood carvings, the hotel's Celadon restaurant serves up excellent Thai cuisine (go for the 8-course tasting menu), while chi chi La Scala ranks high as one of Bangkok's best Italian restaurants. The hotel's prime position along Sathorn Road makes exploring both the Old Town district and the city's downtown a breeze, but the enormous green-tiled swimming pool makes staying in just as appealing.

Price: Rooms from around £260 per night.

Address: 13/3 S Sathon Rd, Khwaeng Thung Maha Mek, Sathon, Bangkok 10120, Thailand
## 137 Pillars Suites & Residences Bangkok

Suite guests also have access to the Baan Borneo Club on the 26th floor for intimate breakfasts and complimentary sundowners, plus exclusive use of one of Bangkok's most spectacular infinity rooftop pools on the 38th floor.

## The Mustang Blu

Pitched around the corner from Chinatown's buzz-hub Soi Nana, [Bangkok's coolest neighbourhood](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/soi-nana-bangkok-neighbourhood-guide), The Mustang Blu is one of the city's quirkiest boutique hotels. Set in an early 20th-century Chinese medicine dispensary-turned-bank building-turned naughty massage joint, this 10-room bolthole unfurls like a cabinet of curiosities and brims with timeworn European antiques and exotic taxidermy (including a glass-encased horse and ostrich skeleton in the lobby).

Fittingly for a building of this age, none of the room's floor plans are the same. Some have large marble-clad bathrooms, others come with roll top tubs and snug balconies. The only con: the rooms aren't exactly sound proof and tuk-tuks start clattering through the street from the early morning – pack earplugs if you're a light sleeper.

Price: Rooms from around £155 per night.

Address: 721 Maitri Chit Rd, Khwaeng Pom Prap, Khet Pom Prap Sattru Phai, Bangkok 10100, Thailand
## CHANN

You’ll have to follow through a dizzying maze of alleyways to reach CHANN, a sleek riverside retreat in the historic Bangkok Noi community, but your trek will be rewarded with a hidden-away escape at the intersection of the mighty Chao Phraya and Bangkok Noi canal. With 18 rooms and four suites spread over several teakwood villas, this is one of the biggest hotels on this list, yet it has a delightfully intimate vibe.

Rooms are kept intentionally simple with lots of warm wood, natural textiles and a touch of mid-century-modern design. On-site restaurant IMM sticks to comforting classics such as roasted crispy pork and massaman curry, and its waterside terrace is a lovely dinner spot.

Price: Rooms from around £55 per night.

Address : CHANN, 30/1 Wat Dusitaram Somdej Phra Pin Klao Road, Bangkok Noi, Bangkok
## Bangkok Publishing Residence

Housed in an old printing factory along lively Lan Luang Road in the Old Town, this intimate but super-smart place to stay rivals the service of many of the city’s five-stars. Its picturesque lobby doubles as a museum (a publisher and his family once lived here), showcasing volumes of old magazines, antique printers and keepsakes. Guests are encouraged to browse around, and the friendly staff are more than happy to share stories about the place.

## Siri Sala

If you have a big budget and it's privacy you're after, Siri Sala is just the ticket. This White Lotus -famous villa, located along the quaint Bangkok Noi canal across the river from the Grand Palace, only opens to one party (of up to 12 guests) at a time. That means you and your crew will have the five bedrooms, lounger-lined lap pool, the indoor-outdoor spa, and game room all to yourselves, and a private chef who whips up Thai and western comfort food at the drop of a hat. T

he villa is a passion project of its Thai-Filipino owners, who took five years to thoughtfully rebuild and renovate the trio of derelict teakwood homes they bought on this riverside lot. While two of them were damaged beyond repair, their floorboards and wall fixtures now live on as upcycled furniture – the third one has been meticulously restored as a jazzy, exclusive-use cocktail bar at the back of the property. With its little-visited temples and ramshackle riverfront homes, this corner of Bangkok feels frozen in time – a historian-guided canal tour on board Siri Sala's teak-clad boat is an unbeatable way to dive deeper into this fascinating district.

Price: Available for rent from about £3,670 per night (sleeps up to 12 persons)

## What's the best area in Bangkok to stay in?

The simplest way to narrow down your Bangkok hotel search is to decide whether you want to stay in the Old Town or downtown. The former offers easy access to Bangkok’s postcard sights – the glittering Grand Palace, the incense-swirled temples, the slow pace of life along the Chao Phraya River – while the latter puts you within striking distance of big-ticket malls, chef-led dining, and late-night entertainment. Bangkok's efficient public transport network and affordable taxis mean you can hop between the two without much effort (though rush hours are best avoided), so it's easy to split your time between neighbourhoods.
