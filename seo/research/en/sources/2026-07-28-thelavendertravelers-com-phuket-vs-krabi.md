Jess went to Phuket at 22 and still thinks about the endless connect-4 defeats. Now as a couple in our 30s Krabi offered us the slower pace we were looking for as part of our 3.5 month backpacking trip.

## Phuket or Krabi? Ask Yourself These 5 Questions

Phuket and Krabi show two very different sides of Thailand.

If you’re unable to visit both places or don’t have time to read this whole blog post, these five questions will help you quickly figure out which destination fits your current vibe:

Do you want to dive into Thailand’s busiest beaches and nightlife or escape to scenic nature and peace?

Busy beaches and nightlife – choose Phuket

Scenic nature and peace – choose Krabi

Do you want a luxury hotel with a rooftop pool or a jungle bungalow with cliff views?

Luxury Hotel – Phuket is home to a wide-range of resorts and chain hotels

Jungle Bungalow – Krabi has lots of design forward hotels often with fewer crowds and better scenery

Would you rather kayak through mangroves or ride a banana boat?

Kayak = Krabi (adventure + nature)

Banana boat = Phuket (touristy fun)

Do you want direct flights into your destination?

Yes – Phuket offers more direct international connections that are further afield

No/Don’t Mind – Krabi only flies direct to nearby places like Singapore, Dubai or Mumbai

Are you traveling with friends to party or as a couple to relax?

Friends/Party → Go with Phuket

Couples/Chill → Go with Krabi

## Phuket in Your 20s: What It’s Like to Party in Paradise

Way back in 2016 Jess visited Phuket with her best friend at the energetic age of 22, staying near Patong Beach. It was an unforgettable holiday filled with cheap drinks and delicious street food right on our doorstep.

Most days followed the same pattern: mornings spent sunbathing on the beach, afternoons reserved for a quick shower and nap, and evenings dedicated to hitting the strip in search of local dishes and clubs we’d never experienced back home.

As we followed the sights and sounds of Patong Beach, it was hard to miss the seedier side of the destination – promoters trying to lure you into shows that we were definitely not the target audience for.

That being said, amidst the chaos, you’ll still find cool bars and quirky clubs that are a blast to experience in your 20s.

Be ready to battle it out over a game of Connect-4 for a free drink and be wowed by the dancers performing atop many of the on-street bars, all hoping to tempt young backpackers looking for a fun place to let loose.

Side note: Thailand’s government is trying to crack down on alcohol consumption that could lead to tourists receiving hefty fines. [Read this article to learn more.](https://www.independent.co.uk/travel/news-and-advice/thailand-alcohol-ban-drinking-fine-b2863488.html)

While most days were about recovering from the night before, Phuket isn’t short on things to do that don’t involve nursing a hangover under a palm tree.

Highlights include visiting Wat Chalong and the iconic Big Buddha – both well worth a few hours of your day.

And it wasn’t all partying. We also indulged in a few treats that we’d still say yes to in our 30s.

One standout was a beautiful three-course meal with a view at [Avista Hideaway Patong Resort & Spa](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/avista-hideaway-resort-amp-spa-phuket.en-gb.html?aid=311984&label=baan-banyan-tree-vRCZdbfep5p7jyDqW06vKwS260743823245%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-408365012366%3Alp9076077%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Appccp%3DUmFuZG9tSVYkc2RlIyh9YXORK0YJiVoOxcWODxYDaAA&all_sr_blocks=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775&checkin=2026-01-06&checkout=2026-01-07&dest_id=416004&dest_type=hotel&dist=0&group_adults=2&group_children=0&hapos=1&highlighted_blocks=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775&hpos=1&matching_block_id=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775&no_rooms=1&req_adults=2&req_children=0&room1=A%2CA&sb_price_type=total&sr_order=popularity&sr_pri_blocks=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775_1170000&srepoch=1764225479&srpvid=52a92ea0b9b2010c&type=total&ucfs=1&) – complete with palate cleansers (a first for an uncultured 22-year-old whose palate barely recognized a cheeseburger).

The final treat in Phuket was an afternoon of decompression at [Spa Burasari](https://phuket.burasari.com/spa/), living a life of luxury with the “Buraliscious Experience.”

Both are still on our must-do list today, so if you’re in Phuket don’t skip them.

While our time in Phuket was exactly what we needed in our early 20s, our priorities shifted with time.

When we planned a return to Thailand in our 30s, we knew we wanted something a little slower, a little quieter, and a lot more scenic. So we headed to Krabi instead.

## Krabi in Your 30s: A Slower, Scenic Escape

In contrast to Phuket’s party pace, Krabi offers a more laid-back experience with dramatic limestone cliffs, peaceful beach mornings, and adventure-filled afternoons.

[Beach towns like Ao Nang and Railay Beach](https://thelavendertravelers.com/krabi-town-ao-nang-or-railay-beach-which-is-better/) are surrounded by world-class scenery, where jungled cliffs meet emerald waters, often just steps from your hotel.

RELATED: If you want more beaches like the ones in Krabi, these are [our favorite beaches in Koh Lanta](https://thelavendertravelers.com/the-best-beaches-in-koh-lanta-swim-relax-and-explore/).

We stayed in Ao Nang, which was the perfect mix of mellow evenings and easy access to adventure.

Our days started with coffee and beach views, and ended with sunset viewpoint hikes or [Thai cooking classes](https://viator.tpx.lt/zrCYlMTg) (one of our favorite immersive travel experiences).

Ao Nang also makes a great base for island-hopping. There are plenty of tour agencies who will take you to [Hong Island, Poda Island, and Koh Phi Phi for snorkeling, swimming and exploring](https://getyourguide.tpx.lt/U9sLHhD1).

But Krabi isn’t just Ao Nang. Railay Beach, only accessible by boat, is a jungle-meets-ocean paradise, and Krabi Town offers a quieter, more local feel.

Across the region, you’ll find fewer crowds, lower noise, and more of that “we’re really away from it all” energy.

TOP TIP : If you love snorkeling, [Koh Tao is the best in Thailand](https://thelavendertravelers.com/the-best-snorkeling-in-koh-tao-tours-tips-and-more/) ! We saw the most beautiful fish and coral here!

Krabi was exactly what we needed in our 30s. It’s a place to slow down, reconnect, and still have enough adventure when you feel like it. If Phuket was our party chapter, Krabi was our reset.

### Activities in Phuket

Phuket is a bustling metropolitan region where you’ll find classic tourist attractions for everyone but may appeal more to families or younger people.

There’s the Thai-heritage themed [Andamanda Waterpark](https://www.andamandaphuket.com/) and beaches such as Kata and Karon Beach offering water sports.

TOP TIP : If you’re looking for more highly-rated activities in Phuket we always check [Seek Sophie](https://www.seeksophie.com/a/81w7).

They offer loads of hand-picked, culturally conscious tours in Phuket such as diving trips with eco-friendly operators or Muay Thai lessons with local professional fighters.
### Activities in Krabi

Krabi is perfect for adventure-seekers who prefer experiences in nature over structured attractions.

One of the highlights is rock climbing at Railay Beach, where local guides will literally rope you into stunning cliffside routes with epic ocean views.

You can also kayak through lush mangroves, hike to panoramic viewpoints like the one near Ao Nang, or cool off at local waterfalls like Huay Toh or Than Bok Khorani National Park.

Want something immersive but low-energy? [Cooking classes](https://viator.tpx.lt/zrCYlMTg) in Krabi are some of the best. Learn how to make Thai curries and gain confidence ordering dishes at local spots after.

[Many of these experiences can be booked through Seek Sophie](https://www.seeksophie.com/a/ndxx), who curate hand-picked, eco-conscious adventures across Krabi and its islands.

Given the selection of tours offers in both destinations, we would say it’s a tie in the Phuket vs Krabi debate in terms of activities available.
### Accommodation in Phuket

Phuket is a highly-developed region with accommodation to suit every budget. Choose between beachfront hostels and luxury resorts with rooftop pools.

There are significantly more high-end hotels and international chains in Phuket than in Krabi, making it ideal if you want familiar comforts or ultra-luxury.

Our favorite resort in Phuket: [Avista Hideaway Patong Resort & Spa](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/avista-hideaway-resort-amp-spa-phuket.en-gb.html?aid=311984&label=baan-banyan-tree-vRCZdbfep5p7jyDqW06vKwS260743823245%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-408365012366%3Alp9076077%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Appccp%3DUmFuZG9tSVYkc2RlIyh9YXORK0YJiVoOxcWODxYDaAA&all_sr_blocks=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775&checkin=2026-01-06&checkout=2026-01-07&dest_id=416004&dest_type=hotel&dist=0&group_adults=2&group_children=0&hapos=1&highlighted_blocks=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775&hpos=1&matching_block_id=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775&no_rooms=1&req_adults=2&req_children=0&room1=A%2CA&sb_price_type=total&sr_order=popularity&sr_pri_blocks=41600429_93750958_2_41_0_1165775_1170000&srepoch=1764225479&srpvid=52a92ea0b9b2010c&type=total&ucfs=1&) – a splurge-worthy spot with sweeping views and a full three-course dining experience that 22-year-old us was shocked to love.
### Accommodation in Krabi

Krabi has a more down-to-earth feel, but that doesn’t mean sacrificing comfort.

In Ao Nang, you’ll find design-forward boutique hotels and adult-only stays with infinity pools and cliff views. It’s perfect if you want peace and personality without sacrificing amenities.

Railay Beach is more expensive (and only accessible by boat), but it’s worth the splurge if you want to wake up steps from dramatic cliffs and turquoise waters – it legit feels like another world.

If you’re after something more local and affordable, Krabi Town offers homestays and guesthouses that put you closer to authentic Thai life.

Our favorite hostel in Krabi: [Sanim Hostel](https://booking.tpx.lt/Xk0y2rqc) – Perfectly located on Ao Nang beach right next to where the longboats take you to Railay Beach.

There’s a 7-Eleven down below where you can try the famous ham+cheese toastie backpackers rave about (spoiler: it’s delicious).
### Phuket or Krabi: Which is Easier to Get To?

Phuket is generally easier to reach, especially from further afield. Its international airport offers direct flights from Europe, the Middle East, and Australia, making it a popular first stop in Thailand.

From Phuket, it’s also easy to reach nearby islands like Phi Phi, Khao Lak, or Koh Yao Noi via ferry or speedboat.

Krabi’s airport is smaller, with limited direct international flights. Flights are mainly from nearby hubs like Singapore, Mumbai, and Dubai. But once you land, you’re well-positioned to explore.

Since it’s on the mainland, overland travel is simple, and [destinations like Koh Lanta](https://thelavendertravelers.com/visiting-koh-lanta-in-2024-old-town-amazing-beaches-more/), [Khao Sok](https://thelavendertravelers.com/khao-sok-national-park-floating-bungalows/), or the Phi Phi Islands are all accessible via van + ferry transfers.

💡 TOP TIP: [Check ferry schedules in advance](https://12go.tpx.lt/pb3ymLJl) – especially if you’re planning to connect to the islands.
### Phuket vs Krabi: Which Is Cheaper?

Phuket is generally more expensive than Krabi, especially if you’re there for nightlife. Bars, tours, and high-end dining in Phuket often come with inflated tourist prices.

It’s not impossible to travel Phuket on a budget, but it does take more effort (and willpower to skip the bar crawl).

Krabi felt more affordable overall, but not as cheap as we’d expected. While Ao Nang has a reputation for being budget-friendly, we often found ourselves paying tourist prices even at local-looking restaurants.

The bill was always a little shocking after coming from places like [Chiang Dao](https://thelavendertravelers.com/a-complete-guide-to-chiang-dao-hot-springs-caves-and-coffee/) and [Chiang Rai](https://thelavendertravelers.com/is-chiang-rai-worth-visiting/), where local prices were much more accessible.
## Which Destination Is Best for You?

If you’re still torn, here’s how we’d sum it all up, based on our own experiences and what we wish we knew before booking.
### Nightlife in Phuket vs Krabi

If you’re in town to party, Phuket is the easy winner. Patong Beach is the heart of the nightlife scene, with loud clubs, street bars, Connect-4 competitions, and neon chaos in every direction.

Krabi, on the other hand, is more low-key. Expect cocktail bars with live music, beach bars for sunset drinks, and a slower pace overall. It’s perfect if you’re not chasing shots but still want to go out.

🏆 Winner: Phuket for nightlife – Wild, walkable, and full of energy.
### Beaches: Where Will You Find the Best Sand and Scenery?

Phuket’s beaches like Kata and Karon are spacious and easy to access, especially if you’re staying nearby. But Krabi’s beaches win for drama and beauty with limestone cliffs rising straight out of the water.

Railay Beach in particular somehow still feels untouched, even when it’s crowded.

🏆 Winner: Krabi for beach scenery – The cliffs and coastlines here feel unreal.
### Is Krabi or Phuket Better for Nature and Adventure?

If you love the outdoors, Krabi is your playground. You can kayak through mangroves, hike up jungle trails to stunning viewpoints, and even rock climb in Railay with local guides.

Phuket has nature too, but it’s usually part of a structured day tour – like banana boating, ziplining, or eco-parks. Think curated adventure vs wild exploration.

🏆 Winner: Krabi for nature and adventure – Rugged, raw, and often tour-free.
### Accommodation: Where Will You Feel More at Home?

Phuket is packed with high-end resorts and chain hotels, making it a good pick if you’re after five-star luxury or familiar brands.

Krabi has more boutique charm. Especially in Ao Nang or Railay, where you can stay in cliff-view hotels, jungle bungalows, or adult-only escapes. It’s less polished, but more peaceful.

🏆 Winner: It depends – Phuket wins for luxury, Krabi for boutique charm.
### Things to Do: Which Destination Keeps You Busy?

Phuket has a bigger list of activities: waterparks, night markets, shows, and structured tours. There’s always something happening.

Krabi’s activities are more laid-back and nature-driven : kayaking, climbing, cooking classes, and beach hopping. You’ll still stay busy but it won’t feel rushed.

🏆 Winner: Krabi for things to do – More nature-driven, less curated.
### Getting There: Which Is Easier to Reach?

Phuket is easier to reach, especially if you’re flying from further afield. Its international airport has direct flights from Europe, Australia, and the Middle East.

Krabi is better connected regionally, with flights from places like Singapore and Dubai. Once you’re there, it’s easy to reach other mainland or island destinations.

🏆 Winner: Phuket for accessibility – Easier international flights with similar island links to Krabi.
## What We’d Do Differently Next Time

We feel lucky to have experienced both Phuket and Krabi at the exact right moments in our lives.

Phuket? We wouldn’t rush back but we’re curious. We’d love to be a fly on the wall along Patong Beach, just to see if it still hits the same way it did at 22.

There’s something nostalgic about those chaotic, carefree nights… but we’ve probably outgrown them.

Krabi? We’d return in a heartbeat but with a slightly bigger budget. A few nights at a cliffside resort in Railay or a design-forward boutique in Ao Nang sounds like the perfect way to recharge.

Next time, we’d stay longer, take it slower, and maybe even explore more of the surrounding islands.

Both places gave us exactly what we needed, right when we needed it. Back then, we wanted shots and beach clubs. Now? We want roti stands and cliffside sunsets.
### Is Krabi better or Phuket?

It depends on your travel style. Phuket is better for nightlife, resorts, and accessibility. Krabi is better for nature, adventure, and a relaxed pace.

Our personal favorite is Krabi as we don’t want to go out every night and when we do go out, we want a chill bar with nice views.
### Is Krabi more expensive than Phuket?

No, Phuket is typically more expensive due to its popularity and nightlife scene, though some areas in Krabi like Railay Beach can also be pricey.

To find local spots in Krabi, we usually just scour Google Maps looking at restaurant menus.
### Is it better to go to Phi Phi from Phuket or Krabi?

Both Phuket and Krabi offer ferries to Phi Phi, but Krabi is slightly closer and has more relaxed transfer routes if you’re already staying in Ao Nang or Krabi Town.
### Is Krabi or Phuket busier?

Phuket is busier, especially around Patong Beach, due to larger crowds and more infrastructure. Krabi offers a quieter, more spread-out experience.

We found the main strip in Ao Nang still gets relatively busy when dinner time comes around though.
### How many days in Krabi is enough?

We recommend at least 3–4 days in Krabi to enjoy the beaches, island hopping, and some relaxed downtime. If you love nature or want to explore Railay and nearby islands, stay longer.

We stayed for 3 nights but next time we will do at least 5 nights to properly relax and unwind.
