# Ayutthaya Day Trip or Overnight: Why We Recommend Staying

Scribbled by [Claire](https://therestlessbeans.com/about/) ⬩ 25 Apr 2026

So, you’ve decided that Ayutthaya is worth a spot on your itinerary but aren’t sure how long to spend there? Find out why we think staying overnight in the area is worth the effort, what you’ll gain from it, and why day trips are overrated!

Ayutthaya, one of Thailand’s ancient capitals and coolest archaeological sites, is a popular day trip option from Bangkok. At just two hours north of the city, most people figure they can nip up on the train or a tour, take a quick look around and head back within a day.

And you can totally do that (and is sometimes even necessary).

I’ve visited Ayutthaya four times now, three times overnight and once as a day trip. Half of these trips were by train and the other half by rental car. Having done a bit of everything, I’m here to convince you that spending a little longer in the area, exploring the region in more depth, and seeing a load of stuff that most people usually miss is the optimal choice.

The thing is, you can absolutely see the primary circuit of temples in a longish day, but my favourite ruins are not included on tours. You’ll miss the night market, the museums, lesser-known temples, palaces, and restaurants that are still very much worthy of your time.

Don’t forget the crowds, either. These are a problem, especially in high season, with the convoys of white vans shuttling people to the same temples, in the same order, at the same time. Then there are the incredible sunsets and sunrises – some of my favourite memories are from ancient, atmospheric ruins at sunset. Plus, your photos will definitely be better.

Even the pass is valid for two days! It’s your sign to stay overnight.

So, although I’m clearly biased, below I’ve detailed the pros and cons of both, and made the argument for an overnight stay.

## The Problem with Day Trips

We’ve already established that day trips, via train or tour bus, are the norm for a visit to Ayutthaya. In fact, the first time I visited, I took the local train in the morning and returned the same day. It was a good trip – I saw the central ruins, took a boat across the river and had a nice walk from the station. It was fine.

However, at the time, I didn’t even actually realise what I was missing.

Since then, I’ve stayed overnight nearby the Historical Park for more than a week (over three visits). This is how I realised the day trip was nowhere near as good. Here are my reasons why:

⬩ I couldn’t really avoid the crowds during the middle of the day, especially during high season. The parking lots are fuller, the photo opportunities are more difficult, and it’s just sort of loud in a bad way.

⬩ Some tours include a trip to a [sketchy elephant camp](https://therestlessbeans.com/ayutthaya-elephants-and-ethics/) – even if you don’t participate, you’ll have funded it.

⬩ Tour groups have a way of being more imposing than their individual parts. So, 30 people turning up in a group with a guide is far more disruptive than 30 individuals. It’s basic crowd science.

⬩ The train back is packed. Like, I mean not even standing room kind of crammed. If you do a day trip, you have no choice but to get a late afternoon or evening train home. Unfortunately, the Ayutthaya station stop is not the first one on the line. So, the train arrives during evening rush hour and it’s nightmarish. Remember, it’s not air-conditioned, either.

⬩ You’ll miss the night markets and sunsets. There are lovely, memorable moments to be had, when the entire old town empties out and you can just chill (not literally, Ayutthaya is always sweltering). It’s easier to breathe when the crowds go home.

⬩ There’s just not enough time to see everything that Ayutthaya and the surrounding area offers in one day. In fact, there’s no way you can see it all in two, to be honest.

⬩ Day trips are generally [not great for local residents](https://therestlessbeans.com/ayutthaya-elephants-and-ethics/). The money you spend in Bangkok rarely reaches anyone in Ayutthaya, unlike an overnight stay, which benefits many more people.

## Why Stay Overnight: Benefits

So, I’ve said why I’m not keen on day trips to Ayutthaya. Below are some detailed reasons why overnight stays are the best.

[For everything Ayutthaya, including transport, costs, food, hotels, itemised itineraries and more, check out our all-encompassing guide](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.en.html?region=15062&aid=8060012)
### 1. Photography Opportunities

If you’re into photography, there are very specific times that work best for the light in Ayutthaya (in my opinion).

Very early morning has the potential to be great, but I’d argue that because most of the individual ruin sites don’t open until around 8-8.30am, it’s not actually ideal. You can only get shots from outside, or around the lakes. As soon as the sun is properly up (around 8am onwards) the light is often hazy, flat and awful. On our most recent visit, I found it extremely difficult to take any photos in the morning that really did the place any kind of justice.

However, the very late afternoon is when the temples (literally) shine. It doesn’t even matter which ones you visit – they all actually glow and it’s so beautiful to see. I’d say that in winter, they start to get pretty after around 4.30pm, which is after the day tours leave. The sun usually sets between 6pm and 6.30pm.

There are some specific sunset spots that people rave about. Wat Chaiwatthanaram, for example, is lovely but I actually found it gets a little busier in the evenings now that everyone is talking about it (there are also a tedious number of photoshoots there in the evening).

Instead, I personally prefer Wat Phra Si Sanphet for sunset (it’s actually my favourite at any time of day). Literally nobody is there in the evening (I’ve been twice now around 6pm) and you can have the entire place and the sunset to yourself. After you’re done, you can then walk or cycle straight from there to the night market!

In terms of crowds, it’s best to avoid photography between the times of 9am to 3pm in the main temple complexes purely because that’s when it gets busy. Places like the Buddha head in the roots at Wat Mahathat can be positively heaving and, honestly, a bit miserable. Most recently, we visited at 8.30am and it was almost empty.

Check out our dedicated post on [photography at Ayutthaya](https://therestlessbeans.com/ayutthaya-photography-guide/) for more tips, information, and photos!
### 2. Great for Budget Travel

Staying in Ayutthaya is a very affordable option if you’re on a budget (way more affordable than Bangkok!).

Hotels and guesthouses are extremely well-priced, local food is as good as you’ll find price-wise in Thailand, and the train ( [or car rental](https://therestlessbeans.com/driving-in-thailand/) ) is fantastic value. The full site ticket for the Historical Park lasts for two days and is only 300 baht. Plus other entry fees, like museums and Buddhist temples are extremely reasonable or even free.

In fact, if you’re a backpacker on a budget, we’d highly recommend spending a few nights and just chilling out a bit – there’s tonnes to do and you’ll save some money on your daily budget compared to nights in Bangkok and the islands.

### 3. Things to do Beyond the Main Circuit

There are too many things to do in Ayutthaya to possibly fit into one day.

The central temples themselves will take a big chunk of your time. There’s also some very cool museums, niche shops, [a Royal Palace](https://maps.app.goo.gl/NrW55rgRuBZ5Mpew9), two first class Royal Buddhist temples, and even one of Thailand’s oldest catholic churches sat on the banks of the Chao Phraya River!

Depending on your interests, the area can easily entertain you for several or more days. So, staying overnight and tailoring a perfect itinerary will ensure you see more than the typical tourist and gain a far greater understanding of the area.

We’ve stayed for more than a week so far and haven’t got bored!
### 4. Avoiding the Crowds, Tours, and Traffic

The tour groups and crowds in Ayutthaya during high season can sometimes feel a little stifling.

I’ve visited a large number of cultural and historical sites all over the world during my travels (they’re my fave), and the best experiences* weren’t always the ones with magnificent architecture. They were the ones where I could hear the birds, imagine what the world used to be like, and where my footsteps were louder than any voices present. It’s about an atmosphere that you can almost cut through.

Ayutthaya is a normal, busy Thai city, and you’ll find that most of the ruin sites run along the main roads. This means that you’ve got peak hours traffic, tour guides with speakers, ticket office queues, lines of vans, and costume photographers. All of these will be vying for those ideal moments and photos.

This, in my experience, is worth avoiding at all costs. So, stay overnight, experience the city without the frenetic energy. It’s worth it.

### 5. Ideal Temperatures and Conditions

It goes without saying, I suppose, but between 9am and 3pm is the hottest time of day in Thailand. So, on an Ayutthaya day trip, you’ll be outdoors and in direct sunlight for basically all that time. Be particularly cautious between the months of March and May when the country is even hotter than usual.

The sites are pretty limited in shade, too, so be prepared for that as well. (At least there are no stairs or hills!).

To me, Ayutthaya has always felt unseasonly warm – hotter than almost any other area of Thailand that I have visited at the same time of year. I’ve been during December, January and April, and even in the coolest months the heat felt oppressive. It seems hotter than Bangkok to me – I don’t know why, maybe it’s the lack of air movement and breeze? Also, there are relatively few places with air-conditioning near the main temples.

Visit for more than a day and you can avoid the most overbearing times of day and take it a little easier. It’s even possible to pop into a cool museum or two during the midday heat. Then, spend the morning and evening outdoors doing the more physical stuff.
### 6. Giving Back

We always [talk about sustainability](https://therestlessbeans.com/what-is-sustainable-tourism/), supporting local businesses, giving back, and being a good tourist. I think most people want to do good, especially as visitors to other countries.

Well, for most Ayutthaya day tours originating in Bangkok, very little of the money you spend is likely to end up in Ayutthaya. The driver, the fuel stop, the tour operator – all likely reside in Bangkok.

When you spend a night in the town, you are funding a local guesthouse or hotel, buying several meals from restaurants or market stalls, having a beer at a local bar, hiring a bike, taxi, or tuktuk, and visiting small Buddhist temples or local museums. All these things avoid the economic leakage that is inherent from outsiders organising quick trips. These typically leave very little in the actual destination the visitors are benefitting from.

Staying overnight is a great way to [ensure your money goes directly](https://therestlessbeans.com/ayutthaya-elephants-and-ethics/) to the people who need it, plus you’ll have a better time because of it!

## A Sample Two-ish Day Itinerary

Below is how we’d spend two fast-paced days in Ayutthaya, seeing all the main spots and including a few bonus places. It assumes you’re arriving at around midday ( [via your chosen transport](https://therestlessbeans.com/getting-to-ayutthaya-from-bangkok/) and accommodation) and leaving your stuff at your guesthouse. Then you’ll be leaving late afternoon/early evening the next day.

Be aware that loads of businesses are closed on Mondays (and sometimes other random days, too – check ahead for your arrival dates).
### Day One

Grab some food before leaving your accommodation.

12:00 – Chao Sam Phraya National Museum (have a drink at [SaNeHa café](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ADXn4Zk28fDxwQX76) )

14:00 – Wat Phra Ram

14:45 – Wiharn Phra Mongkhon Bophit

15:15 – Wat Phra Si Sanphet

16:30 – Wat Lokaya Sutha

Optional – grab a snack at [Roti Sai Mai Abeedeen-Pranom Sangaroon](https://maps.app.goo.gl/75mNYJ1Pe7jM6Rtp7) or at one of the many food stalls just outside Wat Lokaya Sutha

17:30 – Wat Chaiwatthanaram for sunset

Optional: [Million Toy Museum](https://maps.app.goo.gl/GR7MAtEhfGWgfHtg9) (we thought it was really fun and it’s a nice break from the sun). This is good if you’re a fast wanderer like us and have some spare time in the schedule above (be aware that it closes at 4pm).

Go to [Ong Gaan Tolasap Night Market](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3UNDXA7RSfYQz4pk9) and grab a budget dinner. For an upper-market option with a view, head to [River Bar Ayutthaya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/AUnepg7by699J3LN6).
### Day Two

Grab an early breakfast before you start in or around your guesthouse, or have a quick bowl of something warm at [Jieb Tom Lueat Mu](https://maps.app.goo.gl/r2pQDF7bh432s33x7) before getting to Wat Ratchaburana (it opens really early but is very unfriendly for vegetarians I’m afraid). For an alternative veggie breakfast, go to [Ranahat Je Vegan Restaurant](https://maps.app.goo.gl/G4wsi5YBM4k5ax148) – they’re really good value and also nearby the site.

07:00 – Wat Phu Khao Thong (minimally visited white chedi with great views)

08:00 – Wat Ratchaburana

09:00 – Wat Mahathat

10:00 – Wander through Bueng Phra Ram Park

11:00 – Wat Thammikarat (optional if you have time)

13:30 – Wat Yai Chai Mongkol Worawihan

15:00 – Wat Maheyong

Before 18:21 – be at the train station (there’s a train at 18:21 that doesn’t require reservations), or drive home (it’s as quick as 45 minutes to Don Mueang Airport if you [rented a car](https://www.discovercars.com/?a_aid=Restlessbeans) from there – although realistically add 15-30 minutes due to traffic at that time of day).

Optional: If you have time, visit nearby Wat Kudi Dao along with Wat Maheyong.

12:00 – Grab some noodles at [Khanom Jeen Ton Kampu Ayothaya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/CLrWAcym7UFozzo47)

17:00 – Thai desserts at [Khanom Mho Kaeng Mae Yai](https://maps.app.goo.gl/DwH9SATe9hk2svB38)
## Ayutthaya at night

Ayutthaya isn’t exactly a party all night, Khao San Road kind of a vibe, and surely that is a good thing, right?

I’ve seen a few people who recommend staying in Bangkok overnight and only doing a day trip to Ayutthaya because the nightlife is not that exciting. However, I find its lack of Olympic-grade, extreme-sports drinking is actually an appeal.

So many people talk about wanting to see the ‘real Thailand’ (whatever that even is). Well, staying overnight in Ayutthaya is one way to see a different side of the country.

There are still a selection of bars, restaurants and things to do in the evening, they’ll just be unlikely to play Clubland ’93 at full volume. (Although there are quite a few dodgy late night spots in the new town around the Grand area, near the Balloon Ayutthaya Night Plaza).

Options on the island area include:

[River Bar](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9d6WsFGkoS9fuzSy5) : riverside setting, lovely for sunset and evening drinks (and food)

[Earthling Craft – Coffee & Bar](https://maps.app.goo.gl/PeT5xxikc847KGgWA) : quirky spot with decent drinks and pizza

[Brown Ale Ayutthaya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/RbthBVp1tNjj1m5T9) : banging little craft beer place with live music

[Norrasingh Craft Beer Bar & Restaurant](https://maps.app.goo.gl/4FC5j61R8dFsQEXy8) : live music at weekends, decent beer on tap, really comfy
## Where to Stay

Most people who stop overnight in Ayutthaya will choose a hotel near the central temple complexes, on the island. There are also options just off the island, near the train station. Alternatively, you can stay in the newer part of town near the highway, which is super convenient for people who are renting a vehicle and require hyper-modern amenities.

We’ve stayed in a selection of hotels during our time in Ayutthaya, ranging from business hotels to local guesthouses and luxury boutique spots. All our stays were excellent and I’d recommend them all:

[Romyen Café Homestay](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/home-romyen.en.html?aid=8060012) ($): this 9-room riverside guesthouse had lovely rooms and was ideally situated for visiting the ruins on foot or by bicycle. We stayed with a friend, so had two rooms between us, both of which were surprisingly modern and really clean. The owners were really lovely, too, and we felt super welcomed! It was excellent value for money.

[Centara Ayutthaya](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/centara-ayutthaya.en.html?aid=8060012) ($$): this massive business hotel is conveniently connected to the mall and nearby the highway. It has the by far nicest rooms I’ve stayed in for a long while – I’m not exaggerating. Absolutely exceptional value for money, too. If you’re looking for a larger business hotel and you have a rental car, this is the one for you.

[Sala Ayutthaya](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/sala-ayutthaya.en.html?aid=8060012) ($$$): this 26-room boutique hotel is exquisite. We stayed a few years ago now, but it continues to wow people to this day. It’s all about the design – modern, sleek interiors and an exceptional pool with a view. We’d highly recommend checking it out for something luxurious and a little different. It’s also ideally located for an easy walk to the ruins.

## How to Get to Ayutthaya

The most affordable and popular way is the train.

This third-class public train leaves from [Krung Thep Aphiwat Station](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mp9PbXxXBXThYJze7) in Bangkok (not Hua Lamphong, like it used to) with the first leaving at 6.10am. I’ve taken it several times and it’s perfectly okay. Although, it can also be horrendously busy on the return leg if you are travelling back to Bangkok at rush hour (like 4-6pm), and there’s no air-conditioning. The ticket cost in 2026 is 20 baht (no reservation required).

If you don’t take a tour, there are [several ways to get to Ayutthaya from Bangkok](https://therestlessbeans.com/getting-to-ayutthaya-from-bangkok/) independently (for both day and overnight trips).
### Our Favourite Transport Method

The best way to travel around Thailand is [by rental car](https://therestlessbeans.com/driving-in-thailand/).

It’s the ideal way to see everything you want, especially if you are travelling in a pair or group. It’s particularly good if you have kids who like cars, too (Thai public transport isn’t always super child-friendly). Parking is pretty much always free in Thailand outside of Bangkok (and a few other touristy places). We have never paid to park anywhere in Ayutthaya.

We always drive around Ayutthaya now since parking is supremely easy, aircon is a saviour, and it means we can go further afield than a typical tourist trip does. There are so many cool spots a little outside the city, like Thailand’s biggest golden Buddha (and crazy hell garden) at Wat Muang in Ang Thong. It also means we can get around the sites around town more efficiently, too.

## Where to Next?

Ayutthaya Historical Park is worth visiting, even if you only have enough time for a quick day trip. It’s a really lovely stop that’s easy enough to reach from Bangkok. So, if a day trip is your only option, then go for it!

However, I figure you already know that I think an overnight trip is well worth your time, for so many reasons. I hope I’ve persuaded you that it’s the best choice, if it fits your schedule.

For more Ayutthaya information, take a peek at [our mega-guide](https://therestlessbeans.com/ayutthaya-historical-park-guide/) that covers literally everything, from food, costs, transport, a little bit of interesting history, our favourite temples, and loads more.

If Ayutthaya has piqued your interest, take a look at Sukhothai, [Thailand’s oldest capital](https://therestlessbeans.com/sukhothai-historical-park-planning-guide/), and a genuinely incredible experience. It’s a great option if you’re heading towards Chiang Mai on your journey and are willing to travel overland. It’s magical, and one of my favourite places in Thailand.
