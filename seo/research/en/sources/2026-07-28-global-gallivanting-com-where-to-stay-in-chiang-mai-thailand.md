## The Old City: Best For Culture, History, Temples & First Time Visitors

Chiang Mai’s Old City (sometimes referred to as Chiang Mai Old Town) is the cultural and historical heart of Chiang Mai. Surrounded by an ancient moat and old city walls, this area is full of ancient temples and cultural landmarks, as well as numerous restaurants, cafes, shops, spas, museums and lively night markets

Chiang Mai Old City is a must-visit for first-time visitors, it has a laid-back and peaceful atmosphere and is centrally located to explore everything the city has to offer. It’s the perfect place to begin your Chiang Mai adventure!

The top attractions in the Old City include:

Wat Phra Singh : The most revered temple in Chiang Mai that features intricate architecture, sculpture and artwork.

Tha Pae Gate: A historic gate that was once the main entrance to the city of Chiang Mai.

Chiang Mai City Arts and Cultural Center : A museum that showcases the history and culture of Chiang Mai through a variety of exhibits and displays.

Wat Chedi Luang : An ancient temple that features a ruined stupa and a fascinating history.

Sunday Walking Street Market: A bustling market that sells everything from local food and crafts to clothing and souvenirs.

The Three Kings Monument: A monument that pays tribute to the three kings who founded Chiang Mai.

Wat Pan Tao : A unique wooden temple with beautiful gardens.

Wat Chiang Man : One of the oldest temples in Chiang Mai, featuring intricate architecture and stunning artwork.

Lanna Folklife Museum: A museum that showcases the unique culture and traditions of the Lanna people.

Buak Hard Public Park : A beautiful park within the ancient city walls.

The Old City is quite walkable and it’s easy to wander around exploring the temples in this area. Just don’t miss visiting Wat Phra That Doi Suthep. Perched on a mountain top overlooking Chiang Mai, this is the cities most stunning and revered temple. A fun way to visit is with a [Temple Tour by](https://www.viator.com/Chiang-Mai/d5267-ttd/p-163654P9?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&campaign=wheretostaychiangmai) Tuk Tuk which also takes you to one of my favorite temples hidden in the jungle.

In the Old City you’ll find authentic heritage and boutique hotels, some with outdoor swimming pools and spas, as well as affordable guesthouses and budget-friendly backpacker hostels. My favorite place to stay is in one of the [historic boutique hotels in the old town](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/boutique-hotels-in-chiang-mai/) because you are surrounded by temples and immersed in history and old world charm.

Staying in the Old City offers an immersive Chiang Mai experience and is a great option if you want to explore the city on foot, as it’s easy to navigate and walkable.

The Old City is the best area to stay in Chaing Mai for first time visitors, history buffs and culture vultures as you’ll be surrounded by history and within walking distance of the temples, tourist attractions, street food stalls and restaurants.

Plus, the Old City’s central location in the city centre makes it easy to explore other neighborhoods and attractions throughout Chiang Mai.

## Nimmanhaemin: Best For Nightlife, Modern Malls & Digital Nomads

If you’re wondering where to stay in Chiang Mai for nightlife then Nimmanhaemin is the best neighborhood for you.

Nimmanhaemin, or simply “Nimman,” is a trendy area popular among hip Thais, young travelers, and digital nomads. Being close to Chiang Mai University, the area has a youthful, lively atmosphere a vibrant nightlife.

With its trendy cafes, coworking spaces, bars, restaurants offering both international and thai cuisine, nightclubs and shopping malls, Nimman offers a more modern experience compared to the Old City.

The top attractions in Nimman area include:

Maya Lifestyle Shopping Mall – huge shopping mall with international brands.

Nimman One – a European-style shopping plaza offering boutique goods.

Famous bars and restaurants – like Tong Tem Toh, Warmup Cafe and Infinity Club.

Nimman is a great choice if you want to be close to the nightlife and entertainment, yet just a 10-minute ride from the Old City so you can enjoy the best of both worlds.

Staying in Nimmanhaemin means you’ll have easy access to everything the city has to offer, as well as a large variety of accommodations, restaurants, bars, and shops. It’s also the closest area to Chiang Mai Zoo and Chiang Mai University.

It’s one of the more expensive areas to stay in Chiang Mai but there is a wide selection of hostels, budget and mid range boutique hotels and serviced apartments available to suit all budgets.

## Night Bazaar: Best For Shopping, Nightlife and Budget Travellers

Another good option for where to stay in Chiang Mai is the Night Bazaar area. Open every evening, this bustling area is home to the famous Chiang Mai Night Bazaar, a lively night market that stretches over a kilometer long on Chang Khlan Road.

At the night markets you’ll find everything from art and handicrafts to clothing and electronics. There’s numerous street food stalls, live music and you can even watch a Muay Thai fight event.

The Night Bazaar area is a great location to stay in as it’s only 15 minutes walk from the Old City. There are accommodations to suit all budgets, from backpacker hostels to luxury hotels. It’s a great place to stay for budget travelers, culture seekers, foodies and shopaholics.

## Tips for Planning Your Trip To Chiang Mai

Before you book your accommodation and embark on your Chiang Mai adventure, it’s essential to keep a few travel tips in mind like when to visit, how long to stay and how to get to Chiang Mai. Here’s what you need to know:
### How To Get To Chiang Mai

There are several options when it comes to traveling to Chiang Mai. You can take a flight or an overnight train journey or bus from Bangkok. The easiest place to book tickets is on [12 Go Asia.](https://12go.asia/?z=293974)

Chiang Mai International Airport (CNX) receives flights from Bangkok, and other places in Thailand, and some other Asian cities. If you’re are traveling from further afield you’ll likely have to fly into Bangkok first and transfer to a short 1 hour domestic flight up to Chiang Mai.

Note that there are two airports in Bangkok so check which airport your connecting flight is leaving from. Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) receives mostly long haul international flights, while Don Mueang (DMK) is where you’ll find most of the domestic and regional budget airline flights.

There is a shuttle bus between the two airports, but be sure to allow enough time to travel across the often congested capital city, or you can take the BTS skytrain.

Most nationalities receive either a Visa Exemption or Visa on Arrival which are valid for 60 days. You can extend this once by another 30 days for 1,900 Thai Baht at the Immigration Office.

Regular taxis or songthaew are your best options for getting from Chiang Mai Airport to the city center. You can easily find both of these options outside the airport arrivals gate for a fixed fee.
### Getting Around Chiang Mai

Chiang Mai Old City is quite walkable, but for longer journeys, or if you stay in a different neighborhood, the readily available taxis, tuk tuks and songthaews (red trucks) make getting around Chiang Mai easy and affordable.

The most convenient way to arrange transportation is using the Grab App to order taxis online. Grab is a ride-hailing app similar to Uber, while songthaews are shared red trucks that run on routes (like a bus) that you can flag down.

Many Chiang Mai accommodations offer shuttle services and bike rentals, so be sure to check with your chosen hotel or resort for the services they can provide.

For those comfortable with driving a scooter or motorbike, renting one can be an efficient and affordable way to explore the city and its surroundings.

If you stay outside of the city you’ll benefit from renting a car or motorbike to have your own transportation to explore the countryside and get into the city centre.
### The Best Time to Visit Chiang Mai

November through February are the best months to visit Chiang Mai, when the weather is cool, dry and comfortable.

During this period the temperature is mild and the humidity is low, making it the perfect time to get out and explore the city and its surroundings. The days are sunny and dry and the nights are cool, making this the ideal time to visit.

The worst time to visit during the hot and dry months of March to May, and during the burning season which can start in February and can increase air pollution in the city for a couple of months.

During the rainy season from June till October cloud cover and rain showers bring relief from the hot, humid summer weather, but the rain shouldn’t ruin your plans too much so it’s not a bad time to visit.
### How Many Days to Spend in Chiang Mai

I recommend spending at least 3 days in Chiang Mai to give you enough time to see the highlights, but if you have more time then 4 or 5 days is better as this will allow you to explore at a more leisurely pace and discover some more hidden gems.

Staying in Chiang Mai for 5 days (or more) also gives you time to take some [day trips](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/day-trips-from-chiang-mai/) out of the city to explore the nature, elephant sanctuaries, national parks, waterfalls and unique temples nearby so you’ll see all the highlights of northern Thailand.
