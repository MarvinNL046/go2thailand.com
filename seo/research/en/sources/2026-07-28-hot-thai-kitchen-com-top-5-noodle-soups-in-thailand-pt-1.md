# 10 Thai Noodle Soups Thai People Can't Live Without

When most people think of Thai food, they think of curries, satays, and of course, pad thai. But if you actually look at what the average working Thai person eats on a regular basis, one thing will come up very frequently is....noodle soups! We have a huge variety of noodle soups and they are a big part of the Thai food culture, yet they are not well known outside of Thailand.

So in this post, I want to share recipes and cultural tidbits for the 10 most popular Thai noodle soups, so you can make them at home, or look out for them when you go to Thailand. As always, these recipes have been tested to make sure that they taste exactly as you would find them on the streets of Thailand!

## 1. Clear Broth Noodle Soup with Meatballs

The "OG" and the simplest to make.

Let's start with the basic: clear broth noodle soup with meatballs or guay tiew look chin nam sai ก๋วยเตี๋ยวลูกชิ้นน้ำใส, is the absolute classic. It's the ham and cheese of sandwiches, the margherita of pizzas. No matter where you are in the country, you are never a few minutes away from a vendor selling these noodle soups with a clear pork bone broth and some type of Asian style meatballs.

Asian style meatballs are commonly available fresh or frozen at most Asian grocery stores, and you can get them made with pork, chicken, beef, squid, and fish. Fish balls also come in a variety pack of various shapes, sizes, colours and flavours, which is what I bought for the pic above.

This is the simplest noodle soup to make on this list. And if you make the pork stock in advance, it's quick enough for a weeknight dinner!

As with all of the noodle soups in this list, the choice of noodles are up to you! The photo here shows a version with Thai rice vermicelli, but in Part 1 of the documentary above I talk you through all the varieties of noodles you can choose!
## 2. Yen Ta Fo - Pink Noodle Soup

Iconic pink broth with a unique spicy and sour flavour unlike any other.

Yen ta fo เย็นตาโฟ is one dish I always seek out when I go to Thailand. For years I thought yen ta fo was a dish you leave to the experts, but once I made it, it's actually really easy to make at home!

You can spot it by its unique pink broth which gets its colour from the tangy and spicy sauce made from red bean curd. However, Most street vendors nowadays use a lot of food colouring to get the bright pink colour, but my recipe uses only red bean curd for the traditional flavour and look. You can easily add some beets to the sauce for the bright pink colour if you wish!

Noodle options are up to you, as always, but my preference is for fresh wide rice noodles which you can buy at some Asian groery stores, or you can try [making fresh rice noodles at home.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fresh-rice-noodles/)
## 3. Tom Yum Noodle Soup

A classic noodle soup for spicy food lovers

If you love spicy and sour food, this is the one you want to make. Most people know "tom yum" as Thailand's [famous hot and sour soup](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tom-yum-goong/), but this is something else entirely, though with the same hot and spicy flavour.

Take the classic clear broth noodles at the top of our list, and amp up the flavours with lime juice, fish sauce, and roasted chili flakes. Then add roasted peanuts and ground pork for added texture and flavour. This is called the "tom yum" version of noodle soup, where the flavour dials for sweet, salty, spicy, and sour get cranked up!

You can "tom-yumify" many kinds of noodle soups by adding the above modifications (e.g. yen ta fo tom yum is a thing), but the classic tom yum noodles is made by modifying the classic clear broth noodle soup.

I love tom yum noodles with wonton noodles as per the pic above, or with size small rice noodles.
## 4. Boat Noodles

My favourite noodle soup of all time - this should be on your food bucket list.

One of the most iconic dishes in Thailand is boat noodles or guay tiew ruea. Thai people LOVE boat noodles, and every time I have it, I am amazed by how much flavour can be packed into such a small bowl. This recipe will give you boat noodles that taste just like what you get in Thailand, I promise!

The broth can be made with pork bones or beef bones, and it's infused with a lot of spices and seasoned with many umami-packed sauces. You have the option to enrich it with blood (which I highly recommend and is the default way it is served in Thailand) or leave it plain for a lighter broth.

It’s a complex dish with a lot of ingredients that take time to simmer (that’s why it’s so good) but it is not difficult. Many components can be prepped in advance, and in fact, once the broth is made, the rest is super quick!
## 5. Khao Soi

An icon of Northern Thai food: egg noodles in a coconut curry broth

Regional Thai food isn't well known, but khao soi ข้าวซอย, a Northern Thai noodle soup with an aromatic curry broth is an exception. Because Northern Thailand is such a popular tourist destination, khao soi has become popular amongst visitors who inevitably fall in love with it at first bite!

Khao soi broth is a coconut based curry with unique spices. It always comes with flat egg noodles, topped with crispy fried ones, and served with fresh lime, shallots, chili oil, and pickled mustard greens. It's truly a unique dish with lots of flavours and textures going on. With this recipe you can recreate the experience of Northern Thailand at home!
## 6. Duck Noodle Soup

Tender braised duck legs in a five spice broth.

Thai duck noodle soup or bamee ped toon บะหมี่เป็ดตุ๋น is a popular street food that you can easily make at home. Duck legs are slowly braised in a broth seasoned with Chinese five spice, soy sauces and palm sugar until the duck is flavourful and fall-off-the-bone tender. It's absolutely fantastic, and one of the best and easiest ways to cook duck!

Noodles for duck noodle soup are usually thin wonton noodles, or egg noodles, but it wouldn't be wrong to change them to rice noodles if you can't have wheat.
## 7. Soupless Tom Yum Noodles

Noodle soup without soup is the perfect summer noodles!

In Thailand, you can order noodle soup...without soup! While you can have soupless noodles with most kinds of noodle soups, the most popular one has gotta be soupless tom yum noodles.

Take tom yum noodle soup from earlier in this list, keep all the seasonings and fixings, but remove the broth. You end up with chewy rice noodles tossed in a sauce that's equal parts sweet-salty-spicy and sour, and topped with peanuts, fried garlic, and many other tasty toppings!

It's one of my favourite noodles if I'm eating it on a hot day on one of those street side plastic stools! Bonus: now that you don't need to make the broth, you can recreate that experience in your home much faster!
## 8. Soupless Wonton Noodles with BBQ Pork

Chewy egg noodles, tender wontons, and BBQ pork that you can just buy.

We've seen the spicy soupless tom yum noodles, and now for those who prefer things not so spicy, even your kiddos, you need to try the other super popular soupless noodles: egg noodles with wontons and BBQ pork or bamee giew hang moo dang.

Egg noodles are tossed in a flavourful seasoning, and then topped with tender wontons and Chinese BBQ pork, with some bok choy to balance everything. Topped with fried garlic, and if you want to make it spicy sprinkle some roasted chili flakes on top. It's a simple dish that's surprisingly satisfying.

This dish can be as easy or as DIY as you want because the two major components can be homemade or store bought - the wontons and the Chinese BBQ pork. The only thing I recommend you make is the fried garlic, because you'll want the garlic oil to toss the noodles in. If you want to DIY, I have recipes for both [homemade wontons](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wonton-soup/) and [homemade Chinese BBQ pork.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/char-siu/)
## 9. Thai Chicken Noodle Soup

Comforting, satisfying, perfect for your colds, especially with a little chilies!

If my grandma would make some type of noodle soup, it's this one - guay tiew gai cheek or "pulled chicken noodle soup." It's so simple, and we always have chicken in the freezer. It's the ultimate love child of Thai street food and comfort food. Well balanced flavours with lots of textures, topped with crispy garlic, with some chili vinegar for that kick if you wish. This is a chicken noodle soup unlike any other!

To make it taste like how you would get it in Thailand, I recommend using this easy [Thai style chicken stock](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/chicken-stock/) as a base. Store bought stock is fine for a quick meal, as long as it's unsalted and has a relatively neutral flavour.
## 10. Suki Nam - Hot Pot Noodle Soup

Take hot pot ingredients and turn it into a soup!

Suki nam is hotpot-turned-noodle soup. Take the ingredients that would typically go into a hot pot - glass noodles, chicken, egg, lots of veggies - and throw it all into a soup and flavour it with our spicy Thai hot pot sauce. It is SO GOOD, and healthy, too!

The secret is in the sauce, which you can make in bulk and keep in the fridge. And once you make it, you can also use it to make a "suki hang" which is basically this dish, stir fried! It's a two for one!

Fun fact: The word "suki" comes from the Japanese word "sukiyaki" which is a Japanese hot pot. Thai people took the idea and created our own version with a spicy sauce that better matches the Thai palate.
