Published/Last edited on 27th July, 2026.

An ancient centre of Thai culture and politics set along the Nan River, Phitsanulok (or P’Lok for short) today is a busy, dynamic city and one of the larger of Thailand’s provincial capitals. While most travellers see it only through the window of a bus or train enroute to [Chiang Mai](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/northern_thailand/chiang_mai/chiang_mai), those who hop off will find enough to do and eat for a solid break from the tourist trail.

Often cited as one of Thailand’s oldest (if not the oldest) continuously occupied city, the area was first settled by Khmer people as early as the 10th century. In the mid 1300s, the capital of the Sukhothai Kingdom was moved here, and the Ayutthaya Kingdom temporarily made Phitsanulok its capital around a century later. As late as 1775, the city was a strategic defensive outpost during several military attacks from Burma.

One of the most revered kings of Thai history, [Naresuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naresuan), was born in Phitsanulok in 1555. His much-romanticised story of being captured by the Burmese as a boy and later returning to Ayutthaya to defeat his former captors in battle is now the subject of a series of Thai film epics. You can visit his birthplace at Wang Chan Palace and view murals depicting key scenes from his life at [Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/219), which also houses an exquisite 700-year-old Buddha image.

Though a handful of other ancient features are scattered around Phitsanulok, today it’s a modern urban centre with bustling streets lined by cement shophouses, some great markets and often [not-so-great hotels](http://www.agoda.com/partners/tracking.aspx?cid=17598&url=https://www.agoda.com/city/phitsanulok-th.html&tag=phitsanulok). ##### Who will Phitsanulok appeal to?

While Phitsanulok doesn’t have an oversupply of things to see and do, it does have enough of a bit of everything to keep most visitors happy. If you’re considering a stay in Phitsanulok, then chances are you’re a bit of a slower traveller, and you’re biding your time before pushing on to Sukhothai. In this case, wander the town, enjoy its food, and be sure to spend the evening by the river—there are plenty of good spots to lose an evening to good food and cold drinks.
##### How long should you spend in Phitsanulok?

Many treat Phitsanulok as a transit point, arriving on a morning train, seeing a couple of the central sights and then getting an afternoon onwards bus to Sukhothai. This approach works, but if time is on your side, we feel that Phitsanulok is worth at least an overnight stay thanks to its handful of attractions and genuine urban-Thai atmosphere. If you’re planning on heading to [Thung Salaeng Luang](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/516) or [Phu Hin Rong Kla](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/515) national parks, you’ll need to add at least an extra day for each—longer still if you plan to overnight in either park.
##### What is the best time to go to Phitsanulok?

Phitsanulok shares the same climate as most of central and northern Thailand, which means November through to late February will give you the best combination of warm though not crazy hot temperatures and little to no rain. By April it will get uncomfortably hot and you’ll get more of the haze from the burning of fields, and by June the rains will start—not ebbing till October. If you want to see the waterfalls at Thung Salaeng Luang at full strength, then September and October are the best time of the year to visit.
##### What is the best thing to see or do in Phitsanulok?

In downtown Phitsanulok, the two “must sees” are the beautiful [Wat Phra Si Rattana Mahathat](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/219) and the more quirky [Sgt. Maj. Thawee Folk Museum](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/220). These are both quite conveniently located—the temple is just a short walk from the train station. If you’d like to see one of the national parks, but can’t decide which, we’d say if you’re more a nature fan, then [Thung Salaeng Luang](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/516) is a good choice, but if you like hiking and have an interest in history, then [Phu Hin Rong Kla](https://www.travelfish.org/sight_profile/thailand/northern_thailand/phitsanulok/phitsanulok/515) is probably the better bet.
