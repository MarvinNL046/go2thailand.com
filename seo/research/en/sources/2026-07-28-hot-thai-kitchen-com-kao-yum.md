# Thai Rainbow Rice Salad (Khao Yum)

Khao yum, also written as khao yam or kao yum, is a specialty of Southern Thai cuisine that is a favourite amongst health conscious Thais. Not only is it loaded with herbs and veggies, it is full of flavour and texture! The dressing is a sweet, salty and umami sauce called nam budu and it's easy to make and will keep in the fridge for months.

## What is khao yum?

Khao yum ข้าวยำ is a southern Thai salad made mainly of rice, julienned herbs and veggies, tossed with a sweet-salty dressing. It is similar to the Malay nasi kerabu, and is believed to have the same roots. It is loaded with fiber and is gluten free!

The vegetables and herbs of khao yum can change depending on specific recipes and availability, and the rice can be left plain white, or dyed blue, traditionally using butterfly pea flowers.

You can find khao yum quite commonly in southern Thailand, but in other regions, you'd typically have to go to a southern food restaurants or vendors.

#### For the b lue rice

You can leave the rice plain, but I find it much prettier to dye it blue or purple! You can also opt for brown, red, or black rice such as riceberry instead.

Chopped red cabbage or dried butterfly pea flowers. If you have butterfly pea flowers, it's very convenient and easy to dye your rice blue. But if it's not available, you can use red cabbage water (as shown in the video) with a little baking soda to turn it blue!

Baking soda. You'd only need baking soda if you're using red cabbage water and would like to turn it blue. Red cabbage water will actually turn the rice a beautiful purple, but I like to make it blue to make it similar to the version done with butterfly pea flowers in Thailand. The colour from red cabbage changes according to the pH, so adding a little baking soda will tip it over from purple to blue!

#### The Dressing - Nam Budu

The dressing can be made ahead of time and stored in the fridge for many months.

Lemongrass, bottom half only, as this is the most flavourful part

Ginger or galangal, this is one very rare instance where you can use these two herbs interchangeably! Ginger and galangal do not taste the same, but they both taste great in this dressing.

Fish sauce. Traditionally we use a special kind of fish sauce made in southern Thailand called nam budu (yes, same name as the dressing). But I find that a combination of regular fish sauce and a bit of shrimp paste does a great job mimicking the flavour.

Fermented shrimp paste ( gapi). See pic below. Added for extra umami funk, but it will mellow out in the final dish and it will not smell like shrimp paste from the tub. Don't be afraid!

Palm sugar, chopped. [Read more about palm sugar](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/guide-to-palm-sugar/).

Thai tamarind paste. You can buy ready-to-use tamarind paste in a tub or jar (often labelled "tamarind concentrate") or make it yourself easily from tamarind pulp by following my [DIY tamarind paste tutorial.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/making-tamarind-paste/)

Makrut lime leaves. Fresh or frozen will do.

#### Salad Components

Dried shrimp. You can find dried shrimp at any Asian grocery stores, in the fridge. Avoid the very small, thin papery ones with the eyes intact, as there is not much meat and they tend to be salty.

Dried s hredded coconut, unsweetened

Lemongrass, bottom half only, very thinly sliced into rings. Slice them as thinly as you can otherwise they will be tough to eat.

Makrut lime leaves, very finely julienned

Pomelo, or sub grapefruit segments. These tart juicy pieces of fruit really adds a lot to the salad. If you've never worked with a pomelo before, check out my [pomelo salad recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/yum-som-o/) and watch the video to see how to peel it.

A few fresh crunchy vegetables, julienned, such as red/green cabbage, carrots, long beans, bean sprouts, and cucumber. Try to vary the colour for a prettier dish.

Dark leafy greens. Typically we use wild betel leaves, but Chinese broccoli or kale would also work.

Chili flakes, to taste. A little spiciness is common for khao yum, but it's not necessary.
