# Thai Glass Noodle Salad - Yum Woon Sen

Glass noodle salad or yum woon sen is a staple dish in any Thai home, so it was one of the first things I made when I left Thailand and was homesick! Glass noodle salad has wonderfully bright flavours with lots of refreshing crunchy vegetables. It was a regular in our home, and I think it might become a regular in yours, too! It's gluten free, and you can change the pork and shrimp to other proteins of your choice.
## What are glass noodles?

Thai glass noodles or woon sen are thin, clear noodles made from mung bean starch. Do not confuse these with Korean glass noodles which are made from sweet potato starch and require totally different methods for preparation.

Fun Fact: In Thailand glass noodles are considered to be good for those trying to lose weight. Not because they're particularly healthy, but because glass noodles can absorb a lot more water than rice noodles or wheat noodles, so in any given serving there are less calories. In other words, much of the noodles on your plate is just water! This is why they also feel much less heavy than wheat or rice noodles.

The best quality glass noodles are made of 100% mung bean starch, with no other starches mixed in. At most Asian stores, there are usually a few brands available, and the 100% mung bean starch ones tend to be a little more expensive. If gluten is a concern, when buying, always check the ingredient list to make sure there is no wheat starch mixed in. Pure mung bean starch glass noodles are gluten free.
## Ingredients and Notes

Here are all the ingredients you'll need to make yum woon sen. For amounts, check out the full recipe card below.

Glass noodles. These are commonly available at Asian grocery stores, but sometimes they're labelled as "bean threads" or "bean vermicelli". Look for one made of 100% mung bean starch as those are the highest quality with the best texture. Cheaper ones will have other starches mixed in. Thai brands usually come in small 40 g (1.4 oz) packs, and you'll need one of these for this recipe.

Dried shrimp. These are essentially shrimp jerky and are added for extra umami, but they are optional. You can find dried shrimp at Asian grocery stores in the refrigerated section. They come in a range of sizes, but I always go for the medium ones as they are the most versatile. Store leftovers in the fridge or freezer.

Cilantro, if you don't like cilantro, green onions and/or mint can be used instead.

Thai chilies. You can add as many as you want! Start with 1 for medium to mild spice level; if you love it really spicy go with 3.

Palm sugar, finely chopped, packed. Light brown sugar can be used instead. Read [more about palm sugar here!](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/guide-to-palm-sugar/)

Fish sauce. See my post for [how to choose good fish sauce here](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/).

Fresh lime juice. Please only use fresh for this, not bottled lime juice! The flavour of lime is the hero here!

Tomato, cut into wedges. Or sub halved cherry tomatoes. I chose roma tomatoes as they are less watery, but any tomatoes that are good will be fine.

Onion, white, yellow or red will work here. Julienne them finely so they're not too strong.

Chinese celery or inner stalks of regular celery. Chinese celery smells/tastes like regular celery, but they are more leaves than stalks. I don't like to buy them cuz they often come in a big bunch that I can't use. So instead, I use the inner stalks of regular celery which are leafier and thinner, just like Chinese celery!

Ground pork or ground chicken/turkey. The ground pork will give a lot of umami to the salad, so I don't recommend skipping it. It's also the only source of fat in this salad. If you don't eat pork, ground chicken or turkey will be fine, but dark meat preferred.

Fresh shrimp, these are optional but they're commonly added to yum woon sen. You can use more ground pork or sub pieces of chicken or any other protein if you wish.

Roasted peanuts for sprinkling on top. If allergic, cashews will also work.
## Step By Step

Here's a bird's eye view of the process so you know what's involved. The full instructions are in the recipe card below. If this is your first time, I highly recommend watching the video tutorial to ensure success!

Soak the glass noodles in room temperature water for 7-10 minutes until soft and pliable. Drain, then use scissors to cut them so they're half or a third the original length - this will help with serving and mixing.

Soak the dried shrimp in hot water for a few minutes to soften, then drain and pound to break them up into smaller bits. Alternatively, chop them with a knife. Remove and set aside.

In the mortar (no need to clean it in between), pound the garlic, chilies and cilantro stems into a paste. Alternatively, finely grate the garlic, and finely mince the chilies and cilantro stems.

Add the chopped palm sugar and pound until the sugar is dissolved.

Add the fish sauce and the lime juice and stir to mix. The dressing is done!

In a large mixing bowl, add the tomato, onion, celery and the dried shrimp.

Bring a medium pot of water to a boil. Add the glass noodles and boil for 2 minutes. Remove the noodles with tongs or a wire skimmer, and place them in a sieve to drain excess water. Keep the water on the heat.

In the same pot of water, add the shrimp and cook just until they are done, 20-30 seconds depending on the size. Remove them and place directly into the mixing bowl with vegetables.

Pour out most of the shrimp cooking water, leaving just enough to cover the bottom. Add the ground pork and a splash of fish sauce and stir until the pork is fully cooked. Use a wire skimmer or slotted spoon and scoop the pork into the mixing bowl, along with roughly 1-2 tablespoon of pork cooking liquid. The pork cooking liquid will help add umami to the salad.

Add the noodles to the mixing bowl, then pour the dressing over the noodles. Use tongs to mix well. Tip: Do not add the dressing until ready to serve as this salad should be served immediately.

Add cilantro leaves and mix briefly. Ready to plate!

Plate and top with roasted peanuts. Serve immediately otherwise the noodles will continue to soak the dressing up and the salad will become dry.
## Storing Leftovers

I don't actually recommend you store any leftover. Ideally, you make as much as you can eat in one meal because it does not keep well. The noodles will harden once cold, and while they can be reheated to soften, you don't really want to reheat them because of all the fresh veggies.

The dressing will also be all absorbed into the noodles overnight, making the salad completely dry the next day. But fear not, there IS a way you can prep this dish ahead of time! Read the advance prep tips below.

## Advance Prep Tips

You can prepare all the components in advance, but do not mix them with the dressing until right before serving. Here are some things you can do:

The dressing can be made up to a few days ahead and kept in the fridge, and the veggies can also be cut up to a few days ahead as well.

Cooking proteins ahead of time is also possible, just give them a quick trip in the microwave to bring them up to room temp before mixing the salad. When you cook the pork, remember to keep a little of the pork cooking liquid as you'll need it for the salad.

Glass noodles can be soaked in advanced and kept in the fridge in a sealed container for up to a week. I would leave the cooking until serving day because cooked noodles harden in the fridge. They can be reheated to soften in the microwave, but I just find it easier to cook them before serving as they only take 2 minutes anyway.

### Ingredients US Customary Metric 1x 2x 3x

▢ 1.4 oz dry glass noodles, aka "bean threads" or "bean vermicelli" see note 1

▢ 1 Tablespoon dried shrimp, optional, see note 2

▢ 1 medium tomato, cut into wedges

▢ ¼ cup julienned onion, any kind of onion is fine

▢ 1 stalk Chinese celery or 2 inner small stalks and leaves of regular celery, leaves picked and stalks thinly sliced

▢ 6 medium or large shrimp, peeled and deveined

▢ 3.5 oz ground pork, or ground chicken or turkey

▢ 1 teaspoon [fish sauce](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/), for seasoning the pork

▢ ¼ cup roasted peanuts, unsalted, roughly chopped
#### The Dressing

▢ 10 sprigs cilantro

▢ 2 cloves garlic

▢ 1-3 Thai chilies, to taste

▢ 1 Tablespoon [palm sugar](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/guide-to-palm-sugar/), finely chopped, packed

▢ 2 Tablespoons [fish sauce](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)

▢ 3 Tablespoons fresh lime juice
### Notes

If possible use glass noodles that are made from 100% mung bean starch, such as Pine Brand.

Dried shrimp can be found in the refrigerated section of Asian grocery stores. They come in a range of sizes, and I always go for one of the medium ones.

### Instructions

Soak the glass noodles in room temperature water for 7-10 minutes until soft and pliable. Once done, drain and cut them with scissors into 2-3 sections to shorten them. This will make it easier to mix and serve.

Meanwhile, place the dried shrimp in a small heatproof bowl, cover the shrimp with room temp water then microwave on high for about 45-60 seconds. Let it sit for a few more minutes to cool slightly. Without a microwave, you can cover the dried shrimp with boiling water and let them sit for a few minutes.

Use a spoon to remove the dried shrimp from the water, leaving the water behind, and place the shrimp in a mortar and pestle and pound to break them up into bits and remove from the mortar. Without the M&P you can chop them with a knife.

Make the dressing: Chop off the leafy half of the cilantro sprigs and set aside, and chop the stems-only half into small chunks and place in the mortar and pestle along with the garlic and chilies; pound into a paste. Add the palm sugar and pound until dissolved. Add the fish sauce and lime juice and stir to mix well.

Place the tomato, onion, celery and dried shrimp into a large mixing bowl. 1 medium tomato, ¼ cup julienned onion, 1 stalk Chinese celery or 2 inner small stalks and leaves of regular celery

Bring a medium pot of water to a boil. Add the glass noodles and cook for 2 minutes. Remove them from the water with tongs (keep the water boiling) and place into a sieve to drain excess water; set aside.

Add the fresh shrimp into the same pot of water and cook for 30 seconds or so, depending on the size, until they are cooked through. Use tongs to remove the shrimp and place them into the mixing bowl with the veggies.

Pour out the cooking water, leaving just enough water to cover the bottom, and return the pot back on the stove. Once the water boils, add the ground pork and the 1 teaspoon of fish sauce and stir until fully cooked. Remove from heat and use a slotted spoon to scoop the pork from the liquid and place into the mixing bowl. Then add about 1 tablespoon or so of the pork cooking liquid into the mixing bowl as well.

Add the glass noodles into the mixing bowl, pour the dressing over the noodles, and quickly toss to combine.

Roughly chop the leafy cilantro sprigs then toss them into the salad. Plate, and sprinkle with the peanuts. Serve immediately!
