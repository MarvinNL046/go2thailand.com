## Sukothai – Orientation

Sukothai is divided into two parts – new and old. The Old Sukothai and the New Sukothai are 30km away from each other.

Most people stop in the new part because the hotels there are cheaper. I recommend staying in the old one because it’s prettier and has more character.

## What to see and do in Sukothai – The biggest attractions

As the oldest city in Thailand, Sukothai is not that impressive. It’s not big, it’s not a bustling hub, but it is charming. I’d say that yes, Sukothai is worth a visit.

During my stay I found a few things to see and do there. I spent a couple of lovely days, enjoying the sights, driving my motorbike and just learning more about Thailand. This is a place not only for [history buffs](https://www.theblondtravels.com/thailand-history-cultural-places), but also for those, who would like to rest from busy tourist destinations.

Here are things to do in Sukothai.
### Sukothai Historical Park

The ruins of the ancient city in Sukothai are probably the most beautiful ones I’ve seen.

Sukothai was once the capital of Siam and today what’s left of it is 700 years old! The whole park is the UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The park is quite small. It’s not Angkor Wat in Cambodia, but thanks to its modest size it seems a bit more friendly.

The best way of visiting it is renting a bike, which you can hire near the entrance gates. The cost is 20-30 Baht. Go there around 4pm. This way you will avoid the heat. Stay until sunset. The light at that time is really great. Choose a quiet place and see the sun setting over the horizon.

The park is situated at the end of the main road in the Old Sukothai. You can visit it in 2-3 hours – if you’re in a rush or you don’t like places like that. Others can take their time and spend a whole evening here.

The cost is 100 Baht. There is a fee for a bike – 10 Baht.

Opening hours are from 6:30 am to 7:30 pm.

### Witness morning alms

Perhaps you heard of Luang Prabang. The main attraction there is to see [monks receiving morning alms](https://www.theblondtravels.com/morning-alms-sukothai). In South East Asia it always happens in the mornings and it’s a really beautiful ceremony.

Sukothai is an alternative to Luang Prabang. Every day, from the main temple, Wat Traphang Ngoen, in the Old Sukothai, monks go out on the street and walk through a lovely wooden bridge, which connects the street with the temple.

Thai people kneel down and offer gifts to the monks. It’s really a beautiful scene.

Make sure that you come here around 6am or at least 30 minutes before the sunrise to take a good place.
### Eat delicious noodles

Sukothai noodles is a very popular dish in Thailand. They are aromatic, served with different meat and/or with delicious broth.

[The best noodles I had in Sukothai were from Ta Puy Noodle](https://www.theblondtravels.com/sukothai-noodles), which is around 30 km away from the Old Sukothai.
### Visit the night market

On the weekend, from around 6pm, people gather to eat and drink at the night market, near the Historic Park. It’s a very special location. You can buy some food and drinks first and then sit down at a pond and admire the sun setting over one of the temples. It’s really amazing.

Before the pandemic Sukothai had so called a walking street, a street which was closed to traffic and then street sellers were putting up their stalls. This has been cancelled and I’m not sure if they are planning to reintroduce it. During my stay I had an opportunity to go to the weekend market in the Historic Park and that was a great experience.

## Where to drink and eat?

Sukothai has many places to eat and drink for a good price. In the Old Sukothai go to Sureerat Restaurant.

If you want to eat something really delicious, head outside of the New Sukothai for [Sukothai Noodles](https://www.theblondtravels.com/sukhothai-noodles), which are just amazing.

Closer to the Historic Park you’ll find quite a lot of bars.

Don’t count on any night life. The town is quite and calm and many places close after 10pm.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Sukhothai

Is Sukhothai worth visiting?

Yes! Sukhothai Historical Park has some of Thailand’s most beautiful ancient ruins, and the town is charming and less touristy than Ayutthaya.

How many days do you need in Sukhothai?

2-3 days is perfect to explore the Historical Park, witness morning alms, and try Sukhothai noodles without rushing.

How do I get to Sukhothai from Bangkok?

You can take a train, bus, or fly to the nearby airport. The journey takes 6-7 hours by train or bus.

Should I stay in Old Sukhothai or New Sukhothai?

Stay in Old Sukhothai. It’s prettier, closer to the Historical Park, and has more character and restaurants.

What is the best time to visit Sukhothai Historical Park?

Go around 4pm to avoid the heat and stay until sunset. The lighting is beautiful and the park is less crowded.

Sukothai is an amazing destination, one that is worth visiting if it’s on your way. Spend 2-3 days here, visit the historical park and enjoy the famous Sukothai noodles. Take it easy, don’t rush and try to learn as much as possible about the history of this place. I guarnatee that you will not be disappointed by your visit to Sukothai.
