# The best hotels in Koh Samui, Thailand
### Four Seasons Koh Samui

[Four Seasons Koh Samui](https://www.cntraveller.com/hotels/5-surat-thani/four-seasons-koh-samui-thailand-hotel-review)

It’s the island’s priciest place to stay (just shy of £1000 a night in the low season, and double that in high), but it is also the very best. A stay at Four Seasons Samui is like stepping into the White Lotus life – part of the reason why season three of the hit HBO series was reportedly filmed here. It’s easy to see why it was chosen – it’s serene and seriously photogenic, with 78 residences and villas cut into a steep jungly hillside (and former coconut plantation). Architect and interior designer Bill Bensley can take full credit for the stylish and lux feel, from the jazzy artwork to the very cool Coco Rum pool bar, where striped sofas line a palm-dotted pool with sea views beyond

Koh Thai, the open-air restaurant, has food as good as the views (handily, you don’t have to stay to eat here). It’s the same spot for breakfast, where an incredible French pastry chef prepares the most delicious matcha madeleines, vanilla brioche and chocolate financiers – every morning is a surprise. There’s a spa, of course, there is, with treatments using Thai herbal products, a breezy outdoor yoga pavilion and a small kid’s club in a treehouse. Snorkel offshore, laze on a lounger the size of a super-king bed on the beach or sip spirits in the rum vault (one of the many drinking spots) before a lantern-lit meal on the beach.

Serene hotels on Koh Samui – Thailand&apos;s most luxurious palm-fringed island

Back in the 1980s, Koh Samui’s hotel scene looked unrecognisably different. Its first luxury hotel opened that decade – today, 50 or so years later, you’ll find some of [Thailand’s](https://www.cntraveller.com/article/7-wonders-of-southeast-asia-for-2026-from-thailand-to-the-philippines), and the world’s, most esteemed stays on its sun-kissed shores.

This is Thailand’s most luxurious [island](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/thailand-islands-guide) (with Phuket also vying for first place), but still, there are hotels for virtually every type of traveller. It may be that you’re seeking a wellness retreat (it’s home to one of the most life-altering you’ll find) or an infinity pool villa with the most remarkable sunset views. Perhaps you’re keen to stay on your own private island, live the [White Lotus](https://www.cntraveller.com/article/how-locals-really-feel-about-the-white-lotus-coming-to-town-in-st-tropez) life (season three of the hit [HBO drama](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/koh-samui-thailand-white-lotus) was mostly filmed here) or keep your kids entertained morning till night with crazy golf, ball pits and playgrounds. It’s not all luxury, of course; some of [Thailand’s cheapest, nicest hotels](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/the-nicest-affordable-hotels-in-thailand-to-book-for-your-next-holiday) are on the island, too.

Whether you’re travelling as a couple, solo, with family or friends, here we round up the best hotels on Koh Samui.

## Conrad Koh Samui

It may be tempting to book a hotel in the buzzy north of Koh Samui, but if spellbinding views are your thing – and why wouldn’t they be? – then go for Conrad Koh Samui, in the island’s quieter south-west corner. Its location, high up over the water, gives panoramic views of the Five Islands, which rise like jagged teeth from the water, way out in the distance. You’ll see them from your stilted villa’s bedroom and infinity pool, and virtually every other spot in the hotel, including three exceptional restaurants.

The first is Aow Thai, a laidback space of rattan lamps and cream cushions, with a menu of southern specialities. The crispy pork is excellent, but there are great vegan options, too. It doubles as the breakfast room and terrace, where you’ll happily sip the island’s best flat whites (the hotel has its own coffee roastery at Botanikka cafe), and feast on a carefully considered a la carte menu and buffet spread, with smoked salmon, jams made by artisan producers and a brilliant avocado toast with tomato chutney. Azure, the Mediterranean by the main pool, serves up pizzas and Greek classics, while Jahn is the signature restaurant, a moodily-lit space with just 24 covers, and a rich tasting menu of steak tartare, crab cakes and more. There are more views from the spa, with its Aromatherapy Associates treatments. Settle into the sauna at sunset – it has a large floor-to-ceiling glass window to make sure you get the views at virtually every given moment.

Aside from all of that, there are two other reasons this is a truly stand-out stay. The first is Project Iris, the hotel’s very own farm (and the largest hotel farm on the island), which is located at the bottom of the hill and run by Khun Pong and his small team. Step inside on one of the daily tours to a peaceful world of plants, fruit trees and vegetable patches that supply 70% of the produce used in the hotel’s kitchens, and 100% of its eggs (which are collected every morning before being perfectly poached and scrambled). My kids loved the chance to see the resident goats, peacocks, chickens, buffalo and tortoises, which happily roam around enjoying the good life. The other element to love is the seemingly endless list of complementary workshops; during my stay, I learnt to make kombucha, mixed my own tea, plus made an all-natural Thai and relaxing bath bag. If that’s not for you, then free 90 minutes per day in the hotel’s huge, private karaoke room may well be…

## Banyan Tree Samui

A stay at Banyan Tree Koh Samui poses a daily dilemma. Beach or pool? Because here, they’re both sublime. The answer, of course, is the morning at one, the afternoon at the other – meaning you begin your day on one of the most idyllic stretches of sand on the island – a small bay, enclosed by huge granite boulders, and inaccessible to anyone but hotel guests. You’ll lose hours – really, hours – in the sea here, which is perfectly calm, clear and teeming with tiny stripey fish (for a better look, snorkels, fins, paddle boards and kayaks are ready and waiting).

The rest of the elegant resort tumbles down a steep hillside that’s pleasingly reminiscent of the Four Seasons Seychelles. Jungly pathways wiggle and wind between the stilted pool villas and restaurants, with highly efficient buggy drivers (which is not a given in luxury hotels on the island) getting you from A to B in a matter of minutes. That jungly setting is just one of the reasons that a stay here feels so special – you’re surrounded by hundreds of plant and tree species, yet the likes of bustling Lamai are just a short drive away. In the spa, softly spoken therapists deliver muscle-melting massages and facials, but it’s worth leaving time for the ‘Rainforest’, a vast space of steam rooms, saunas, huge hydrotherapy pools and bracing bucket showers. In the evening, there’s casual beachside dining beneath lantern-lit tree branches on the edge of the water, but the really special spot is Michelin guide-listed Saffron, located high up in the hills. The mixed starter is divine – with betel leaf and fried prawns, and the chicken and cashew nuts is a must order.

Address: [99, 9, Maret, Surat Thani 84310, Thailand](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JcGuQwLsNkzVHZqK7)

Price: From about £520 per night.

## SALA Samui Cheongmon Resort

If you’re looking for a stay that straddles a chilled-out beach club vibe with stylish and serene accommodation, SALA Cheongmon is the perfect choice. It’s located on a buzzy curve of sand in Cheongmon, where jet skis zip across the sparkling sea and ridiculously inexpensive masseuses tout for business in one of their open-air cabanas.

Step inside SALA and things feel considerably more zen: the entire resort is decorated in a palette of whites, greys and greiges, from the oversized parasols that are suspended over huge double-bed size sunloungers, to the Tic Tac white walls that ensure the privacy of the gabled roofs of various villas and rooms. Inside, everything feels minimalist and contemporary, with polished cream floors and low-slung beds draped with floaty white voile. Bathrooms are all open to the elements, with sunken stone tubs and rain showers, and there are sizeable pools – big enough to cool off, not big enough for a swim – in the villas. But who needs a big pool anyway? There are two others to choose from, including an 82-ft-long adult-only pool lined with black tiles, and a family pool, with tiered and shaded areas. And while this isn’t the first place you might head to with young kids if you’re looking for a bells-and-whistles family resort with a kids club and splash zones (it has neither – and some parents are here to celebrate that), any children will be more than welcome here, feeding koi carp in the pond and watching fire shows after dark. Really, though, it’s a spot for couples, solos and friends – anyone happy to dine on the sand, guzzle cocktails at the twice-daily happy hour or stargaze on a moonlit beach.

Address: [10 Baan Plai Laem 9, Tambon Bo Put, Amphoe Ko Samui, Surat Thani 84320, Thailand](https://maps.app.goo.gl/oedtZJ1sEqrY3kD58)

Price: From about £360 per night.
## Anantara Lawana

Couples and families mingle happily together at Anantara Lawana, a resort-style hotel located close to the nightlife of Chaweng on Samui’s east coast. Sociable types should go for one of the standard bedrooms, located in the soft grey two-storey Thai-style houses. For sunshine? You’ll need to head to the buzzy pool or beach, a great spot for a paddle at low tide or a proper swim at high tide, when colourful long-tail boats bob on the water just along the shore.

Those seeking quiet may prefer the private pool villas – with their tiled floors and traditional dark wooden furniture, including four poster beds, they feel very grown up. The spa is another sanctuary in the hotel, where discreet therapists deliver back-crunching bamboo massages and ayurvedic Shirodhara therapy. Crab Shack is your sand-between-the-toes sunset cocktail spot, while Tree Tops, built in a treehouse-style wooden structure wrapped around a century-old tree, is the hotel’s romantic dining option (book early to catch the sunset here). The kids club is a hive of activity, with wooden tractors and play kitchens.

## Cape Fahn

One of Samui’s best – and most luxurious – places to bed down isn’t actually on Samui, but even better: a private island around 100 metres offshore (you’ll be whisked there and back in a small speedboat or glorified tractor depending on the tide levels). Here, you’ll find 24 villas, from Ocean Cottages to two-bed family villas, all with private pools where you can snooze to the sound of birdsong and gently lapping waves.

All other spaces are pure bliss, too: like the breakfast terrace views and the peaceful, sun-soaked adjoining room, not just because of its pretty canary yellow cushions and bamboo-style chairs, but for its spread of pastries, baked in house; or Hue restaurant, where you can gaze out past the pin-stripe loungers, pool and palms to the shimmering sea beyond. Long Dtai, overseen by Michelin-recognised chef David Thompson, is worth a visit even if you can’t stay here: not just for the panoramic views over the turquoise Andaman, which are arguably some of the best restaurant views on the island, but for the menu: the Southern egg curry being a highlight. Despite being one of the Samui’s most expensive stays, it’s also one of the most relaxed: where retired business owners wander the same winding paths around the small island as young honeymooners.

## Six Senses Samui

One of the most sustainable stays on Samui is on a sloping jungle-clad headland on the island’s northeastern tip. The bamboo walls and high thatched roofs of 66 multi-level villas (most with private pools) give the feeling of staying in a luxe treehouse.

The vibe is chic, yet fun: natural wood tones are combined with pops of colour from the neon-green and sherbet-yellow cushions and loungers, and staff stroll around in Millennial pink and dusty rose linen two pieces. The first is quiet (one of the island’s best and biggest infinity pools), the other buzzier and next to the beach, on a cove shared with neighbouring hotels – one of the only clues that you’re on a popular island rather than a remote hideaway. Mornings start with a shot of ‘rocket fuel’ (cider vinegar and citrus) at breakfast, and there’s an excellent menu of mocktails, free ice cream and several scenic restaurant options, where fragrant curries are served up next to sunset views (although the best views at sunset are from the spa). Little and large guests love the on-site farm, where they can pet and feed a herd of well-preened goats.

## Silavadee Resort

Young honeymooning couples and equally loved-up retirees happily mingle at Silavadee, a boutique resort built across a hillside on a quiet peninsula on the east coast of Samui. Although the entry-level rooms are pretty, with their outdoor jacuzzis and airy interiors, if you’re staying here it should be an Ocean Front Pool Villa. Our pick? Number three: with its separate living room, freestanding bathtub, huge bedroom and even bigger pool, you could happily spend your entire stay in the privacy of your villa, floating in your pool or gazing out at miles and miles of empty sea. There’s a simple spa where efficient therapists expertly deliver tension-relieving Thai massages, and the two open-air dining spots are both romantic and well-priced (the wine pairings, in particular, are generous and a steal). Lively Lamai beach is a short scooter ride away, or if that’s too taxing, you’ll find some of the best snorkelling on the island right off the hotel’s quiet curve of beach – handily, for guests, it’s a small cove

that’s near impossible for anyone else on the island to access.

## Kimpton Kitalay

Choeng Mon beach, with its fire shows, jet skis and beach bars, sets the tone for Kimpton Kitalay, a buzzy hotel in Samui’s north. It’s built in the style of a fisherman’s village, with six, low-rise blocks of rooms wrapped in dark wood. You can bed down in two-bed villas, but entry level rooms are large and stylish, with Victorian-style tiles, curvy freestanding bathtubs and jazzy blue rugs.

The main pool is a sociable spot, as is the splash park and kids club, but there’s always a quieter place to escape to – especially if you opt for a swim up suite on the ‘quiet’ side, or book time in the spa. There are four restaurants and bars, and if you tire of Thai food, Fish House serves excellent steak and huge sides, plus fresh-off-the-boat fish. You may spot one or two dogs strolling the grounds: the hotel works with a local rescue dog charity, and dogs that are up for adoption visit every fortnight to meet guests.

Address: [Kimpton Kitalay, 10, Bo Put, Ko Samui District, Surat Thani 84320, Thailand](https://www.google.com/maps?sca_esv=920940ff3dbc57d7&rlz=1C5GCCM_en&output=search&q=Kimpton+Kitalay,+10,+Bo+Put,+Ko+Samui+District,+Surat+Thani+84320,+Thailand&source=lnms&entry=mc&ved=1t:200715&ictx=111)

## Ritz Carlton

This sprawling hotel, built across 58 hilly acres in a former coconut plantation, works hard to impress – and delivers. Kids will be overjoyed at the island’s best kids club: a huge light-filled space of ball pits, slides and toys, with an outdoor crazy golf course and playpark. For adults, there’s the Spa Village, which is indeed a village – with eight standalone treatment rooms (try the pre-spa Songkran Spa Ritual) and a large, barely-used adult-only pool dotted with cabanas.

The View serves up some of the island’s highest sea views alongside creative tasting menus, while Baan Talta is modelled on a Thai street food market, but there are several more options should you dine here each night. A Muay Thai boxing ring, basketball and tennis courts are great if you get restless, while a beautiful beach of cream-coloured sand and a large curvy pool if you long to just laze. There are 175 bedrooms, suites and villas, but oceanfront pool villas have the real wow factor: outdoor decks and infinity pools, where you can gaze over the edge and see nothing but turquoise water and hear nothing but the waves meeting the rocks below. If you’re travelling with kids – or even if you’re not – the Ritz Carlton Bangkok is the perfect partner. It’s located inches away from Lumphini Park (watch monitor lizards glide across the lakes from your suite), has a cute sky- and space-themed kids’ club and is minutes away from Harborland One Bangkok, the biggest, best and cleanest soft play you will ever come across.

## Samujana

Twenty-three contemporary villas, cut into a meticulously landscaped flower-filled slope on the north-east of the island, every one staggeringly luxurious and big on views: strictly speaking Samujana isn’t a hotel, but it operates very much like one, with added privacy and space, making it particularly popular with large groups of family and friends.

Unlike a lot of villa stays, you won’t need to lift a finger: breakfast is provided in the villa every morning, rooms are made up and turned down, and there are team members on hand to rustle up meals. It’s hard to choose the best: perhaps it’s villa 12, with its own boxing gym, basketball court, games room and cinema room, or maybe the ludicrously photogenic villa 30, with 360-degree views and the country's best infinity pool – a serious accolade when the competition is fierce. Others, with their sun-flooded open-plan open-air communal areas, large pools and calm-inducing minimalist styling are so lovely that you could happily lose a week or two here, never venturing out at all.

## Kamalaya

To call Kamalaya a hotel wouldn’t be doing it justice: yes, there are hotel rooms and villas dotted across this bougainvillaea-draped hillside on the island’s quieter south coast, but at the heart of this sanctuary is a focus on wellness, both physical and mental. Most guests here book in on dedicated programmes, whether it’s detoxes and cleanses or programmes such as Embracing Change, in which you’ll spend time with Life Enhancement Mentors, unravelling past trauma with talking therapy and meditation. Treatments – many of them ayuverdic – are all tailored to your issues, with acupuncture and heavenly massages. The site is built around a 300-year-old mediation cave, which you can still use today, plus there’s walking meditation, yoga and a peaceful stretch of sand for late-afternoon siestas. Solo travellers here may like the communal dining, where everyone from opera singers to burnt out CEOs gather to knock back green juices at breakfast and feast on sugar-free curries in the evening, but you’d also feel entirely comfortable dining solo, reading a book over meals, or simply gazing out at the water.

## Vana Belle, A Luxury Collection Resort

You’ll have your pick of big-brand hotels on Koh Samui, but Vana Belle – part of the Marriott group – still manages to retain a boutique hotel feel, thanks to its size (just 79 rooms and villas dotted across 9 acres). Its location, spread out along Chaweng Noi beach, is one of the main reasons to choose here; it’s quieter and a little more under the radar than buzzy Chaweng, just around the bay, with fewer jetskis and just a smattering of people, all pleasingly spread out.

Days here are lost snoozing in the shade of dozens of varieties of trees on the golden ribbon of sand. In the British summer, the sea is a shade of zingy turquoise and virtually flat; around March, there are some livelier waves to jump and dive into, thanks to the winds across the water. At the edge of the beach, a large pool is the perfect choice for those with babies and pre-schoolers, and the beach bar is a hit every night with golden hour cocktails – in fact, you’ll see guests linger far longer on the beach here as sundown approaches, because it’s that pretty and peaceful.

And while you’re a short walk away from a main road of beauty parlours and cheap restaurants, make the time to dine here too, either on the lantern-lit lawn to a soundtrack of waves at Thai restaurant Panali, or higher up with serene sea views at Kiree.

Address: [9 Noi Beach, 99 Chaweng Beach Rd, Tambon Bo Put, Amphoe Ko Samui, Surat Thani 84320, Thailand](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ednnJ5jig6Nn9v1L6)

Price: From about £350 per night.
## Garrya Tongsai Bay Samui

There are plenty of lovely beaches on Samui, but Tongsai Bay? This curve of golden sand, with its gently tumbling turquoise waves, feels impossible to beat – and, unless you’re willing to paddle in by kayak, it’s only accessible to Garrya guests. The hotel that opened here back in the mid-80s was the first five-star resort on the island and has been welcoming loyal guests, some of them as many as 20 times, since.

A recent rebrand to a Garrya resort has kept some of the much-loved elements of the hotel, like outdoor bathtubs, and refreshed others: high-ceilinged rooms are simple yet luxurious, with calming beige and burnt orange palette and varnished wooden floors. There’s a spa, breezy outdoor massage cabanas set inches from the waves and three restaurants, including beachside dining at Fish Tales (don’t miss the vegan chocolate ice cream), and Evo, where much of the food is made with produce taken from the hotel’s garden. Then there are decisions to make – the half-moon adults-only pool, the curvy beach-level pool, or a spot on the beach itself…

Address: [Garrya Tongsai Bay Samui, 84 Moo 5, Bophut, Koh Samui Suratthani 84320, Thailand](https://www.google.com/maps?sca_esv=920940ff3dbc57d7&rlz=1C5GCCM_en&output=search&q=Garrya+Tongsai+Bay,+84,+Tambon+Bo+Put,+Amphoe+Ko+Samui,+Surat+Thani+84320,+Thailand&source=lnms&entry=mc&ved=1t:200715&ictx=111)

Price: From about £200 per night.
## Centara Reserve

Special stays on Koh Samui aren’t just about stilted villas and private islands. This large but luxurious resort on Chaweng Beach, on Samui’s east coast, ticks so many boxes, with room rates that leave plenty of leftover budget for splurging on excursions to ethical elephant sanctuaries and daily massages. Rooms are bright, modern and stylish – with jute rugs, pretty linen curtains (with excellent black-out lining) and bathrooms with matt-gold taps and marble counters. Suites – with sliding doors between living and sleeping space – are great for families, and while you could opt for a ground-floor room with a private pool, you’ll be so happy with shared the adult-only pool, large family pool and the glorious beach, where waves vary between gentle and fun and jumpable, depending on the season. That’s if you can drag them from the light-flooded Kid Zone kids club, where shelves are lined with every wooden toy imaginable, plus plenty of craft activities (my eldest made a pair of cardboard binoculars for spotting the planes that take off nearby).

There are other lovely elements here: unlike many luxury hotels that still opt for plastic plates for kids, even children’s cutlery and bowls are wooden – the resort is proudly plastic-free, with some interesting initiatives (how about swapping daily room cleaning for credit in one of the restaurants?). As for those restaurants? You may be tempted by the cheap and cheerful eateries just outside the hotel’s tree-dotted gardens, but leave time for a meal or two here. The multi-course sharing menu at Sa-Nga, which featured goong chom cha (raw blue prawn with granita and crispy garlic), with a very reasonable wine-pairing and thoughtful non-alcoholic cocktail pairing, was so delicious and pleasingly unpretentious. Then, virtually touching the sand of the beach, is Salt Society. It’s famous for its Sunday brunches, but the regular menu, featuring seabass with red and green chimichurri, served with a choice of 12 salts, is very, very good.

Address: [38, Chaweng Beach, 2 Borpud, Tambon Bo Put, Amphoe Ko Samui, Surat Thani 84320, Thailand](https://maps.app.goo.gl/2TaMFBDHSsZ3pM517)

Price: From about £230 per night.
