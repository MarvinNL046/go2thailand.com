## Rambles you can skip

I have initially thought of creating a section exploring the brief history of Chiang Rai. But the information I read was contradictory. Since I am no historian, I thought it best to set this as my rambles instead.

Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai are distinctly different places about 3-4 hours apart. Several sources state that Chiang Mai was founded by King Mangrai in 1296. The nomadic king changed his capital city several times.

Chiang Rai was also founded by King Mangrai, three decades earlier than Chiang Mai in 1263. There’s an old 1989 PDF of a research report on the history of Chiang Rai that you can easily Google, if you are interested. 😛

Whatever the case is, for now, Chiang Mai is known as a bustling city with international conveniences. Whereas Chiang Rai is the sleepy laid-back town where artists flourish and where you can enjoy a slower pace of life (and better air quality).
## Best Time to Visit Chiang Rai

Similar to [Hanoi](https://www.roowanders.com/hanoi-itinerary/) and [Luang Prabang](https://www.roowanders.com/luang-prabang/), the best time to visit Chiang Rai and Northern Thailand is from November to February.

There are three distinct seasons – summer, rainy and winter.

November to early February is known as the winter season. This is the best time to visit Chiang Rai for a comfortable climate – minimal rain, low humidity, and cool temperatures. However, this season coincides with festivities like Loy Krathong (Festival of Lights) in November, Christmas and New Year, and Lunar New Year in January/ February. Expect higher prices. [Book your transport](https://12go.asia/en/?z=8031163), lodgings and [activities in advance](https://gyg.me/JG6ZvwUn) if you visit during this time.

Temperature increases as we approach the middle of the year. There’s research indicating that certain parts of Thailand could be [as hot as the Sahara by 2070](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/se-asia/thailand-could-become-as-hot-as-the-sahara-by-2070-research-shows). 😱 March to July is hot, hence the summer season.

The monsoon season is from July to October, with heavy downpours and storms almost daily. September tends to be the worst. [Chiang Mai and the surrounding region were inundated by severe floods in early October 2024](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2878053/chiang-mai-flood-crisis-worsens). Debris was mostly cleared off during my visit later in the month, but I could see packs of sandbags stacked outside some closed shops in the Chiang Rai Night Bazaar. July to October is wet, hence the rainy season.

I visited [Chiang Mai](https://www.roowanders.com/chiang-mai/) and Chiang Rai in late October/ early November to take advantage of a public holiday in Singapore, and the transition period of the seasons. During my week-long trip, I encountered only one morning of heavy rain – on my last day – in Chiang Mai. 🤭
### Bus

Taking the bus is without a doubt, the easiest and cheapest way to travel from Chiang Mai – if you were to start there – to Chiang Rai. Buses from Chiang Mai to Chiang Rai depart from Bus Terminal 3. You could buy your tickets physically at the bus terminal, or book them online in advance at [12Go](https://12go.asia/en/?z=8031163) – the easiest way to buy bus tickets in Thailand, as recommended by all my hosts in Chiang Mai.

Buses start running from 7 AM with the last bus leaving at 6 PM. There’s a bus almost every hour. The journey between Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai takes about 4 hours.

### Follow the odyssey of the Emerald Buddha at Wat Phra Kaew

Phra Kaew is the holiest Buddha image in Thailand. Known as the Emerald Buddha, it is currently housed in Wat Phra Kaew of the Grand Palace in [Bangkok](https://www.roowanders.com/bangkok/).

I first learned about the Emerald Buddha when I solo-travelled in [Vientiane, Laos](https://www.roowanders.com/vientiane/). As you’d see from that post, I stumbled onto Ho Phra Keo, which sounds vaguely similar to Phra Kaew, and learned about the sacred Buddha. This Vientiane shrine which used to house the Emerald Buddha in 1565 is now a museum.

According to various sources on the internet, the Buddha image supposedly came from [India](https://www.roowanders.com/posts/india/) or [Sri Lanka](https://www.roowanders.com/colombo/) before making its lengthy odyssey to Southeast Asia, namely [Thailand](https://www.roowanders.com/posts/thailand/) and [Laos](https://www.roowanders.com/posts/laos/). It might even have journeyed to [Cambodia](https://www.roowanders.com/posts/cambodia/) and briefly stayed in [Angkor Wat](https://www.roowanders.com/the-angkor-complex-everything-you-need-to-know/) during the reign of the Khmer Empire.

However, the information board in Wat Phra Kaew of Chiang Rai claims that the Buddha image was revealed when a bolt of lightning struck a reliquary in 1434.

Wat Phra Kaew of Chiang Rai is open every day from 6 AM – 6 PM – about the same time as from sunrise to sunset.

There’s a lot of mystery surrounding the Buddha image, now revered by the Thais for bringing stability and prosperity to their country. Interested in learning more? [This article from World History is a good starting place to follow the footsteps of the Emerald Buddha.](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1555/the-temple-of-the-emerald-buddha/)
### Be dazzled by the stunning white glow of Wat Rong Khun

Better known as the White Temple, Wat Rong Khun is Thai visual artist Chalermchai Kositpipat’s biggest art piece. There used to be an abandoned ruinous temple on the very site, until Chalermchai decided to rehabilitate the area and rebuild the temple in 1997.

Chalermchai is known worldwide for his incorporation of Buddhist imagery into his works. Google to see it.

Construction of Wat Rong Khun was completed in 2008. But new elements are constantly added to the site, such as a Ganesha Exhibition Hall and a Buddha cave. The artist estimated that he’d need decades (until 2070) before he’s finally pleased with the result. The construction of his masterpiece totalled tens of millions of baht, paid solely from his pockets (and likely from our admission tickets, generous donations, or souvenirs bought onsite).

Contrary to what he first pledged, [an update from the artist in 2022](https://www.bangkokpost.com/life/arts-and-entertainment/2398903/renowned-artist-chalermchai-lays-down-his-brushes) mentioned that Chalermchai has found a new love in motorbiking and art is now relegated to the backstage. [You can stalk him on his new journey on his Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/fanofchalermchai/).

Is the White Temple worth exploring?

It still is if you’re up for photos or an art exploration that happens to be a temple. The exterior is stunning, but I thought the orange interior was eye-catching with images of a man in pain, sorrow and other emotions, but I didn’t see any Superman, Harry Potter or Matrix that other websites have described.

Only the White Temple is ticketed (THB 100). It’s free to wander around the compound, such as visiting the Ganesha Exhibition Hall or art galleries. The Cave of Art is ticketed at THB 50. There are free chilled water dispensers and the toilet is clean! ✨

Wat Rong Khun is open from 8 AM to 5 PM.
### Marvel at the blue hues of Wat Rong Suea Ten

Wat Rong Suea Ten, more popularly known as the Blue Temple of Chiang Rai, is a modern temple designed in the same manner as the White Temple. But this temple is not a creation of Chalermchai, but that of his student, Putha Kabkaew.

Translated as the ‘Temple of the Dancing Tiger’, the big orange cats were said to have roamed in the temple grounds a century ago. As with the White Temple, construction of the Blue Temple was initiated over an old abandoned temple in 1996. But work only started almost a decade later.

Artist Putha Kabkaew combined elements from his teacher and that of artist Thawan Duchanee who designed the Baan Dam, or the Black House.

Just like our visit to the White Temple, the Blue Temple felt like a refreshing take on traditional Thai temples and a fun place to enjoy the peculiarity of the statues, Buddha images and visuals.

Wat Rong Suea Ten is free to visit. It is open from 7 AM to 8 PM.
### Explore the spookish charm of Baan Dam Museum

Baan Dam, or The Black House of Chiang Rai, is not a temple – although its exterior looks like one – but a house featuring the designs and artworks of the late artist Thawan Duchanee.

Artist Thawan combined traditional Thai architecture and his unconventional, bordering-on-eerie style to create a series of black houses topped by pointy roofs. The museum has been around since the 1970s.

During our visit, we saw the Main Sanctuary Hall decorated with several large pieces of fearsome paintings, a bust of Thawan, and long tables draped with crocodile skins. Visitors were encouraged to interact with the art pieces using Instagram filters designed for each artwork by scanning QR codes. We had fun using their free Wi-Fi and scanning all the QR codes. Who would have expected the use of filters to make the paintings come alive!

Surrounding the main hall were other smaller houses featuring other art pieces or collections of the artists. I saw several wooden phallic objects, polished white conch shells looking like human skulls, crocodile skins draped over tables and on the floor, cattle skulls hanging off the pillars, and the full skeleton of an elephant underneath a hut.

Artist Thawan used to live in the compound, and you can see what his house looked like in one of the smaller huts.

The signage for the toilets is alarmingly unusual – as expected for this unorthodox place – and might not be suitable for everyone, especially those with young children.

We spent about an hour here, but you could spend plenty exploring the grounds and investigating his collection, wondering what went into the artist’s mind when he created them.

Not all huts are open for walk-throughs. Since the decades-old huts are made of wood, you could smell dampness, mould or poor ventilation in some of them. Those definitely evoke a sense of hauntedness, like the [ruinous English castle](https://www.roowanders.com/kellies-castle/) we visited in Ipoh, Malaysia.

Baan Dam is open every day from 9 AM to 5 PM. Tickets cost THB 80.
### Climb the steps of Wat Huay Pla Kang

Wat Huay Pla Kang, a Buddhist monastery established in 2001, is perched on the top of a hill at the edge of the Chiang Rai district.

The compound may not feature unorthodox coloured temples or houses like those that we’ve seen, but it comprises two stunning Chinese-Lanna style architectures – a 9-storey brilliantly coloured pyramid-shaped pagoda, and a giant seated white Guan Yin, or the Bodhisattva of Compassion. Both structures reminded me of my earlier trip to [Kek Lok Si Temple](https://www.roowanders.com/kek-lok-si-temple/) in [Penang](https://www.roowanders.com/penang/) which also has a pagoda and a giant standing Guan Yin.

Chinese-influenced dragons, not the regular Thai-style nagas, flanked staircases leading to both buildings.

Within the colourful pagoda is a giant wooden Guan Yin and her two disciples. Climb the steps to reach the top where a refreshing view awaits. Each level is adorned with at least one Buddha or Chinese deity image, and the steps get narrower and harder to climb as one ascends.

Likewise, for the seated statue of Guan Yin, you could visit the 25th floor via a ticketed lift for THB 40. As our visit was in the late afternoon, the lift was closed and we didn’t have the fortune to discover the summit.

Sandwiched between the two eye-catching structures is a white traditional Thai Lanna-style temple with a white Buddha image seated within.

Wat Huay Pla Kang is free to visit from 7 AM to 9 PM. However, the lift for the Guan Yin statue closes early.
### Learn the history of opium at the Golden Triangle

The Golden Triangle is as mysterious as the Bermuda Triangle when I first learnt about it as a student some decades ago. Why the shape of a triangle? Why not a circle, a rectangle or a rhombus?

Unlike the mythical Bermuda Triangle in the North Atlantic Ocean, this Golden Triangle in Southeast Asia refers to the confluence of borders between three countries – Thailand, Laos and Myanmar. It is here that the opium trade flourished during the 16th and 17th centuries.

Tribes within this mountainous region have always tended poppy plants for medicinal or ritual purposes. The rise of opium as an international cash crop by colonial powers and demand on its derivatives, such as heroin and morphine, by various warring parties, elevated the Golden Triangle as the largest opium-producing area in the world since the 1950s.

Most of the illegal opium plantations have since ceased to operate or converted to tea plantations, at least in Thailand and Laos.

Visit one of two opium museums while you’re there to learn about the devastating effects of opium and how the poppy plant changed world history. The Hall of Opium, the bigger of the two museums, is open every day except Monday from 8.30 AM to 3.30 PM and costs THB 200. I visited the smaller [House of Opium](https://houseofopium.co/en/visit-us/), open every day from 7 AM to 7 PM for THB 50, as my visit happened to be a Monday.

[Some tours organise boat trips on the Mekong R[boat trips on the Mekong River](https://gyg.me/X6FBeKqU)https://gyg.me/X6FBeKqU) You could also take the boat from Thailand to Laos at Chiang Saen, or do the [2D1N slow boat ride from Chiang Rai to Luang Prabang](https://gyg.me/uO4fVhol).

There’s also a Golden Buddha Park in Sop Ruak, the Thai town at the centre of the triangle.

Across the Mekong River is the Golden Triangle Special Economic Zone of Laos with opulent skyscrapers, and a few garish ones like the lotus-crowned Kings Roman Casino. This place is now [a zone of controversy](https://www.crisisgroup.org/asia/south-east-asia/stepping-south-east-asias-most-conspicuous-criminal-enclave) relating to drugs, endangered wildlife and human trafficking, money laundering and employment scams.
### Enjoy the lovely sights at Choui Fong Tea Plantation

Perched in the mountainous region near Chiang Rai, Choui Fong Tea Plantation has produced quality teas in Thailand for decades.

Situated between the Golden Triangle and Chiang Rai, this is a favourite stop for travellers and tours to break the long journey.

Aside from the beautiful panoramic views of rows of green shrubs, the plantation has a modern cafe with a terrace selling photogenic cakes and drinks. You can also sample freshly brewed tea and buy tea leaves from a gift shop.
### Shop for food and souvenirs at the Night Bazaar

Almost every Southeast Asian town or city I visited has a night market. The same goes for Chiang Rai. It’s smaller than those in Chiang Mai, but still a good place to find street eats, enjoy music or buy souvenirs.
### Spend a day at Singha Park

[Singha Park](https://www.trip.com/t/RNiEV23SSU2) is suitable for families and people of all ages and interests as it has numerous activities – petting zoos, tea plantations, farms, zip lines, hot air balloons, rock walls and glamping.

Formerly a barley plantation for beer production, the acres of land have since been converted towards agritourism a decade ago. Visitors could enjoy a cup of their famed oolong tea while gazing at the graceful swans, tour their sprawling tea plantation, feed their giraffes or enjoy the scent of blooms in their flower gardens. There are bicycles and electric bikes for rent and a ticketed tram for a hassle-free tour around the park. You could camp overnight in the park and enjoy the serenity after dark without the day crowd.

According to their website, the restaurant uses fresh farm produce in their dishes and products like jams. There’s also a coffee shop for local students to work or learn during their school holidays, which doubles as a good spot for nomads looking to get some digital work done.

During certain times of the year, the park hosts music concerts, cycling races and even a hot air balloon festival. How crazy!

[Singha Park](https://www.trip.com/t/RNiEV23SSU2) is about 12 km southwest of Chiang Rai and is open daily from 8 AM to 6 PM.
## Accommodations in Chiang Rai

Accommodations in the heart of Chiang Rai are just [affordable](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.en.html?city=-3247118&aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2).

The most budget-friendly choices are usually homestays or rooms in old apartments. These are excellent choices for a no-frills stay. You can find several located in the centre with very good reviews. There are hostels within walking distance from the bus station too.

I stayed at [Grandma Kaew House](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/grandma-kaew-house.en.html?aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) and found my experience superb. We could walk from the bus station and they have quality motorbikes for rent.

Even luxurious stays don’t cause too much of a dent in one’s wallet, though of course, you could splurge a little more for even better upgrades. Check out the deluxe double room of [Le Patta Hotel](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/le-patta.en.html?aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2). And if you’d like to relax in the hot tub of a hotel somewhere more remote in nature, hop over to the modern and beautiful [Riva Vista Riverfront Resort](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/riva-vista-riverfront-resort-chiangrai.en.html?aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) with spacious rooms facing the river.

Special mention to [Huean Himbo](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/ehuue-nhimb.en.html?aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) with views of a lush rice field. This unique accommodation is near the White Temple. Definitely a place to hammock and relax on the terrace while the rain pelts outside. Simply wonderful!
## Our 2D1N Itinerary

My partner and I are a pair of ambitious travellers who want to maximise our annual leave as corporate workers. Here’s how our overnight Chiang Rai itinerary panned out.
#### Morning Ride from Chiang Mai

We left on the second-earliest bus from Chiang Mai to Chiang Rai booked on [12Go](https://12go.asia/en/?z=8031163).

The four-hour ride was okay – I dozed off several times, there wasn’t much of a view. We might have reached earlier since the driver was so efficient. But he waited out at some random roadside until the time was near.

A Thai or Burmese grandma seated diagonally next to us heading to Mae Sai had motion sickness for the entire trip.
#### Lunch Noms

After checking into our no-frills homestay at [Grandma Kaew House](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/grandma-kaew-house.en.html?aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2), we rented a Honda Click for THB 250/ day and scooted off for a much-needed midday nom.

It was terribly hot in Chiang Rai as we curved around the Clock Tower.

Lunch was at 4-Stars, a family-owned eatery, where we had the ubiquitous pad thai and the rarer hoy tod, or crispy oyster omelette, for THB 180.
#### Temple Run

Post-lunch, we headed to Wat Phra Kaew, one of the first temples recorded to have housed the most sacred Buddha image in Thailand – The Emerald Buddha. I am now satisfied with my almost-completed, coincidental mission to visit all the Emerald Buddha’s old and current haunts since I visited [Vientiane](https://www.roowanders.com/vientiane/) a year earlier.

Then it was a 13 km long and hot motor ride south to Wat Rong Khun, the iconic white temple of Chiang Rai. Finding the side road leading to the temple from the highway wasn’t as easy as expected.

An hour flew by as we explored the compounds of the White Temple, through corridors with hanging well-wishes written on aluminium leaves, into the glaringly bright white-walled temple that has reflective mosaic lined on the edge to create outlines, took funny family shots with the strange mythical lion face-in-hole photo prop, and discovered an enormous golden Ganesha Exhibition Hall. All is free in the compound except the White Temple which costs THB 100.

We took a break at the air-conditioned bliss of Alexta Coffee and shared a matcha roll and a Thai-style iced latte for THB 175. Refreshing! Then it was across Mueang Chiang Rai north to Wat Rong Suea Ten, the Blue Temple that some bloggers/ reviewers like to describe as psychedelic.

Not sure how this unconventional temple can create new psychotic states, or reveal the soul for them. I thought the temple with hues between ultramarine and cobalt blue had plenty of excellent photo spots. There are intriguing statues outside the main temple, and a marble-white giant Buddha image resting within. We happened to catch the contrasting fiery orange sunset from the cool blue temple.

It’s common sense and respect to take off the shoes before entering the religious hall, but not all visitors have both – a self-entitled man in a basketball tank and shorts (horrible attire for a religious visit) glared at me when I reminded him at the top of the stairs. He needed the resident monk to remind him. 🙄
#### Evening Activities

We had a glorious dinner of hung lay, laab and nam prik at Laab Sanaam Keela for THB 413 before the local weekend evening crowd set in.

The last activity we did that day was aimless strolling at Chiang Rai Night Bazaar. In the end, we bought new cotton pants for THB 160 each, and enjoyed coconut ice cream for THB 45.
#### Morning Ride to Golden Triangle

We woke up early to make our way to the Golden Triangle from Chiang Rai. The motorbike road trip reminded me of my previous long bike journey in Cebu to [Simala Parish Church](https://www.roowanders.com/simala-parish-church/), except this one in Thailand happened immediately after sunrise.

It was actually quite cold riding so early in the morning. We were mostly sharing the road with farm workers.

Breakfast was at a random noodles shop in Chiang Saen once we hit the first town by the riverside. Maybe we were very hungry, but their THB 50 pork rice noodles were so rich, umami and heavenly delicious.

We saw glitzy tall modern buildings and a lotus-shaped casino on the Laotian side. We also stopped to browse the golden Buddha images and other religious statues along the way.

I wasn’t sure what to expect when we reached the Golden Triangle. It looks as average as any portion of a Southeast Asian river can be, with brownish water and barren coasts, except this portion is shared between Thailand, Myanmar and Laos. We wondered aloud about the probability of swimming across, and the percentage of illegal crossings.

Without the historical context of the Golden Triangle from the nearby opium museum (THB 50), my mind would have sought meaning for the region and road trip by amusing itself with the jutting river boulder where the borders might have been trisected.
#### Stops from the Triangle to Chiang Rai

As we retraced our route to Chiang Rai, we stopped for iced tea and cakes amidst rows of green tea shrubs at the stunning Choui Fong Tea Plantation. The ride within their compound was hilly and unshaded.

The presentation of their cakes and tea in the airy and spacious eatery with the plantation as backdrop looked Instagram-worthy, but tasted disappointingly average. It cost us THB 380 in total and we spent a good hour resting and people-watching before the crowd occupied the entire place at noon.

Since the Black House or Baan Dam Museum is often advertised as one of the three coloured must-see places in Chiang Rai and happened to be on our way, we dropped by for about an hour. Tickets cost THB 80 and we were impressed, tickled and startled by the artist’s wackiness, his use of Instagram filters on his art pieces, and the abundance of wooden penises and animal carcasses.

Our late lunch was at Khao Soi Pa Yao, which sells the best khao soi I’ve ever eaten in Thailand for THB 80.

Just before returning to Chiang Rai town centre, we turned right to Wat Huay Pla Kang, a Chinese-influenced temple compound. The view from the nine-storey pagoda was alright, but the breeze was welcoming. A giant seated Guan Yin was closed, so we loitered around the base before leaving.
#### Evening Ride to Chiang Mai

We returned the motorbike, bade Grandma Kaew sayonara, and boarded the evening bus to Chiang Mai. Since it was close to sunset by the time the bus left Chiang Rai, I spent most of my time in and out of la-la land until Chiang Mai.

Our accommodation in Chiang Mai was near both bus stops at [The Y Smart Hotel](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/the-y-smart.en.html?aid=7923553&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2), as we planned to [travel to Pai](https://www.roowanders.com/pai/) the following morning.
## Conclusion & Rambles

I enjoyed my road trip in Chiang Rai.

The temples were quirky and a welcoming respite from the traditional Thai temples. They’re like temples doubling as art museums.

I also checked off another temple where the Emerald Buddha used to be located centuries ago. The place feels like a quiet neighbourhood. It is hard to imagine that the Phra Kaew was once here, relative to the newer temple where it now resides, at the bustling [Grand Palace of Bangkok](https://www.roowanders.com/bangkok/).

We also did a remarkable road trip to the Golden Triangle. The towns we passed through have traces of Chinese influence, and the Mekong River was surprisingly clean. Seeing the skyscrapers and the funky casino on the opposite coast was jarring. The other side oozes seediness.

I thought our 2D1N trip was just nice for the key spots. Having additional days would have been superb to explore the other districts of Chiang Rai province in depth.
