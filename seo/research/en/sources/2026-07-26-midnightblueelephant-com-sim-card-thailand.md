### Mobile network providers in Thailand

Thailand has a few different mobile network operators with AIS being the largest one. However, in 2023 the second and third larges, true and dtac, have merged setting out to become the market leader. However, for your SIM card needs it shouldn’t make a difference which one you choose.

All of them have pretty good coverage and they each have their own set of Thailand SIM cards for tourists with pretty similar prices. I tend to choose the one with the shortest line when I arrive at the airport.

Under the name true|dtac they have some combined SIM cards now however, other cards still show individual branding – it is a bit confusing, to be honest. But as I said, don’t worry too much about it.

The only difference is that AIS only has tourist SIM cards with unlimited data while true|dtac offers SIM cards with unlimited data as well as those with a set amount of data.

While you would think that unlimited data is the way to go, most of them have a so-called “fair use policy”. This means you can usually use about 2,5 GB per day at full speed before your data speed gets capped until the next day. This is of course fine for daily use, but if you are planning to use your Thailand SIM card to stream or send a lot of data, it might become an issue.

### Tourist SIM card for Thailand vs. regular prepaid SIM cards

So here is the deal : most network providers want you to go for a Thailand SIM card for tourists. These are readily available at most international airports and also in some stores. Unfortunately, they are more expensive than regular prepaid SIM cards.

Personally, I do not care. I think that data is pretty inexpensive in Thailand and to me, nothing beats the convenience of getting my SIM card at the airport. At many airports, you can even get it next to the luggage claim so you can sort your SIM card while you wait for your suitcase.

However, if you really need to penny-pinch you could always try for a regular prepaid SIM card. But you will need to find a store from a network provider or try your luck at 7-Eleven or such. The problem here: some may not want to sell you, a tourist, a non-tourist SIM card. In addition, you will need to find a store where the staff speaks enough English (or you need to polish up your Thai skills) and carry your passport with you.

To me, that is an added hassle not worth saving a few bucks.

My recommendation: Buy the best SIM card in Thailand for tourists at the cheapest price and use the network provider’s app to top up if you need more data or extend the life of your SIM. I recently did this after buying a data package from DTAC at the airport. The prices on the app are incredibly cheap and you can even pay with your international credit card.

This way, you get the best of both worlds – a convenient albeit slightly more expensive Thailand travel SIM card from the airport and an inexpensive top-up later.
### Where can you buy a SIM card in Thailand?

As mentioned above I usually buy my SIM card at the airport. You will find kiosks of the different operators at international airports in Bangkok, both at Suvarnabhumi and Don Muaeng, in [Phuket](https://www.midnightblueelephant.com/surprise-paradise-the-best-beach-in-phuket/), [Koh Samui](https://www.midnightblueelephant.com/how-to-get-from-phuket-to-koh-samui/), [Chiang Mai](https://www.midnightblueelephant.com/chiang-mai-lantern-festival/), and others. Some have them located directly next to the luggage claim while others have the kiosks outside in the arrival hall – do not fret, it is honestly hard to miss them.

At some airports, they even have self-serve machines now.

If you prefer to save, you will need to find a store like 7-Eleven or Family Mart or go to one of the operator branches. There are also sales points at some BTS and MRT stations in Bangkok. However, they might be limited in the SIM cards they have available.

Important: You will need to have your passport with you in order to register your new Thailand SIM card, it’s the law. Usually, the staff will do all of the registration bits and bobs for you, switch out your SIM card, and make sure that everything works as it should.

Unlike places like Vietnam or India, I have yet to hear about SIM card scams in Thailand but of course, it is a good idea to make sure your new number and data works before you leave the airport/ shop.
### How do you top up a Thailand SIM card?

During my last Thailand trip, I got a dtac SIM card at Phuket Airport with a set amount of data. Once this ran out, I simply downloaded the app while I had wifi and registered my account with my local Thai number. As you can see, buying data on the app is quite a bit cheaper than getting it with your tourist SIM – I only paid 168 baht for 50 GB!

The only small nuisance: you will need to buy a refill first via the app and then choose the data package you want. But honestly, it is just an extra step and is worth the savings. In order to refill, you can use your international credit card on the app.
## eSIM card for Thailand

If you like to be prepared and not have to worry about comparing prices at the airport because you will arrive late or be tired, you can simply choose to buy an eSIM for your Thailand trip.

What is an eSIM? An eSIM is a digital SIM card that you download and activate with a link/ QR code while leaving your SIM card from home in your phone. You are foregoing switching out one physical SIM card with another, simply adding a second digital one.

The advantages: You can buy your eSIM card for Thailand from the comfort of your home and just activate it once you arrive – no waiting in line at an airport kiosk. You will also keep your original SIM card which can be useful if you need to receive a PIN code or do an authentication with your regular number.

Most modern phones are equipped for the use of eSIMs these days, but if you have any doubt you can check the list of models on the providers’ websites.

That said, eSIMs have a couple of disadvantages too which keeps me from using them often. First of all, many are quite pricey compared to buying a local Thai SIM card with data. Secondly, most won’t come with a local phone number – they are purely for data use. That is fine in most cases, but there are instances when a Thai number is super useful i.e. to register for apps like Line or Grab.

The choices for Thailand eSIMs these days are sheer endless. A favorite for many is Holafly, but I find them vastly overpriced.
