# The best hotels in Krabi, Thailand

Where Thailand&apos;s laid-back feel meets some of the best places to stay in the country

[Phuket](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/phuket-hotels) and [Koh Samui](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/best-hotels-koh-samui-thailand) steal much of the limelight when it comes to the [best hotels in Thailand](https://www.cntraveller.com/gallery/hotels-thailand), but the Krabi region – on southern Thailand’s west coast – is home to some of the very best places to stay in the country. It’s a place of dramatic scenery, from the sheer limestone cliffs of famous Railay Beach and the turquoise-hued swimming spots of Koh Phi Phi, to the mangroves and low-key beaches of Koh Lanta – a laid-back island that feels like a throwback to the Thailand of several decades ago. It’s also easy to reach, whether ifrom Krabi airport itself, or skirting across the Andaman Sea from Phuket, with a stop off on Koh Yao Yai along the way. If you’re longing for a slightly quieter take on Thailand – with the option to seek out the buzzier parts, before retreating back to your peaceful hotel – look no further.

### Varana Hotel

When many hotels in coastal Thailand opt for a traditional style, Varana’s slick exterior – with its six steel-grey buildings and breezy balconies – is a refreshing alternative option. In fact, there’s a more modern approach to everything here – this has to be one of the only hotels in Thailand that has its own climbing wall, and the gym, which is kitted out like a trendy commercial workout space, is far better than virtually every other hotel gym in the country (there’s also a spa for repairing muscles afterwards). Health and fitness is at its heart: there are four swimming pools, one being a huge Olympic size pool that's so big that some guests stand-up paddleboard down it, others with jets of water and a swim up bar.

The inside is just as pleasing: interiors are modern and stylish, with rattan chairs and cement-grey and white palettes in the 114 rooms. Dining is inexpensive and health-orientated, with fiery salads and a serious seafood menu. You’ll have to cross a road to reach the beach, but the beach bar, with its swaying rattan lamps and fairy lights, is yet another chill place to hang out here. A fun touch? The sage green tuks tuks, for moving luggage (and guests) between lobbies and rooms.

Price: From around £100 per night

### Tubkaak Krabi Resort

You’ll immediately feel at peace in this boutique stay between Hang Nak Mountain and the Koh Hong archipelago. Everything has been cleverly crafted to ensure that – despite the villas and low-rise buildings around you – you’re still immersed in nature (a forest existed here before, and mature trees were built around instead of cut down). A stream runs from the mountains and along the hotel’s lush grass lawns (which are used daily for yoga practice) and the private villa pools are filled with natural water – chilly, but refreshing and pure.

A small spa has unusual cocoon shaped treatment rooms with impossibly high ceilings and sunlight filtering in from above, or for something more active there are SUPs, or longtail boat trips to the archipelago. A stay here is about slowing down and soaking it in: from a lounger by the pool, or a hammock or bean bag on the beach. Come sunset, the powder-soft sand is lit with twinkling fairy lights and lanterns, making it a romantic spot for crab curries and more, although if that doesn’t take your fancy, the other restaurant’s pizzas are good enough to rival Italy.

Price: From around £150 per night

## Phulay Bay, a Ritz-Carlton Reserve

The smartest stay in the region – so smart that your sprawling villa will come with your very own butler – is undoubtedly Phulay Bay, a Ritz-Carlton Reserve. Despite its location on the Andaman Sea, the hotel doesn’t have a beach that’s suitable for swimming. While that may bother you initially, you’ll soon forget it once you start revelling in the rest of the reserve, which is filled with bougainvillaea, palms and frangipani trees.

Villas are vast and light-filled, with lofty ceilings and Moorish interiors – metal pendant lamps and palettes of cream, deep purple and burgundy. Huge bathrooms come complete with deep, stone, standalone bathtubs and huge brass shower heads, while many villas have private pools and shaded lounging areas. It’s tempting to never leave, but there’s plenty to entice you throughout the rest of the property, from the huge infinity pool which stretches out to one of the most sensational views in the country – the karsts of Phang Nga Bay. The spa is suitably opulent, as is the welcome area, which is lit up with 2,000 candles every night. But the real star here may just be the food. From the faultless breakfast buffet, to elevated takes on Thai cuisine and multi-course tasting menus, every meal somehow becomes more remarkable than the last.

Price: From around £420 per night

Address: [111 Moo 3 Nongthalay, Muang Krabi 81180, Thailand](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Phulay+Bay,+a+Ritz-Carlton+Reserve/@8.0848737,98.7457033,15z/data=!4m2!3m1!1s0x0:0x3080f479f6094379?sa=X&ved=1t:2428&ictx=111)

## Banyan Tree Krabi

You’ll be dazzled from the moment you arrive at Banyan Tree Krabi – the open air lobby has an infinity pool and tumbling water feature. And the views? Well, they’re some of the best in the region. You’ll spend most of your time here hypnotised by limestone karsts of Phang Nga Bay that dot the horizon.

In fact, this is a view you’ll have from every single room: the property has been cleverly built to allow for all villas and suites to look out over the glittering Andaman. Every room type has its own private pool and jet pool, and white walls and bathroom tiles make them feel like little sanctuaries – fitting, given Banyan Tree is a wellness-focused brand. There are plenty of peaceful places to hang out, from the tranquil pool with its huge cabanas and thickly padded loungers, to the long beach, which is one of the best on this stretch of Krabi coastline. The village-like spa goes beyond facials and massages with a hydrotherapy complex of ice fountains, jet pools, sauna and steam rooms that even the most committed of spa goers would be impressed by. Kids, too, are very happy here – there’s a mini waterpark with a slide, jets and sprays, and a small but crammed-with-fun kid's club. The Bird's Nest restaurant – with private tables scattered through the jungle looking out at the sunset – is undeniably romantic, but the best meal may be at Saffron, where Thai cuisine and an elevated position make for a really memorable meal. Don’t miss the khong wang ruam, a selection of appetisers which includes crispy fish rolls and an unforgettable pomelo crispy crab meat salad.

Price: From around £364 per night

## Rayavadee

Thousands of tourists descend on Railay Beach every day, but by staying at Rayavadee, you’ll get to enjoy one of Thailand’s most famous destinations after everyone leaves. In fact, it’s one of three beaches you’ll have access to during your time in this hotel, which is located on the Phranang Peninsula on the edge of Krabi Marine National Park, and surrounded by dramatic scenery of towering cliffs, swaying palms and turquoise sea.

It’s huge – in a good way – with standalone pavilions dotted through the grass and greenery, with families of monkeys scampering through the trees above you, and bouncing off your pavilion roof. The two-story pavilions feel elegant inside – with shiny dark wooden furnishings in the living room and bedroom, and cream bathrooms. Many have standalone pools, but really, you may not need them as there’s so much to do here, from the spa, to the tennis courts and multiple communal pools. Dining here isn’t just a meal but something so much more special, whether you’re one of a handful of people eating in the grotto, a shaded spaced tucked into the limestone cliffs, or watching the sunset over the beach, with bats swooping through the sky at Krua Phranang, the romantic Thai option.

## Pimalai Resort & Spa

Guests return to Pimalai year after year, and after just one day here it's easy to see why – this is a hotel where you’re made to feel immediately at home, with the warmest welcome from all of the staff. Its location, on the south-west coast of the island of Koh Lanta, is another big draw: this feels like Thailand several decades ago, with a low-key, jet-ski free beach with golden sand and gentle waves, while beach bars and cheap Pad Thai can be found nearby.

There are various room types to suit what you’re after, from double bedrooms with breezy balconies to beachside villas and then – the real showstoppers – the hillside villas, which are dotted through the jungly ridge. A fleet of open-air minivans will whisk you around the resort, meaning you’re never more than a few minutes away from the spa, tennis courts or numerous restaurants.

The day starts in the best way: with huge grins from the team and a faultless breakfast buffet, fresh juices and Eggs Benedict, with views. Then you have a choice: lounge by your own pool, or head to one of two others – one high up with ridiculously good sea views, the other more sociable and close to the small kid's club, before sunset cocktails and a relaxed meal with your toes in the sand. There’s more to a stay here than lazing in the hotel: you can venture out on the hotel’s motoryacht to try some of the best snorkelling in the region, or opt for a more peaceful trip to the mangroves of the east coast.

Price: From around £155 per night

Address: [99 Moo 5 Ba Kan Tiang Beach, Ko Lanta District, Krabi 81150, Thailand](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Pimalai+Resort+and+Spa/@7.496316,99.0758139,15z/data=!4m2!3m1!1s0x0:0x50fcca1411fda539?sa=X&ved=1t:2428&ictx=111)
