## Why I Love Phuket

When I moved to Phuket, I had no idea what to expect. However, I instantly fell in love with the island, and although it does get hectic, the pros undoubtedly outweigh the cons.

Here’s what I love the most about Phuket:

There is abundant nature – In Phuket, you’re constantly surrounded by vibrant greenery and never have to go far to enjoy a spectacular view.

The are numerous beautiful beaches – Phuket’s beaches are indeed picture-perfect: white sand and clear, turquoise waters. While the sea is only safe for swimming some of the year, you will NEVER get tired of long beach walks feeling the soft sand beneath your toes.

The sunsets are mind blowing – I don’t know why, but I’ve honestly never seen more beautiful sunsets (and as frequently) as in Phuket.

The cuisine is diverse and delicious – On every street, you’ll find many local seafood restaurants dishing up top-quality Thai food. Plus, as an international destination, you can find EVERY type of cuisine you can imagine. Seriously, I’ve had pizzas here that are as good as in Italy!

Most days are sunny – While the rainy season can be ‘hit or miss’ (see below), if you come during the dry season (November to April) you’re pretty much guaranteed to get beautiful weather.

There are endless day trip options – Phuket has many nearby islands, some of which you can access in just 20 minutes. Along with island hopping, Phuket borders Phang-Na, a province full of waterfalls, lakes, untouched beaches, caves, and bays to explore. Check out this post for [my favorite island hopping day trips from Phuket.](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/island-hopping-tours-day-trips-from-phuket/)

Of course, nowhere is perfect. Here are a few things you may want to bear in mind when visiting Phuket:

High season is EXTREMELY busy – If you don’t like crowds or traffic, I recommend planning your trip any time other than December to February!

Rainy season can be relentless – Technically, the rainy season lasts from May to October. Most years, the monsoons will be on and off, meaning you can have weeks of little or even no rain during this time. However, you cannot predict the weather; sometimes, you’ll get seven days of non-stop downpours.

The sea can be unsafe for swimming in rainy season – Another thing to consider if you plan to come during the rainy season is that the sea may not be safe to swim in all year round. The ocean can get wild during the monsoon months, and there can be weeks with red flags marking the sea as unsafe.

## About Phuket

Phuket is Thailand’s largest island, located in the south of the Andaman Sea. Spanning 576 square kilometers, it takes about 1.5 hours to drive from the south to the north, but going east to the west is much quicker.

Phuket is known for many things, including beautiful beaches, luxury hotels and beach resorts, world-class diving, vibrant night markets, colorful historic shophouses in Phuket Town, stunning temples and the famous hilltop Big Buddha.

What makes Phuket island so popular is how diverse it is, there’s so many accommodation options, [tourist attractions and things to do in Phuket](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/things-to-do-in-phuket-thailand/) so whatever you are into, you will find it on this bustling Thai island!

## Best Beaches and Areas to Stay in Phuket

While Phuket is technically an island, it is very long. All the best beaches and touristy areas are found along the coast, mainly along the island’s west coast. However, each area has a very different vibe so the best area in Phuket for you will depend on your personal needs and preferences.

Therefore, choosing where to stay in Phuket depends on what you desire. For example, you’ll love Patong if you’re a party animal, but if you prefer peace and quiet, Mai Khao will be more suitable for you.

If you dream of a secluded off the beaten track escape then head to Cape Panwa, or Coconut Island.

While most visitors stay on Phuket’s beaches, don’t forget to make time to visit Phuket Town to soak up the unique culture and colorful heritage of the island.

## Best Places to Stay in Phuket: Top Hotels by Area

Still trying to decide which is the best area in Phuket for you? No worries; I’ll explain what to expect from each area in this section and give you my recommendations for the best hotels for all budgets.
### Kata Beach

Kata is conveniently located between the island’s southern tip (Rawai and Nai Harn) and the bustling tourist towns of Karon and Patong and contains two beaches – Kata Beach and Kata Noi Beach. This small beach town is ideal for families and couples looking for plenty of fun activities nearby but without a 24/7 noisy nightlife.

Kata Beach is pretty long, great for long beach walks, sunbathing and swimming. It’s also popular with surfers as you’ll find medium waves (in terms of power and size) here – ideal for learning the sport. There are many surf schools in Kata – you can join a lesson directly on the beach.

Next to the main beach is Kata Noi Beach, voted one of the most beautiful beaches in the world, which offers a smaller and quieter alternative with some amazing luxury beachfront resorts like [The Shore at Katathani](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/the-shore-at-katathani.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) a romantic adults only resort.

From Kata, you can also start the challenging but rewarding uphill hike from Kata town up to the Big Buddha where you’ll get amazing views (and a bit of a work out!)
#### Best Accommodation in Kata:

$$$ Luxe – [The Shore at Katathani](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/the-shore-at-katathani.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) is a 5-star cliffside resort that sits above the shore with private pool villas, incredible views and direct access to the most beautiful beach

$$ Mid – [OZO Phuket](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/ozo-phuket.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) boasts a beachfront location and a large outdoor swimming pool, fitness center and kids club in a peaceful garden setting.

$ Budget – [Hotel Kata Hill Sea View](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/kata-hill-sea-view.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) lives up to its name with incredible ocean views and a beautiful pool, just a 5-minute walk from the beach.
### Karon Beach

Karon is a larger beach town than Kata, just a 5-minute drive north. Karon has one of the best beaches in Phuket – super soft white sand spanning 5 km!

Karon Beach boasts a fab location, as the party resort Patong and the hidden Freedom Beach are nearby which makes it one of the best areas to stay in Phuket for those who want to stay in peace but be close to the action – Karon offers a bit of everything and is more spacious that Kata.

Along with beach lovers, Karon is popular with families, couples, and groups of friends who want to balance beach time, long sunset walks, relaxation and partying.
#### Best Accommodation in Karon:

### Patong Beach

The famous Patong beach is known for its 24/7 nightlife scene, predominantly centered along Bangla Road, a long strip of go-go bars and nightclubs. Therefore, you’d think Patong Beach attracts a particular crowd – groups of young lads looking to party and older men looking to get lucky – but actually you’ll also see plenty of families here too!

As the tourist hub of Phuket, Patong has the biggest choice of everything, from accommodation to dinning, day trip and shopping options. [The Jungceylon Shopping Center](https://www.jungceylon.com/en) is a beautifully designed indoor – outdoor mall – it’s a great place to stock up on souvenirs as well as international brands with international chain restaurants if you’re missing some comfort food from home. The famous Ladyboy extravaganza – [Simon Cabaret Show](https://www.viator.com/tours/Phuket/SIMON-CABARET-SHOW-PHUKET-HOTEL-PICK-UP-AND-DROP-OFF/d349-170728P47?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&campaign=wtsphuket) is also found on the outskirts of Patong.

While Patong doesn’t have the best reputation – it’s not as bad as it seems. Yes, Bangla Road can be a bit loud and seedy at night ( think [Bangkok’s](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/thailand/bangkok/) Khao San Road mixed with Soi Cowboy) so I don’t personally recommend it to families, but it’s not too bad in the day. However, if you’re with a group of friends looking to party, or visiting Phuket for a stag or hen do, then Patong will undoubtedly be the best place to stay in Phuket!

Aside from its nightlife, Patong boasts a long sandy beach with numerous water sports options, the largest Muay Thai boxing stadium that hosts famous fighters, and offers an incredible central location for accessing the rest of the island – all the [Phuket day tours and island hopping trips](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/island-hopping-tours-day-trips-from-phuket/) can be easily arranged from Patong.

And if the crowds get too much, you can find some quieter small bays and cute cliffside cafes and bars just beyond the town, including the gorgeous Paradise Beach, the jungly Freedom Beach, and scenic Kalim Beach just around the corner.
#### Best Accommodation in Patong:

$ Budget – [U Sabai Living Hotel](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/u-sabai-living.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) is one of the many budget hotels in Patong that gives easy access all the action and is just 10 minutes walking distance from the beach.

$ Backpacker – [Lub D Phuket](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/lub-d-phuket-patong.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) sociable and stylish backpacker hostel with dorms, private rooms, pool and a cafe great for meeting people and close to the nightlife.
### Kamala Beach

Kamala is a beautiful beach yet quieter area just north of Patong beach. Despite its proximity, Kamala beach has a totally different vibe. It’s very relaxed, and much more family friendly – the sandy beach is beautiful, and there are some excellent bars and restaurants nearby.

That’s not to say that Kamala is boring – there’s plenty to do here including the famous Cafe Del Mar Beach Club which hosts famous DJs and attractions like [Carnival Magic](https://www.viator.com/tours/Phuket/Carnival-Magic-Entry-Ticket-With-Buffet-Dinner/d349-42771P106?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&campaign=wtsphuket) and Phuket Fantasea. Kamala beach is also just a short drive to popular Patong Beach, Blue Tree Lagoon, and Green Elephant Sanctuary.

Overall, Kamala is a great place to stay for families and couples seeking beautiful beaches and plenty of activities while avoiding the craziness of Patong!
#### Best Accommodation in Kamala:

### Surin Beach

Surin Beach is a small, quiet bay between Kamala and Bang Tao. It’s ideal for those who want to stay in a calm area but still be a short drive from many top beaches, attractions, restaurants, and markets.

While Surin Beach is small, it has a natural, untouched feel as it is lined with trees, it’s home to the exclusive Amanpuri luxury resort, and there are some lovely elevated viewpoints on either side of the bay.
#### Best Accommodation in Surin Beach:

$$ Mid – [Outrigger](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/outrigger-surin-beach-resort.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) is a boutique ocean-facing resort with a modern and stylish feel just a 2-minute stroll from the sand.

### Bang Tao Beach

Bang Tao Beach is one of the longest in Phuket, spanning 6 km. However, this area is truly special because of the beachside Laguna – multiple small lagoons just behind the shoreline.

The Laguna part of Bang Tao has been transformed into a luxurious green oasis full of luxury hotels, peaceful walking trails, golf courses and spa resorts. Outside the laguna is the popular Boat Avenue and Porto de Phuket, a lively open-air lifestyle and shopping center and market.

Towards the south of Bang Tao Beach, you will find many beachfront restaurants, cool beach clubs like Carpe Diem, Catch and Maya, and accommodation options for all budgets, while the north is mostly untouched nature perfect for relaxation in this tropical paradise.

Bang Tao is also close to Khao Phra Thaeo National Park where you can [hike to Ton Sai waterfall](https://www.viator.com/tours/Phuket/4-Hours-Phuket-Jungle-Hiking/d349-91315P3?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&campaign=wtsphuket) and visit [The Gibbon Rehabilitation Project](https://www.gibbonproject.org/). It’s also not far from [Phuket Elephant Nature Reserve](https://www.viator.com/tours/Phuket/Phuket-Elephant-Nature-Reserve-Half-Day-Program/d349-393669P3?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&campaign=wtsphuket), the most ethical elephant sanctuary in Phuket so it’s ideal for nature and wildlife lovers too.

If you’re looking for luxury, then Bang Tao is one of the best areas in Phuket for anyone who prioritizes comfort and pampering, from couples seeking a romantic getaway, to golf, spa and nature lovers, and anyone who enjoys chilling in a trendy beach club. Bang Tao has something for everyone and does it with style!

### Rawai and Nai Harn Beach

Rawai Beach and Nai Harn Beach are two adjacent areas in the southern tip of Phuket, popular with the younger generations, from digital nomads to fitness fanatics and anyone joining muay thai training camps.

Rawai and Nai Harn are the most “up-and-coming” areas of Phuket, with new coffee shops, restaurants, villas and condo complexes popping up weekly. Rawai Beach is full of longtail boats so it’s not ideal for swimming but offers great island hopping opportunities and an amazing local seafood market, while nearby Naiharn beach is one of the best beaches in Phuket.

There are also many hidden bays and excellent nature spots, including the stunning windmill viewpoint, and the breathtaking Promthep Cape. Even though these southern beach towns are far from the rest of the island, there is always plenty to do and explore here!

### Mai Khao Beach

At the other end of the island is Mai Khao Beach, Phuket’s northernmost beach stretching 7 miles. Mai Khao is very close to the airport, offering convenience for jet-lagged travelers and great plane spotting opportunities, and Sirinath National Park.

Despite being so close to the airport, Mai Khao beach is quiet undeveloped and usually pretty peaceful. The long beach offers just a smattering of luxury beachfront resorts, so it’s popular with those looking for pampering and relaxation, as well as golfers as it is close to the well-known Blue Canyon Country Club.

Mai Khao Beach is the best area in Phuket for introverts and tired travellers who seek almost deserted beaches, peaceful nature, and early nights. As most of the accommodation here is close to the beach and far from Phuket’s crazy traffic and nightlife, Mai Khao offers a very tranquil vibe – even though it’s so close to the airport – it’s quite the hidden gem!

### Phuket Town

If you’re not fussed about being right on the beach and want to learn about Phuket’s history and culture, adore beautiful architecture, craft coffee, and cute boutique stores, then staying in Phuket Town is for you!

Phuket Town is on the island’s east side, far from the tourist beach towns but still close enough to visit for a beach day. The historic part is known as [Phuket Old Town](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/old-phuket-town-guide/), the streets here are lined with colorful 19th-century shophouses and stunning Sino-Portuguese buildings turned into quirky cafes and boutique hotels.

There’s also a really interesting cultural side to Phuket Town and you’ll find a variety of colourful temples and shrines here, from Thai temples to Chinese shrines and much more – it’s a fascinating place to wander and explore – keep an eye out for the street art too!

Phuket Old Town is one of the best areas to stay in Phuket if you want to experience authentic Phuketarian culture and integrate with the locals. However, the area still has a modern feel, as it’s full of Western-style cafes and restaurants you can enjoy and take a break from the heat.

The area becomes even more vibrant every weekend when the Sunday walking street market takes place. And if you find the beach calling you, you’ll be close to Cape Panwa and Ko Sire on the east coast which both have pristine and uncrowded beaches.

Staying in Phuket Town is also super convenient for Rassada Pier, where ferries and boats travel all over the Andaman sea, and is also one of the cheapest places to stay in Phuket!

$ Budget – [Blue Carina Inn](https://www.booking.com/hotel/th/blue-carina-inn.en.html?aid=855319&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2) offers value, convenience, and clean rooms, some of which have private balconies overlooking the outdoor swimming pool.
## Tips for Choosing Your Phuket Accommodation

There’s no shortage of choice when it comes to hotels in Phuket so the best place to stay really comes down to your needs, personal preference and budget.

Here’s some things to consider when choosing your Phuket accommodation:

Consider how close to the beach you want to be. Beachfront hotels are generally more expensive, but the additional expense is worth it if you dream of waking up and stepping onto the sand!

Check the rating and reviews of your accommodation before booking. I recommend choosing hotels with a rating of 8 or above on [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/index.html?aid=855319).

Enquire with your accommodation to see if they organize airport pickup, as this will significantly reduce travel stress!

Seek out local restaurants. Most restaurants near the beach are aimed at tourists and, thus, have more of an international menu. If you want to taste authentic Thai food (and can handle the spice), I recommend choosing restaurants on the back streets where you see the locals dining.
### How to Get to Phuket

Having Thailand’s second busiest airport, most travelers can fly directly into Phuket International Airport. 47 airlines operate from Phuket airport, including Emirates, Etihad, and Virgin Australia.

Most nationalities can get a 60 day visa exemption or visa on arrival which makes entering Thailand and visiting Phuket easy and convenient. You can extend your stay by another 30 days by visiting the immigration office in Phuket.

Even if you’re coming from other parts of Thailand, air travel is the quickest and easiest way to get to Phuket. There are various buses services, but note that Phuket is approximately a 12-hour drive from Bangkok or a 1-hour flight away. You can also get a ferry from Krabi to Phuket. The best place to book domestic flights, trains, buses and ferries is on [12Go Asia](https://12go.asia/?z=293974).

Once you land in Phuket, you’ll need to navigate your way to your accommodation. Some resorts and hotels offer pre-arranged airport transfers and you can also book an [airport transfer on Viator](https://www.viator.com/tours/Phuket/Phuket-Airport-Transfer-Service/d349-167656P8?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&medium_version=selector&campaign=wtsphuket). Otherwise, you can take a taxi, hop on a public bus, or hail a ride through an app like Grab.

However, many taxi drivers at the airport prey on unsuspecting tourists and significantly overcharge. Because of this, some tourists opt to book a ride through the Grab app.
### How to Get Around Phuket

Taxes are the most expensive choice when it comes to getting around the island. If you do use them I recommend asking your hotel reception to call you one – that way, you’re less likely to be overcharged.

Grab and other ride-hailing apps like Bolt offer a more convenient and cheaper option to taxis.

You will also see lots of tuk-tuks around the touristy areas. They are costly, so they are not ideal for long distances. Still, many tourists like to use them for short rides for the experience.

If you want to visit lots of attractions consider a guided tour or day trip as it will be more convenient and better value than taking lots of taxis. I prefer to book these on [Viator](https://www.viator.com/Phuket/d349-ttd?pid=P00074732&mcid=42383&medium=link&medium_version=selector&campaign=wtsphuket) and [Get Your Guide](https://www.getyourguide.com/phuket-l32123/?partner_id=CE8KE83&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=wtsphuket) as I can read reviews so I know I’m going to get a good experience.

For a lower-cost way to get around, you can consider the [Smart Bus](https://phuketsmartbus.com/), which runs from Rawai to the airport, stopping at all the major beach towns. A single ride is 100 THB, but you can buy day or multi-day passes for a very reasonable price.

Finally, some tourists prefer to hire a scooter while in Phuket for the ultimate freedom and flexibility. However, as Phuket’s roads are busy and can be dangerous, I only recommend this if you have experience with scooters and have driven in Thailand before. Ensure you have an international driving license and your travel insurance covers driving motorcycles, too.

### Best Time to Visit Phuket

Weatherwise, the best time to visit Phuket is December to March. You can expect consistently good weather during these months – blue skies and abundant sunshine. However, they are also the busiest months, so while the weather is wonderful, the island is crowded.

November can be a lovely month to visit as it is relatively quiet. Still, as this is the end of the rainy season, the weather can be unpredictable.

Alternatively, consider April and May (the other shoulder season) if you don’t mind the heat. The island is not too crowded by this time, but it can be scorching, ranging from 27°C to 36°C.

Phuket’s rainy season is between June and October with frequent, but often short, downpours, cloudy and overcast skies and unpredictable weather.

Rough seas mean that it is sometimes not safe to swim during this time but you will get considerable discounts on accommodation and experience less crowds if you visit during the rainy reason.
### How Long to Stay in Phuket

Whilst Phuket International Airport makes it possible to visit Phuket for a short weekend trip there’s much more to see and do here. I’d say anywhere from 5 to 14 days is an ideal length to stay in Phuket.

Spending 1 week or 7 days in Phuket will give you enough time to enjoy several different beaches, see the top sights and squeeze in a couple of island hopping day trips – check out this [7 day Phuket itinerary](https://www.global-gallivanting.com/phuket-itinerary/) for inspiration on how to see Phuket, Krabi and Koh Phi Phi in just 1 week.

However, if you’re not in a rush, I recommend staying for 10 to 14 days. This extra time will allow you to check out more parts of the island, take some boat trips, and explore at a more relaxed place.
