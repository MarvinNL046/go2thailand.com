## Bangkok Night Market Culture

The Ratcahada Train Night Market, one of the most iconic night markets in Bangkok

In Thailand, street food and night markets go hand in hand. You can expect to find typical dishes like grilled pork skewers or the classic [Pad Krapow](https://www.feastographyblog.com/blog/what-is-pad-krapow) but more often than not, especially in Bangkok, the night markets here are an outlet for vendors to create new deliciousness.

A lot of these markets also have spaces where you can sit down and enjoy your food, plus some of them even have stalls selling alcohol and cocktails if you want to relax the night away. If you’re looking for classier way to spend your night with alcohol then check out my recommendations for the [best rooftop bars in Bangkok](https://www.feastographyblog.com/blog/rooftop-bars-in-bangkok) ( [Pastel](https://www.pastelbangkok.com/) is on of my favorites).

Weekend Night Markets are even a bigger affair, popular markets like Chatuchak will often host larger numbers of stalls and vendors during the weekends.

Many temples throughout Thailand also have Temple fairs such as the [Phra Pathom Chedi Annual Festival](https://www.feastographyblog.com/blog/phra-pathom-chedi-festival-at-nakhon-pathom), and the [Wat Saket Songkran Festival](https://www.feastographyblog.com/blog/wat-saket-golden-mount-temple) which celebrates both the traditions of the temples as well as hosting big open air markets and often carnival rides and games for locals to entertain and enjoy themselves,
