From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wat Plai Laem ( [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language) : วัดปลายแหลม ) is a [wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat) on the resort island of [Ko Samui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Samui), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand). Like the nearby [Wat Phra Yai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_Phra_Yai) or "Big Buddha Temple", it is a modern [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple). The temple's design incorporates elements of Chinese and Thai traditions and was in part designed by distinguished [Thai artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_art) Jarit Phumdonming. Its main statue, which is in Chinese style, is not of [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha). It is a form of the [bodhisattva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva) of compassion and mercy, [Avalokiteśvara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalokite%C5%9Bvara), called [Cundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cundi_(Buddhism)). This form of the deity is known in Chinese as " [Guanyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanyin) with eighteen arms".

In addition to the main statue, there is also a white statue of [Budai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budai) and smaller shrines dedicated to [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha), [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu), [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) and [Sakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Aakra_(Buddhism)). [ 1 ] [ 2 ]

Although it is an open-air image, it is located within an elaborate [ubosot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubosot) on a platform-pavilion surrounded by a temple lake.
