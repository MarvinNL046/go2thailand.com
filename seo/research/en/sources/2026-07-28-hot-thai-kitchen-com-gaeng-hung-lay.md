# Gaeng Hung Lay - Northern Thai Braised Pork Curry

Gaeng Hung Lay แกงฮังเล is one of the tastiest dishes of Northern Thailand! Pork belly and pork ribs are slowly braised in an aromatic mix of herbs and spices. The result is melt-in-your-mouth pork that has absorbed so much flavour from the rich curry sauce. It is a dish to impress, and though it takes time, it is very easy and gluten-free!

## Cultural Context

Gaeng hung lay is believed to have come to Northern Thailand via Myanmar, and in fact the word "hung lay" comes from the Burmese language. Burmese cuisine has a lot of Indian influences, which is why this recipe uses many more dry spices than a typical Thai dish. It is also why some people use Indian garam masala instead of making hunglay curry powder.

In Thailand, you can find gaeng hung lay almost exclusively in northern Thailand or in restaurants that specialize in northern cuisine. But it's a must try if you ever see one!

## Ingredients and Important Notes

I hope this section answers all your questions about ingredients, some of which may be unfamiliar. For amounts, check out the full recipe card below.

#### The Curry

Pork belly, cut into 1.5-inch chunks. I prefer skin-on pork belly because I like the texture of the cooked skin, but skinless also works. If you prefer a less-fatty dish, you can use pork shoulder cut into 1-inch cubes

Asian-cut pork ribs. These pork ribs are cut across the bones into about 1-inch pieces. Sometimes they are sold in long strips, so you'll need to separate them into individual bones, other times they are sold already cut. They are common at Asian butchers and are available as both spare ribs and back ribs; either can be used. If not available, you can sub pork shoulder cut into 1-inch cubes.

[Tamarind paste,](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tamarind-everything-you-need-to-know/) look for tamarind in a jar that is from Thailand (sometimes labeled as tamarind concentrate). It should be brown in colour and quite loose. You can also make it from tamarind pulp quite easily; [see my homemade tamarind paste recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/making-tamarind-paste/).

Dark brown sugar, or you can sub light brown or chopped palm sugar.

Thai black soy sauce or dark soy sauce (optional) this is added to intensify the colour and makes for a richer flavour, but it is okay to skip it. Omitting this will also make this dish gluten free.

[Fish sauce](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)

Garlic, peeled, but keep cloves whole. One unique point of hung lay curry are tender-braised whole cloves of garlic. It's also okay to omit this if you don't like to eat it.

Pearl onions or Thai small shallots (optional).

Julienned ginger

#### Hung Lay Curry Curry Paste

Tip: No time to make the curry paste? "Cheat" by combining 3-4 tablespoon store bought red curry paste with 2 tablespoon hung lay curry powder or garam masala.

Dried mild red chilies. I use dried guajillo peppers for colour because they're mild, and then add a few dried arbol or Thai chilies to adjust the heat. In Thailand, the mild dried chilies you're looking for is spur chilies or "prik chee fa."

Garlic, chopped

Shallots, chopped

Lemongrass, bottom half only, finely chopped

[Galangal](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/galangal-101/), finely chopped

Fresh turmeric, sliced, or you can use powdered turmeric

Hung lay curry powder, recipe below. You can also substitute garam masala if you already have it.

Fermented shrimp paste (gapi). This is a purplish brown funky-smelling paste that will add a lot of depth and umami to the curry. Don't skip it if you can find it!

#### Hung Lay Curry Powder

Whole spices are always preferred as the flavours are more aromatic when freshly ground, but if you don't have it, ground version can be substituted.

Cinnamon stick or ground cinnamon

Black peppercorns

Green or white cardamom (optional) these can be found a Chinese grocery stores.

Cumin seeds

Coriander seeds, this is the one spice I ask you use only whole seeds because in my experience ground coriander has very weak aroma.

Star anise

Fennel seeds

## Process Overview

Here's a bird's eye view of all the steps. For full instructions, see the recipe card below, and if this is your first time, I highly recommend watching the video tutorial to ensure success.

Grind the dried chilies into a powder in a coffee/spice grinder.

Make hung lay curry powder by grinding all the spices together in a coffee grinder. If you're using any whole spices, consider toasting them first to enhance the aroma.

If using an immersion blender, combine lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, shallots, garlic and shrimp paste in a narrow container and blend into a paste. \*See below for a manual method.

Add the dried chilies and the hung lay curry powder and blend just until well combined.

- If using a mortar and pestle: Pound the lemongrass, galangal and turmeric into a fine paste. Add the shallots and garlic and pound until fine. Then add the shrimp paste and ground dry spices and pound to mix.

Heat a large wok or pot until hot, then add the pork belly, skin facing sideways, and sear until browned and fat is rendered. Flip and sear the other side. \*If using a leaner cut of pork you'll need to add a little oil to the pan.

Add the ribs and give everything a quick toss.

Add the curry paste.

Toss the curry paste with the pork and let it fry in the rendered pork fat for 2-3 minutes.

Add water just until it barely covers the pork. Add fish sauce, black soy sauce, tamarind, sugar, and simmer gently for 1 hour 45 mins to 2 hours, loosely covered, until the pork is fork tender.

Towards the last 20 minutes, check the amount of liquid, and if it's very soupy, uncover the pot completely to allow liquid to evaporate. You want the sauce to be thick and rich in the end.

Once pork is tender add whole garlic, pearl onions, and julienned ginger. Simmer for another 15-20 minutes until the garlic is tender. You can add a little more water at any point if it gets to dry.

Taste and adjust seasoning and serve with rice, garnish with extra ginger if desired.
