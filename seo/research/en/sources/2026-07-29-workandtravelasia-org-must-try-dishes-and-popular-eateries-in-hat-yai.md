## Must-Try Dishes in Hat Yai

Hat Yai Fried Chicken (Gai Tod Hat Yai)

If there’s one dish that Hat Yai is famous for, it’s the legendary Hat Yai Fried Chicken.

Imagine biting into crispy, golden-brown skin that gives way to tender, flavorful meat—it’s no wonder this dish has a cult following!

Typically served with sticky rice and a sweet and tangy dipping sauce, Hat Yai Fried Chicken is a street food staple that you simply can’t miss.

Kai-Tod Decha (Bangloh) Chee Uthit Branch : Known for its perfectly fried chicken, which boasts a unique Southern Thai flavor profile. The chicken is marinated in a blend of local spices, resulting in a crispy, golden-brown skin and succulent meat.

## Classic and Nostalgic Restaurants

For those who prefer dining in a more traditional setting, Hat Yai has several classic restaurants that have stood the test of time.

These eateries offer a nostalgic glimpse into the city’s culinary past while serving up timeless dishes.

Dao Thiam

Dao Thiam is a classic breakfast restaurant in Hat Yai, steeped in over 65 years of history.

The nostalgic atmosphere is enhanced by walls adorned with banknotes from around the world and the melodies of old songs from the 70s and 80s.

The signature ‘ Guay Tiew Lod ‘ (rolled rice noodles) and pork toast with Ajad (pickled vegetables) are a must-try.

Complement your meal with ‘Cha Puchino’ (milk tea) or freshly brewed coffee made cup by cup.

The warm welcome and attention to detail makes dining here a delightful and memorable experience.

Ko Chon Rad Na

Ko Chon Rad Na has been a beloved spot for generations, known for its delicious Rad Na (stir-fried noodles with gravy).

The noodles are stir-fried to perfection and served with a rich, savory gravy, accompanied by a choice of meats or seafood.

The recipe has been perfected over the years, ensuring each dish is packed with flavor. The casual setting and friendly service make it a popular choice among locals.

Gotee Ocha

Gotee Ocha is an iconic Chinese restaurant in Hat Yai, celebrated for its Bak Kut Teh and Hainanese chicken rice.

The Bak Kut Teh, simmered in a wood stove, blends Singaporean soup recipes with pepper, angelica root, and garlic, adjusted for Malaysian tastes with added herbal medicines, creating a mellow, flavorful broth served in clay pots.

The dry Bak Kut Teh is a standout dish.

The Hainanese chicken rice features juicy, sesame oil-fragrant meat, a recipe passed down from the owner’s mother who learned it in Hong Kong.

The restaurant’s delicious food and attentive service ensure a memorable dining experience.

Bankampu Hatyai is a beloved destination for seafood enthusiasts.

This restaurant prides itself on serving only the freshest catches, which are expertly prepared to highlight their natural flavors.

Signature dishes include steamed crab, stir-fried shrimp with garlic, and whole fried fish with spicy Thai herbs.

The focus on quality and freshness ensures a memorable dining experience for those who love seafood. The ambiance is casual and inviting, making it a great spot for both family dinners and casual outings with friends.

Kanomjeen Pa Chuen is renowned for its authentic Kanom Jeen, a traditional Thai rice noodle dish served with various curries and fresh vegetables.

Siri Tom Lued Moo is a must-visit for pork lovers.

Mun Duey Tua

Mun Duey Tua is a legendary dessert spot located in front of the Hat Yai train station.

This cherished establishment has been serving delightful treats for over 60 years.

The signature dessert is a symphony of flavors: a sweet and creamy mixture featuring candied yam, mashed red beans, and Job’s tears, all drenched in rich, fresh coconut milk and served in a large glass.

The dessert is topped with crushed ice and slightly tart candied pineapple, offering a perfect balance of sweetness and freshness.

You can customize the sweetness to your liking, making it a personalized treat that’s loved by both locals and visitors from neighboring countries.
