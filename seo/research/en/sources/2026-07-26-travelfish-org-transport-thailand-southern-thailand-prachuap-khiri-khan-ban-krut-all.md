Set a little to the north of Bang Saphan Yai, Ban Krut is easily approached by train or bus, with the former being our recommended way to arrive. Once you’re settled in, renting a bicycle or scooter—or just using your legs—is the best way to explore the beachfront and hinterland.

## Ban Krut Railway Station, train advice and timetables

Sleepy seaside Ban Krut is best reached—and left—by train, with its train station ( [Google map](https://maps.app.goo.gl/w6Fvrb3NxFV2PTRv5) ) set around a one kilometre or 20 minute walk to the west of the beachside strip. Unlike some “coastal” stations, the walk from the station to the beach is quite pleasant—as long as, speaking from experience, it is not undertaken in torrential rain or blazing heat.

Not all express trains on the north-south train line stop at Ban Krut, but enough do to make it straightfroward to select a train with fairly convenient timing. There’s also one ordinary train that works well for short hops—or a budget blast to or from Bangkok. Depending on the train taken, Ban Krut is five to six hours from Bangkok, two and a half to three and a half hours from [Phetchaburi](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/southern_thailand/phetchaburi/phetchaburi), about two hours from [Hua Hin](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/southern_thailand/prachuap_khiri_khan/hua_hin), and under an hour from [Prachuap Khiri Khan](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/southern_thailand/prachuap_khiri_khan/prachuap_khiri_khan).

Heading southbound by train from Ban Krut, it’s about an hour and a half to [Chumphon](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/southern_thailand/chumphon/chumphon), five to six hours to [Surat Thani](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/southern_thailand/surat_thani/surat_thani), and nine to ten hours to [Hat Yai](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/southern_thailand/songkhla/hat_yai).
### Southbound trains (Bangkok → Ban Krut → South)

At five to six hours from Bangkok, Ban Krut isn’t quite there on the sleeper scale, and if you do go for a sleeper train from Bangkok to Ban Krut, you’re going to be alighting around 3am in the morning—not ideal. Instead we recommend taking a day train from Bangkok and so on to Ban Krut, but do consider a sleeper berth if you’re taking a longer haul train southbound from Ban Krut.

#### Bangkok to Ban Krut

From Bangkok’s Krungthep Apiwat Central Station heading south, the 07:30 departing Special Express #43 arrives in Ban Krut at just after midday and is the pick of the bunch of the long distance express day trains. You could go for the afternoon Rapid #171 which leaves Bangkok at 15:10 and arrives at 20:50, but we reckon the earlier train is the better bet.

If you’d prefer a budget train between Bangkok and Ban Krut, the Ordinary #255 leaves Bangkok Thonburi at 07:20 and arrives at Ban Krut at 14:33—yes the trip is a couple of hours longer than the express option, but it costs a fraction of the price. Do note that ordinary trains cannot be booked online—just buy your ticket at the train station. Both the express and ordinary stations stop at many of the key stations between Ban Krut and Bangkok including Prachuap Khiri Khan, Hua Hin, Phetchaburi, and [Ratchaburi](https://www.travelfish.org/location/thailand/bangkok_and_surrounds/ratchaburi/ratchaburi).

#### From Ban Krut to southern Thailand

Southbound from Ban Krut, for nearby destinations such as Chumphon and Surat Thani, aim for the Special Express #43 which leaves at 12:25 and arrives at 14:05 and 16:20 respectively. For Sungai Kolok on the Thai Malaysian border, the Rapid #171 leaves at 20:50 and arrives at 10:10, while for those headed to Trang, the Rapid #167 leaves at the ungodly 02:36 and arrives in Trang at 11:05. Please note the Bangkok to Padang Besar Express #45 does not stop at Ban Krut, but it does pick up at Prachuap Khiri Khan (a little under an hour to the north) at 21:05, meaning you could feasibly have dinner in Ban Krut then head up to Prachuap Khiri Khan to grab the train south.

If you just want to get a train from Ban Krut to Chumphon, the Ordinary #255 leaves Ban Krut at 14:33 and arrives in Chumphon at 16:50, leaving you plenty of time to wash off the grit before hitting Chumphon’s night market.

### Northbound trains (South → Ban Krut → Bangkok)

Closer destinations such as Surat Thani, Chaiya, and Chumphon are too close to Ban Krut to make a sleeper train worthwhile, but once you’re looking at Trang or Hat Yai, then a night train is the way to go. Remember, if you’re travelling in high season, it is essential to book sleeper trains in advance.

Top of page
#### From southern Thailand to Ban Krut

As mentioned, from regionally southern destinations like Surat Thani and Chumphon, catching a sleeper train to Ban Krut isn’t going to work. Instead, go for either the Special Express #40 (leaves Surat Thani at 09:00, Chumphon at 11:18, and arrives at Ban Krut at 12:42), or if you’re coming from Chumphon, the Ordinary #254 which leaves at 07:50 and arrives at 9:57 also works.

From further south, if you’re coming from Hat Yai, get the Rapid #170 which leaves Hat Yai at 18:20 and arrives in Ban Krut at 04:20—early, but could be worse! From Trang, your only option is the Rapid #168 which leaves Trang at 14:45 and arrives in Ban Krut at 23:05—this train has plenty of second class air-con seats, so there’s no need for a sleeper.

#### Ban Krut to Bangkok

Heading towards Bangkok from Ban Krut, the Special Express #40 leaves Ban Krut at 12:42 and arrives in Bangkok at 18:05, while if you prefer a night train, the Rapid #168 leaves at 23:05 and arrives at 05:10. If you want a sleeping berth on the latter train, be sure to book it in advance.

For an ordinary train, the Ordinary #254 leaves Ban Krut at 09:57 and arrives at Bangkok Thonburi at 17:25. This is quite a long haul on a third class train, but if you’re trying to keep your spending down, this is the way to go. Note ordinary trains cannot be booked online—you just buy the ticket at the train station.

## Ban Krut bus services

Unless you arrive on a direct bus to Ban Krut, you’re most likely to be dropped on the superhighway, from where it’s around ten kilometres into town—you’ll need to get an expensive motorbike taxi or hitch into Ban Krut itself.
