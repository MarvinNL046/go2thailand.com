## Ownership boundary

`/food/oh-aew/` owns Phuket's Oh Aew / O-Aew dessert: its local identity, translucent fig-seed jelly, shaved ice, syrup, familiar red-bean, black-grass-jelly and fruit choices, taste and texture, ordering choices and dietary checks.

The owner may explain that Oh Aew belongs to a wider family of fig-seed jellies and compare it carefully with Taiwanese aiyu. It must not claim that every Phuket formula uses the same botanical variety, processing method or additives. Grass jelly remains a separate jelly that can be served alongside Oh Aew. Phuket Old Town restaurant routing and general Phuket planning remain with their destination owners. This is a traveller and ordering guide, not an independently tested recipe; Recipe schema is prohibited.

It establishes that aiyu extract is obtained by washing *Ficus pumila* var. *awkeotsang* seeds and that low-methoxyl pectin and divalent-ion crosslinks create the gel. It supports a related gel mechanism, not a claim that all Phuket Oh Aew is botanically or procedurally identical.
- Taiwan Ministry of Agriculture, current Aiyu feature: independent primary context that aiyu jelly is produced by washing/rubbing aiyu fig seeds in water. This is used only for the comparison boundary.
- UNESCO Creative Cities Phuket material: primary civic context for Phuket's intergenerational Hokkien-Chinese gastronomic heritage; it does not prove a specific Oh Aew formula.

## Fact, dietary and safety boundaries

- The stable traveller-facing identity is translucent Oh Aew jelly served cold with shaved ice and syrup. The jelly, syrup and topping proportions vary by shop.
- Red beans, black grass jelly, watermelon and other fruit are documented options, not mandatory ingredients.
- `White`, `black` and `red` can refer to Oh Aew, grass jelly and red beans in a documented local ordering shorthand; a traveller should still point and confirm because menus vary.
- Oh Aew and grass jelly are not the same jelly. Serving both in one bowl does not merge their ingredients or dietary properties.
- Oh Aew is related conceptually to aiyu through fig-seed pectin gelation, but the page must say `related` rather than `identical` and keep Phuket's serving identity distinct.
- The jelly itself may appear plant-based, but condensed milk, dairy powders, honey, coloured syrup, packaged toppings, setting agents and shared utensils can change vegan, vegetarian, halal and allergen suitability.
- A translucent appearance does not certify gluten-free status. Check packaged toppings, flavour powders, syrups, cross-contact and preparation surfaces when medically necessary.
- Shaved ice and dilution water are direct hygiene checks. Choose a clean, busy stall using protected ice and toppings held cold; visible cleanliness is not a medical guarantee.
- Never publish a fixed price, calorie total, hydration/heat-treatment claim, health verdict, automatic vegan/gluten-free/halal status, permanent restaurant ranking, exact origin date or one universal formula.
