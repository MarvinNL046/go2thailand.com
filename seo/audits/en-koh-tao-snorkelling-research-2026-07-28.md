# Koh Tao snorkelling — English research brief

**Proposed owner:** `/islands/koh-tao/snorkeling/`
**Market:** United Kingdom / English
**Captured:** 28 July 2026

## Evidence collected

- The primary `snorkeling koh tao` DFS cluster returned 72 keyword records and 50 competitor domains. A separate UK-spelling `snorkelling koh tao` validation cluster returned nine records and confirmed the same intent.
- Eleven current UK-English SERPs returned 87 organic appearances and 54 People Also Ask appearances across 35 case-normalised distinct questions.
- Six usable complete parses cover two ranking editorial competitors, two Visit Koh Tao destination pages, Tourism Authority of Thailand and a current local tour operator. A third ranking competitor returned no DFS markdown and is retained only as SERP evidence.
- The exact route has zero ranking keywords and no returned backlink or referring-domain signal.

## Search intent

The durable owner should help a traveller choose a suitable bay or boat day for the current conditions. It is not a permanent “best beaches” ranking and should not guarantee turtles, sharks, visibility or public access.

DFS supports `koh tao snorkeling` (140 UK volume), the main `snorkeling in koh tao` and tour family (50), British `snorkelling koh tao` (30), Shark Bay variants (20), and smaller spot, beach, turtle, map, price, private-tour and neighbouring-island intents. The spelling variants belong to one canonical English owner.

## Competitor pattern

Common coverage:

- ranked lists of Shark Bay, Ao Leuk, Sai Daeng, Tanote, Japanese Gardens and other named bays;
- self-guided shore snorkelling versus around-island tours;
- turtles, blacktip reef sharks and occasional whale-shark references;
- Koh Nang Yuan as a separate boat or tour stop;
- best-season, gear and price sections;
- personal-experience claims and operator recommendations.

Weaknesses the Go2Thailand owner should solve:

- one permanent ranking ignores wind direction, wave exposure, visibility, current, boat traffic and the exit route;
- “free” or “from the beach” is presented without verifying current land access, resort conditions or a contribution;
- wildlife is used as a product promise rather than an unguaranteed encounter;
- more tour stops are treated as automatically better without working group, actual water time, equipment fit or weather rerouting;
- beginner language rarely distinguishes a calm-looking surface from a safe entry and manageable return;
- fixed prices and access details age quickly;
- generic island comparisons and diving intent are allowed to dilute the snorkelling decision.

## Information-gain plan

- Make the morning decision start with the sheltered island side, not the most famous bay.
- Turn wind, wave/current, visibility and access/return into four explicit condition signals.
- Compare six bay profiles through island side, access, traveller fit, suitable conditions and a visible caveat rather than one universal ranking.
- Separate one self-paced shore session from an around-island boat or private longtail day by consequences, not price alone.
- Ask tour operators about the real route, group and guide coverage, flotation options, equipment sizes, actual water time, early-stop procedure and wildlife policy.
- Make reef fish and coral the reliable purpose; turtles and blacktip reef sharks remain possible but never guaranteed. Do not frame whale sharks as a snorkelling expectation.
- Keep access, contribution, reserve rules and boat routes dynamic and point readers to the relevant first-party source or operator before departure.
- Use Klook only to compare current tour availability after the route and safety decision.
- Retain natural Amazon OneLink exits in both languages for a drybag, water shoes when the specific entry warrants them, and a quick-dry towel. Do not force a snorkel set, sunscreen or unrelated gear purchase.
