# Diving in Koh Tao — English research brief

**Proposed owner:** `/islands/koh-tao/diving/`
**Market:** United Kingdom / English
**Captured:** 28 July 2026

## Evidence collected

- The primary DFS cluster returned 220 keyword records and 50 competitor domains; the `scuba diving koh tao` validation cluster returned another 63 records and 50 competitor domains.
- Eleven current UK-English SERPs returned 92 organic appearances and 40 People Also Ask appearances across 22 case-normalised distinct questions.
- Six complete source parses cover PADI (about 830 parsed words), Big Blue Diving (about 2,500), Scuba Shack (about 750), Crystal Dive (about 2,400), Midnight Blue Elephant (about 3,200) and Stingy Nomads (about 4,600).
- The exact route has zero ranking keywords and no returned backlink or referring-domain signal.

## Search intent

The durable owner must help a traveller choose the right dive experience and evaluate the actual operator, instructor and schedule. It should not become an unverified league table of dive schools or a price list that becomes stale.

DFS supports the primary `diving koh tao` family at 880 UK searches, course and school queries around 210, PADI variants around 90 and smaller price, beginner, certification and dive-site questions. Current SERPs mix destination inspiration, course sales, operator comparison, dive-site lists and practical questions about season, cost and beginner suitability.

## Competitor pattern

Common coverage:

- why Koh Tao is popular for learning to dive;
- Discover Scuba, Open Water, Advanced and certified fun dives;
- PADI and SSI course options;
- named dive sites such as Chumphon Pinnacle, Twins, Japanese Gardens and HTMS Sattakut;
- course duration, package inclusions and live operator prices;
- seasonal conditions and possible marine-life sightings;
- broad advice for choosing a school.

Weaknesses the Go2Thailand owner should solve:

- commercial pages present their own centre as the answer rather than teaching a reusable operator check;
- fixed prices and rigid day counts are copied without defining inclusions, group size or training pace;
- the certifying agency logo is treated as a substitute for the actual instructor and centre;
- dive-site names are recommended without tying depth, current, recent experience and daily conditions to the choice;
- whale sharks and other wildlife are used as an expectation instead of an unguaranteed possibility;
- medical screening, refresher decisions and post-dive travel are often buried below sales copy;
- beginner, certified-diver and specialty intents are blended into one product list.

## Information-gain plan

- Start with the desired outcome: a supervised first experience, Open Water certification, certified fun dives or a specific continuing-education goal.
- Give travellers a six-question school scorecard covering the named instructor, working group size, medical process, emergency plan, confined-water training and exact inclusions.
- Explain the learning sequence rather than promising one universal duration. Training should not be squeezed between ferries or flights.
- Compare agencies neutrally: standards provide structure, while the centre, instructor, staffing and learning environment determine the day-to-day experience.
- Organise local sites by dive profile and decision consequence instead of publishing a universal “best sites” ranking.
- Treat season, visibility, boat departure, wildlife and remote-site access as dynamic. The operator should make a same-day decision for the diver in front of them.
- Include conservative post-dive travel guidance and direct readers to their training agency, dive computer, operator and medical professional when a longer interval applies.
- Use Klook only as a current activity-discovery exit after the safety and course decision. Do not place an OTA above direct operator evaluation.
- Keep Amazon absent: this is a safety-sensitive course and operator decision, not a natural product-shopping intent.
